>   - **Desirable Analytical Imprecision:** $CV_{\text{des}} = 0.50 \times CV_I$
>   - **Desirable Analytical Bias:** $B_{\text{des}} = 0.25 \times \sqrt{CV_I^2 + CV_G^2}$
>   - **Total Allowable Error (`TEa`):** $TE_a\% = 1.65 \times CV_{\text{des}} + B_{\text{des}}$ (or `CLIA 2024 / RCPA / AIIMS ISHBT` regulatory limit)
>   - **Expanded Measurement Uncertainty ($k=2$, $95.45\%\text{ CI}$):** $U\% = 2 \times \sqrt{u_{Rw}^2 + u_{\text{bias}}^2}$
>   - **Six-Sigma Process Capability:** $\sigma = \frac{TE_a\% - \lvert\text{Bias}\%\rvert}{CV_A\%}$

## Section 9: Analytical Sensitivity (`CLSI EP17-A2 LoB/LoD/LoQ`), Polynomial Linearity (`CLSI EP06-Ed2`), Index of Individuality (`II`), & Reference Change Value (`RCV 95% / 99%` Delta-Check) Master Verification Table

- **Mathematical Equations (`CLSI EP17-A2`, `CLSI EP06-Ed2`, & `EFLM EuBIVAS`):**
  - **Limit of Blank (`LoB`):** $\text{LoB} = \mu_{\text{blank}} + 1.645 \times \sigma_{\text{blank}}$
  - **Limit of Detection (`LoD`):** $\text{LoD} = \text{LoB} + 1.645 \times \sigma_{\text{low sample}}$
  - **Limit of Quantitation (`LoQ`):** Lowest measurand concentration where functional inter-assay imprecision satisfies $CV_A \le 10\%$ (or $\le 20\%$ for ultra-low cell counts) and $\text{Total Error} \le TE_a$.
  - **Index of Individuality (`II`):** $\text{II} = \frac{\sqrt{CV_A^2 + CV_I^2}}{CV_G} \approx \frac{CV_I}{CV_G}$. When $\text{II} < 0.60$, population-based reference intervals are insensitive to significant intra-individual change, making **serial Delta-Check (`RCV`)** clinically superior (`< 0.6` = High individuality; `> 1.4` = Population RI optimal).

    $$\text{RCV}_{95\%\text{ (Bidirectional)}} = 1.96 \times \sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{CV_A^2 + CV_I^2} = 2.77 \times \sqrt{CV_A^2 + CV_I^2}$$
    $$\text{RCV}_{99\%\text{ (High-Specificity LIMS Delta-Lock)}} = 2.58 \times \sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{CV_A^2 + CV_I^2} = 3.65 \times \sqrt{CV_A^2 + CV_I^2}$$

## Section 10: `CLSI H21-A5` High-Hematocrit ($\text{Hct } 55\%\text{ to }75\%$) Citrate Anticoagulant Adjustment Lookup Table & Step-by-Step Bench Preparation Protocol

> **Biophysical Rationale (`CLSI H21-A5`):** Trisodium citrate ($3.2\% = 0.109\text{ M}$) remains strictly in the extracellular plasma compartment and does not penetrate erythrocytes. In a patient with polycythemia ($\text{Hct} > 55\%$, e.g., neonate, COPD, Polycythemia Vera, cyanotic congenital heart disease, or severe dengue hemoconcentration), the plasma fraction $(100 - \text{Hct})\%$ is severely contracted. Drawing blood into a standard $9:1$ citrate tube results in **relative citrate excess in plasma**, which chelates the fixed $0.025\text{ M }\text{CaCl}_2$ added during `PT`/`aPTT` testing and produces **artifactually prolonged clotting times**.
>
> **Exact `CLSI H21-A5` Mathematical Equation:**
> $$C_{\text{required (mL)}} = 1.85 \times 10^{-3} \times (100 - \text{Hct [\%]}) \times V_{\text{blood (mL)}}$$

### Step-by-Step Bench Protocol for Preparing an Adjusted Citrate Tube (`SOP 1.3.1`):
1. Identify patient $\text{Hct} > 55\%$ from the concurrent $\text{K}_2\text{-EDTA}$ `CBC` or neonatal heel-stick/venous `PCV`.
2. Take an unused, in-date $3.2\%$ ($0.109\text{ M}$) blue-top trisodium citrate tube ($2.7\text{ mL}$ or $3.0\text{ mL}$ nominal mark).
3. Using a sterile $1\text{ mL}$ tuberculin syringe with a $25\text{G}$ needle (or calibrated micropipette after opening cap in a clean hood), aspirate and discard the exact **Volume to Discard ($\mu\text{L}$)** from the table above.
4. Draw fresh venous blood via a clean, atraumatic venipuncture using a syringe and immediately transfer blood smoothly down the wall of the adjusted tube to the exact nominal fill mark, cap tightly, and invert gently **$3\text{–}4\text{ times}$**.
5. Log the adjustment in **Register 7 (`SDL/HEM/REG/07` Part 7B)** and append the report comment: *"Anticoagulant volume adjusted for patient Hematocrit ($>55\%$) per CLSI H21-A5."*
