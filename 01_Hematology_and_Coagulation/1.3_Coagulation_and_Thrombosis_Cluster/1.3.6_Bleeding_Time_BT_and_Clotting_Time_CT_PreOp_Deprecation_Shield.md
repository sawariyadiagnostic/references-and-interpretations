## Section 2: Pre-Analytical Patient Preparation, Contraindications & Safety Matrix
1. **Why Clinicians in India Still Order `BT / CT` (And How the Lab Must Protect Patient & Surgeon):**
   - Despite formal deprecation by CLSI, CAP, ASH, and BCSH over two decades ago, `BT` and `CT` remain routinely ordered on pre-operative surgical, dental, ENT (tonsillectomy), obstetric, and cataract checklists across India.
   - **Institutional Policy:** When ordered by a surgeon/anesthesiologist, the laboratory performs the standardized **Ivy `BT`** and **Lee-White / Capillary `CT`** under strict safety controls **AND appends the mandatory CLSI/CAP Medico-Legal Shield (Section 10)** while strongly recommending **Platelet Count, `PT/INR`, and `aPTT`**.
2. **Absolute Contraindications to Performing Skin Bleeding Time (`BT`):**
   - **Severe Thrombocytopenia ($\text{Platelets} < 50,000/\mu\text{L}$):** Check CBC first if suspected; do NOT perform skin incision if $\text{PLT} < 50\times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$ (prolonged oozing/hematoma).
   - **Ipsilateral Mastectomy / Axillary Lymph Node Dissection or AV Fistula Arm:** Never inflate a $40\text{ mmHg}$ blood pressure cuff or puncture an arm with lymphedema or a dialysis shunt.
   - **History of Keloid Scarring or Active Forearm Cellulitis/Edema.**
3. **Mandatory $7\text{–}10\text{ Day}$ Drug History Capture:**
   - Document intake of **Aspirin (irreversible COX-1 inhibitor for $7\text{–}10\text{ days}$)**, **Clopidogrel / Prasugrel / Ticagrelor (`P2Y12` inhibitors)**, **NSAIDs** (`Ibuprofen`, `Diclofenac`, `Ketorolac`, `Naproxen` — reversible COX-1 inhibition for $24\text{–}72\text{ hours}$), or high-dose Valproate / Beta-lactam antibiotics.

## Section 3: Standardized Bedside Analytical SOP (`Ivy BT` & `Lee-White / Capillary CT`)
### 1. Standardized Ivy Forearm Bleeding Time (`BT`) SOP *(Preferred Over Duke Ear-Lobe/Finger Method)*
1. **Hydrostatic Pressure Standardization ($40\text{ mmHg}$):**
   - Seat patient comfortably with forearm resting palm-up on a steady support at heart level.
   - Place a calibrated sphygmomanometer cuff around the upper arm and inflate to a **constant pressure of exactly $40\text{ mmHg}$** throughout the entire procedure (standardizes capillary hydrostatic pressure and venous return).
2. **Volar Forearm Puncture & Blotting Technique:**
   - Select an avascular area on the **volar (flexor) surface of the forearm**, $5\text{ cm}$ below the antecubital crease, free of visible superficial blue veins, scars, hair, or edema. Cleanse with $70\%$ isopropyl alcohol and **allow to air-dry completely** ($30\text{–}60\text{ s}$).
   - Perform a standardized sterile puncture (**$1.0\text{ mm}$ depth $\times 2.5\text{–}3.0\text{ mm}$ width** spring-loaded disposable safety lancet/template) and **simultaneously start the calibrated stopwatch**.
   - **Every $30\text{ seconds}$**, gently touch the **edge of the blood drop** with sterile Whatman No. 1 circular filter paper **WITHOUT touching the skin wound margins or disrupting the fragile platelet plug!**
   - Record the time (to the nearest $30\text{ seconds}$) when blood no longer stains the filter paper (**Endpoint**). *(If bleeding continues beyond **$10\text{–}15\text{ minutes}$**, terminate the test, deflate the cuff, apply firm sterile pressure dressing for $10\text{ minutes}$, and report as **`> 10 min 00 sec (Critical Prolongation)`**).*

### 2. Standardized Clotting Time (`CT`) SOP (`Lee-White Venous` vs `Wright Capillary`)
1. **Lee-White $3$-Tube $37^\circ\text{C}$ Venous Glass Method (Superior to Capillary Method):**
   - Draw $3.0\text{ mL}$ of venous blood via clean, rapid, atraumatic venipuncture (start stopwatch as blood enters syringe; **discard if venipuncture is slow/traumatic, as tissue factor contamination falsely shortens `CT`!**).
   - Deliver **$1.0\text{ mL}$ of blood gently down the side of THREE clean, dry, unsiliconized $12\times 75\text{ mm}$ borosilicate glass tubes** (`Tube 1`, `Tube 2`, `Tube 3`) pre-warmed in a **$37.0^\circ\text{C}$ water bath**.
   - After $3\text{ minutes}$, tilt **`Tube 1` only** at a $45^\circ$ angle every $30\text{ seconds}$ until it can be inverted $90^\circ$ without blood flowing. Then tilt **`Tube 2`** every $30\text{ seconds}$ until clotted, and finally tilt **`Tube 3`** every $30\text{ seconds}$ (since `Tube 3` experienced minimal mechanical agitation, the **clotting time of `Tube 3` is reported as the true Lee-White Clotting Time**!).
   - **(2) Wright Capillary Glass Tube Method (Bedside Fingerstick Screening):**
   - Perform a free-flowing $2.0\text{ mm}$ lancet puncture of the warmed lateral fingertip (**never squeeze or "milk" the finger**, as expressed interstitial tissue juice contains **Tissue Factor [Thromboplastin]** that accelerates clotting and **masks Hemophilia**!).
   - Wipe away the **first drop of blood** with dry sterile gauze.
   - Fill two non-heparinized plain soda-lime glass capillary tubes ($1.2\text{ mm}$ bore $\times 100\text{ mm}$ length) by capillary action ($75\%$ full) and start stopwatch.
   - After $2\text{ minutes}$, gently snap off a $1.0\text{ cm}$ segment of the capillary tube every **$30\text{ seconds}$** until a continuous **cohesive fibrin thread ($\ge 5\text{ mm}$)** bridges the separated glass ends (**Endpoint**).

## Section 7: Why a Normal `BT` & `CT` Can Be Surgically Fatal (Biophysical Sensitivity Analysis)
1. **Why Capillary Clotting Time (`CT`) Misses $90\%$ of Clinically Significant Coagulopathies:**
   - First, squeezing a fingerstick puncture contaminates capillary blood with **extravascular Tissue Factor (`Factor III`)**, which triggers the **Extrinsic (`Factor VIIa`) pathway** and completely **bypasses Factors VIII, IX, XI, and XII**!
   - Second, even in a clean venous Lee-White `CT`, whole blood clots within the normal $5\text{–}11\text{ minute}$ window as long as Factor VIII or Factor IX activity is **above $1\% \text{ to } 2\%$**! Consequently, a patient with **Moderate Hemophilia A ($3\%$ Factor VIII) or Mild Hemophilia A ($12\%$ Factor VIII)**—who will experience **catastrophic, life-threatening hemorrhage during tonsillectomy, dental extraction, or abdominal surgery**—will have a **$100\%$ NORMAL Clotting Time (`CT = 05 min 30 sec`)**!
2. **Why Skin Bleeding Time (`BT`) Fails to Predict Visceral Surgical Hemorrhage (`CLSI H45-A2`):**
   - Dermal capillary closure depends heavily on skin thickness, ambient temperature (cold vasoconstriction falsely shortens `BT`!), operator lancet pressure, and senile collagen atrophy. In over $1,000$ prospective surgical studies reviewed by CAP/ASCP, skin `BT` showed a Positive Predictive Value of **$< 5\%$** and failed to detect $> 50\%$ of mild Type 1 von Willebrand Disease cases.
