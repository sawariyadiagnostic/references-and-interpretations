## Section 1: Purpose, Regulatory Scope, 10-Persona Adversarial Audit Charter & NABL 112 Compliance Mandate

### 1.1 Purpose & Analytical Scope
This Standard Operating Procedure (`SOP`) establishes the mandatory engineering, metrological, pre-analytical, analytical, and quality-assurance protocols governing all hemostasis and thrombosis instrumentation at **Sawariya Diagnostic Lab, Charkhi Dadri, Haryana**. Unlike routine spectrophotometric biochemistry or impedance hematology, plasma-based coagulation assays measure the **kinetic velocity of a self-amplifying serine protease cascade** culminating in the conversion of soluble Fibrinogen ($340\text{ kDa}$) into an insoluble cross-linked Fibrin gel at exactly **$37.0^\circ\text{C}$**. Because clotting endpoints are measured in **tenths of a second ($0.1\text{ s}$)** and raised to an exponential power (`ISI`) to compute the **International Normalized Ratio (`INR`)**, minor deviations in centrifuge Relative Centrifugal Force (`RCF`), incubation block temperature, reconstitution water ionic strength, or geometric mean normal prothrombin time (`MNPT`) propagate into life-threatening clinical dosing errors for patients on Vitamin K Antagonists (`Warfarin` / `Acenocoumarol`) and Unfractionated Heparin (`UFH`).

This SOP applies to:
1. **Primary & Backup Coagulometers:** Photo-optical turbidimetric/nephelometric analyzers ($\lambda = 405\text{ nm}, 575\text{ nm}, 660\text{ nm}, 800\text{ nm}$), Electromechanical/Viscosity steel-ball oscillation coagulometers (`Stago STart Max` / opto-mechanical bench units), and Chromogenic/Latex Immunoturbidimetric channels supporting `PT/INR` (`SOP 1.3.2`), `aPTT` (`SOP 1.3.3`), `Quantitative D-Dimer` (`SOP 1.3.4`), `Clauss Fibrinogen` (`SOP 1.3.5`), `Thrombin Time` (`SOP 1.3.7`), `Lupus Anticoagulant dRVVT/SCT` (`SOP 1.3.8`), and `Thrombophilia Protein C / Protein S / AT-III` (`SOP 1.3.9`).
2. **Dedicated Coagulation Centrifuges (`CENT-COAG-01` & `CENT-BACKUP-02`):** Swing-out and fixed-angle centrifuges used to harvest Platelet-Poor Plasma (`PPP`, residual platelet count **$< 10 \times 10^9/\text{L}$** [`< 10,000/\mu\text{L}`]) via single-spin ($1,500 \times g \text{ for } 15\text{ min}$) and double-spin ($2,500 \times g \text{ for } 15\text{ min} \times 2$) protocols.
3. **Ancillary Metrological Equipment:** NABL-calibrated `Pt100` Resistance Temperature Detector (`RTD`) probes (`ISO 17025` traceable), non-contact optical tachometers, calibrated digital stopwatches, `CLSI GP40` Type-I/II reagent water purification systems ($\ge 10\text{ M}\Omega\cdot\text{cm}$), and dedicated coagulation starter/reconstitution micropipettes (`ISO 8655:2022`).

### 1.2 Mandatory `NABL 112 (Issue 05)` & International Accreditation Requirements
In strict compliance with **NABL 112 (`Specific Criteria for Accreditation of Medical Laboratories — Hematology & Immunohaematology`)**, **ISO 15189:2022 Clauses 6.4 & 7.3**, and **CAP HEM**, the Coagulation Section enforces six non-negotiable metrological mandates:
1. **Daily $37.0^\circ\text{C}$ Thermal Block Verification:** The coagulometer sample incubation block and heated reagent positions must be verified before every analytical shift to remain within **$37.0^\circ\text{C} \pm 0.2^\circ\text{C}$** (NABL absolute rejection limit: **$\pm 0.5^\circ\text{C}$, i.e., $36.5^\circ\text{C}\text{–}37.5^\circ\text{C}$**), supplemented by a weekly liquid-in-cuvette cross-check using an external NABL-calibrated `Pt100` digital thermometer.
2. **Local Geometric Mean `MNPT` & `MNAPTT` Establishment (`WHO TRS 889` / `CLSI H54-A`):** Arithmetic means and package-insert midpoint values are strictly forbidden. For every new lot or manufacturer of `PT` Thromboplastin or `aPTT` Cephalin reagent, the laboratory must establish or verify the **Geometric Mean Normal Prothrombin Time (`MNPT`)** and **Geometric Mean Normal `aPTT` (`MNAPTT`)** using fresh Citrate plasmas from **$n \ge 20$ healthy local adult donors** (or rolling lot verification with $n = 10$ normal donors plus certified `INR` verification plasmas when $\Delta\text{MNPT} \le \pm 3.0\%$).
3. **Local `ISI` Verification & Direct `INR` Calibration (`CLSI H54-A`):** Whenever a new thromboplastin reagent lot or coagulometer optical/mechanical channel is introduced, the instrument-specific **International Sensitivity Index (`ISI`, target $0.95\text{–}1.15$, mandatory $\le 1.20$)** must be verified across **$3\text{–}5$ certified lyophilized `INR` calibration plasmas** ($1.0\text{ to } 4.5\text{ INR}$) to confirm $\lvert \text{INR}_{\text{Calculated}} - \text{INR}_{\text{Certified}} \rvert \le 10.0\%$ (maximum permissible $\le 15.0\%$).
4. **Semi-Annual Centrifuge `PPP` Residual Platelet Qualification (`CLSI H21-A5`):** Every **6 months** (January and July) and following any motor, brush, or rotor servicing, the coagulation centrifuge must undergo tachometer `RPM`/`RCF` verification, timer check, bowl temperature check ($\le 22^\circ\text{C}$), **Brake = OFF** verification, and empirical platelet counting of **$5$ routine single-spin Citrate plasmas (`< 10 x 10^9/L`)** plus **$1$ double-spin Lupus Anticoagulant plasma (`< 5 x 10^9/L`)** on the `Nihon Kohden MEK-1503` hematology analyzer.
5. **Reagent Reconstitution & On-Board Dead-Volume Governance:** All lyophilized thromboplastins, calibration plasmas, and controls must be reconstituted exclusively with **`CLSI GP40` Type-I/II Reagent Grade Water ($\ge 10\text{ M}\Omega\cdot\text{cm}$)** using `ISO 8655:2022` gravimetrically calibrated pipettes, hydrated for $15\text{–}30\text{ minutes}$ without vortexing, and labeled with exact reconstitution and expiry timestamps.
6. **Two-Level 8-Hourly Internal Quality Control (`IQC`) & External Quality Assessment (`EQAS`):** Both **`Level 1` (Normal Control Plasma)** and **`Level 2` (Abnormal Therapeutic/Pathological Control Plasma)** must be analyzed every **8 hours / every shift** prior to releasing patient reports, governed by Westgard Sigma rules and monthly `AIIMS ISHBT` / `CMC Vellore` / `Bio-Rad` EQAS participation (`NABL 163`).

## Section 3: Deep Biophysical, Engineering & Clot-Detection Signal-Processing Architecture

### 3.1 Photo-Optical / Turbidimetric & Nephelometric Clot-Detection Physics ($\lambda = 405\text{ nm}, 575\text{ nm}, 660\text{ nm}, 800\text{ nm}$)

#### 3.1.1 Biophysical Mechanism of Fibrin Polymerization Light Scattering
In plasma-based clotting assays (`PT`, `aPTT`, `Clauss Fibrinogen`, `Thrombin Time`, `dRVVT`), the reaction begins in a homogeneous liquid phase containing soluble **Fibrinogen** (a $340\text{ kDa}$ trinodular glycoprotein of length $\sim 45\text{ nm}$ composed of $(\text{A}\alpha, \text{B}\beta, \gamma)_2$ chains). Because the hydrodynamic diameter of soluble fibrinogen is much smaller than the incident wavelength of visible/near-infrared light ($\lambda = 405\text{–}800\text{ nm}$), baseline platelet-poor plasma exhibits high optical transmittance ($T_0 \approx 85\text{–}95\%$) and low baseline optical density ($\text{OD}_0$).

Once the coagulation cascade generates **Thrombin (`Factor IIa`)**, three sequential biophysical phases occur inside the $37.0^\circ\text{C}$ reaction cuvette:
1. **Proteolytic Lag Phase (Enzymatic Initiation & Amplification):** Thrombin cleaves polar **Fibrinopeptides A and B (`FpA`, `FpB`)** from the central E-domain of soluble fibrinogen to generate **Fibrin Monomers**. During this phase ($0 \to t_{\text{lag}}$), optical density remains flat ($\text{d}(\text{OD})/\text{d}t \approx 0$).
2. **Protofibril Longitudinal & Lateral Aggregation Phase (Rapid Turbidity Rise):** Fibrin monomers spontaneously polymerize via half-staggered non-covalent D–E knob-hole interactions into two-stranded **protofibrils**, which then aggregate laterally into thick, branched **insoluble Fibrin fibers** ($d = 100\text{–}400\text{ nm}$). By the **Rayleigh-Debye-Gans light scattering law**, scattered light intensity ($I_s$) scales with the sixth power of particle radius ($I_s \propto r^6 / \lambda^4$), causing a steep sigmoidal surge in optical density ($\Delta\text{OD}$) and $90^\circ$ nephelometric scatter.
3. **Cross-Linking Plateau Phase ($\text{OD}_{\max}$):** Activated **Factor XIIIa** catalyzes $\varepsilon$-($\gamma$-glutamyl)lysine covalent cross-links between adjacent $\gamma$-chains and $\alpha$-chains, stabilizing the gel network at a maximal turbidity plateau ($\text{OD}_{\max}$).

```
Optical Density (OD) / Turbidity & Derivative Signal Architecture
  ^
  |                                              ___________________ OD_max (Plateau Phase: FXIIIa Cross-Linking)
  |                                            /
  |                                          /   <-- 50% Threshold Endpoint (t_50%: OD_0 + 0.50 * Delta_OD)
  |                                        *
  |                                      / :     <-- 1st Derivative Peak [d(OD)/dt_max]: Max Polymerization Velocity (PT)
  |                                    #   :
  |                                  / :   :     <-- 2nd Derivative Peak [d^2(OD)/dt^2_max]: Max Acceleration Onset (aPTT)
  |                                @   :   :
  |   Lag Phase (Cascade)        / :   :   :
  |____________________________/   :   :   :
  +--------------------------------:---:---:------------------------> Time (Seconds)
  t = 0 (CaCl2 / Reagent Start)   t_2d t_1d t_50%
```

#### 3.1.2 Mathematical Comparison of Clot-Endpoint Signal-Processing Algorithms
Modern photo-optical coagulometers digitize raw photodetector transmittance readings at **$10\text{–}20\text{ Hz}$ (every $50\text{–}100\text{ ms}$)**, apply a Savitzky-Golay digital smoothing filter to suppress lamp ripple, and apply one of three mathematical endpoint algorithms depending on assay kinetics and fibrinogen concentration:

| Endpoint Algorithm | Mathematical Formulation | Primary Assay Application | Biophysical Advantage | Vulnerability & Built-In Algorithmic Safeguard |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. First-Derivative Velocity Peak (`d(OD)/dt_max`)** | $$t_{\text{clot}} = \arg\max_{t} \left( \frac{\text{d}(\text{OD})}{\text{d}t} \right)$$ | **`PT / INR`**, **`Thrombin Time`**, **`High-Concentration Factor Assays`** | Identifies the exact inflection point of **maximum fibrin polymerization velocity**, independent of final plateau drifts. | In severe hypofibrinogenemia ($< 80\text{ mg/dL}$), $\Delta\text{OD}$ amplitude is small and baseline noise may mimic a derivative spike. **Safeguard:** Requires minimum total $\Delta\text{OD} \ge 15\text{ mAbs}$ before validating peak. |
| **2. Second-Derivative Acceleration Peak (`d²(OD)/dt²_max`)** | $$t_{\text{clot}} = \arg\max_{t} \left( \frac{\text{d}^2(\text{OD})}{\text{d}t^2} \right)$$ | **`aPTT`**, **`Lupus dRVVT / SCT`**, **`Intrinsic Factor Assays (VIII, IX, XI, XII)`** | Captures the **earliest point of maximum polymerization acceleration** (where the curve bends sharply upward from baseline), yielding superior precision ($\text{CV} < 1.5\%$) in slower intrinsic pathways. | Sensitive to micro-bubbles or incomplete silica mixing during the first $8\text{ seconds}$. **Safeguard:** Software applies a mandatory **"Blind Time / Guard Window" ($t_{\text{blind}} = 7.0\text{ s}$ for `PT`, $15.0\text{ s}$ for `aPTT`)** during which early mixing transients are ignored. |
| **3. #### 3.1.3 Multi-Wavelength Spectral Switching (`405 nm -> 660 nm -> 800 nm`) for `HIL` Interference Mitigation
Endogenous plasma chromophores and lipid particles interfere with photo-optical clot detection through two distinct mechanisms: **(a) Spectral Absorbance Overlap** (saturating the photodetector baseline $\text{OD}_0$) and **(b) Biological Interference** (ruptured RBC membranes and free hemoglobin activating contact factors):
- **Oxyhemoglobin ($\text{HbO}_2$):** Exhibits intense Soret-band absorbance at **$415\text{ nm}$** and secondary $\alpha/\beta$ bands at **$541\text{ nm}$ and $577\text{ nm}$**, but virtually zero absorbance above **$650\text{ nm}$**. However, because *in-vitro* hemolysis releases **erythrocyte phosphatidylserine** and **ADP** (which artificially shorten `aPTT` and activate platelets), **all visibly hemolyzed Citrate specimens ($\text{H-Index} > 100\text{ mg/dL}$ free Hb) must be rejected and recollected (`SOP 1.3.1`)**, regardless of optical wavelength!
- **Unconjugated & Conjugated Bilirubin (Icterus):** Exhibits broad absorbance peaking at **$450\text{–}460\text{ nm}$** and extending across $405\text{ nm}$, but drops to near zero at **$\lambda = 660\text{ nm}$ and $800\text{ nm}$**.
- **Chylomicrons & VLDL (Lipemia):** Scatters light across all wavelengths inversely proportional to $\lambda^4$ ($\text{Scatter}_{405\text{ nm}} \approx 7.1 \times \text{Scatter}_{660\text{ nm}}$). Shifting from $405\text{ nm}$ to **$660\text{ nm}$ or $800\text{ nm}$** reduces lipid baseline turbidity by **$85\text{–}93\%$**.

| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |

#### 3.1.4 Biphasic Waveform Analysis (`BWA`) on Photo-Optical Coagulometers: Early Sepsis & `DIC` Alert
On photo-optical coagulometers monitoring `aPTT` at $405\text{ nm}$ or $660\text{ nm}$, normal plasma maintains a **sigmoidal waveform**: a flat horizontal baseline ($100\%$ light transmittance) during the pre-clotting lag phase after $\text{CaCl}_2$ addition, followed by a sharp downward drop in transmittance at clot onset.
- **Biophysical Mechanism of the Biphasic Waveform ("Seesaw" Pattern):** In patients with **early systemic Sepsis, Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome (`SIRS`), or non-overt Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (`DIC`)**, hepatic acute-phase **C-Reactive Protein (`CRP` $> 50\text{–}100\text{ mg/L}$)** and **Very-Low-Density Lipoprotein (`VLDL`) / Intermediate-Density Lipoprotein (`IDL`)** are markedly elevated alongside exposed anionic phospholipids. The instant $0.025\text{ M }\text{CaCl}_2$ is injected into the cuvette, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ bridges phosphorylcholine binding sites on `CRP` with `VLDL` particles, forming microscopic **`CRP–VLDL–Ca2+` lipoprotein-pentraxin precipitates** *before* Thrombin is generated!
- **Quantitative Waveform Signature:** Instead of a flat lag phase, light transmittance immediately begins a linear downward decay (**`Slope_1 < -0.10%T/sec`** during the first $5\text{–}18\text{ seconds}$), followed by a second steeper drop (`Slope_2`) when true Fibrin polymerization begins.
- **Mandatory Clinical Action at Sawariya Diagnostic Lab:** Whenever the coagulometer optical curve displays a **Biphasic Waveform (`Slope_1 <= -0.15%T/sec` or transmittance drop $> 3\%$ prior to the $2\text{nd}$-derivative inflection point)**, the technologist must print the optical waveform, verify absence of reagent contamination, and alert the Consultant Hematopathologist to append the **Critical Sepsis/DIC Early-Warning Comment (`ISTH Non-Overt DIC Alert — Correlate with D-Dimer, Platelets, Fibrinogen, and Serum Procalcitonin/CRP`)**.

### 3.2 Electromechanical / Viscosity Steel-Ball Oscillation Detection & Opto-Mechanical Hybrid Systems
To guarantee $100\%$ analytical reliability when plasma is optically opaque (e.g., severe hypertriglyceridemic pancreatitis with chylomicronemia $\text{TG} > 1,500\text{ mg/dL}$, severe obstructive jaundice $\text{Bilirubin} > 35\text{ mg/dL}$, or patients receiving intense chromogenic drugs such as **Rifampicin [orange-red]**, **Eltrombopag [brown-red]**, **Phenazopyridine**, or **Hydroxocobalamin [deep red]**), Sawariya Diagnostic Lab utilizes **Electromechanical Steel-Ball Viscosity Clot Detection** (`Stago STart Max` / opto-mechanical architecture) as its gold-standard reference and backup modality:
1. **Biophysical Principle of Electromagnetic Steel-Ball Oscillation:**
   - The reaction cuvette features a curved bottom track housing a single medical-grade paramagnetic **stainless-steel microbead ($d = 2.0\text{ mm}$)**.
   - Two alternating electromagnetic drive coils positioned beneath the cuvette energize alternately at a fixed frequency, driving the steel ball in a smooth pendulum-like oscillation from left to right across the bottom of the plasma-reagent mixture.
   - An electromagnetic receiver/emitter sensor pair continuously monitors the **oscillation amplitude and transit velocity** of the steel ball.
   - In unclotted liquid plasma (regardless of whether the plasma is milky white, dark icteric brown, or cherry red), viscosity ($\eta$) is low and ball oscillation amplitude remains constant at $100\%$.
   - The instant soluble Fibrinogen polymerizes into three-dimensional cross-linked **Fibrin strands**, the viscoelastic resistance of the medium rises abruptly, tangling and braking the steel ball. When the ball's oscillation amplitude drops below **$50\%$ of its initial baseline excursion**, the electronic chronometer locks the exact clotting time ($0.1\text{ s}$ resolution).
2. **Comparative Engineering Matrix Across Coagulometer Detection Modalities:**

## Section 4: Complete `CLSI H21-A5` Platelet-Poor Plasma (`PPP`) Centrifuge Metrology, `RCF` Nomogram Mathematics & Semi-Annual Verification Protocol

### 4.1 Biophysical Rationale & Exact Derivation of the Relative Centrifugal Force (`RCF`) Equation
Per **CLSI H21-A5 (`Collection, Transport, and Processing of Blood Specimens for Testing Plasma-Based Coagulation Assays`)** and **NABL 112**, every plasma specimen analyzed for coagulation testing must be **Platelet-Poor Plasma (`PPP`)** containing a residual platelet count of **$< 10 \times 10^9/\text{L}$ (`< 10,000/\mu\text{L}`)**.

#### 4.1.1 Why Residual Platelets ($> 10 \times 10^9/\text{L}$) Invalidate Hemostasis Assays
1. **In-Vitro Neutralization of Unfractionated Heparin (`UFH`) via Platelet Factor 4 (`PF4`):** Residual platelets sitting in spun Citrate plasma undergo progressive alpha-granule degranulation, releasing positively charged tetrameric **`PF4`**, which binds and neutralizes negatively charged `UFH` chains *in vitro*. If centrifugation is inadequate or plasma sits on cells for $> 1\text{–}2\text{ hours}$, a patient with therapeutic or supratherapeutic heparinization will yield a **falsely shortened/normal `aPTT`**, prompting dangerous clinical heparin dose escalation!
2. **False-Negative Lupus Anticoagulant (`LA`) via Anionic Phospholipid (`PF3`) Quenching:** Platelets contain abundant inner-leaflet **phosphatidylserine** and **phosphatidylethanolamine** (`Platelet Factor 3 / PF3`). If plasma containing $> 10 \times 10^9/\text{L}$ platelets is tested—or worse, frozen at $-20^\circ\text{C}/-80^\circ\text{C}$ and thawed (which lyses $100\%$ of residual platelet membranes into phospholipid microvesicles)—the excess platelet phospholipid neutralizes Lupus Anticoagulant antibodies during the dilute-phospholipid screening step (`dRVVT Screen` / `SCT Screen`), producing a **false-negative Lupus Anticoagulant result**!
3. **Consumption & Release of Coagulation Factors:** Activated residual platelets release platelet-derived **Factor V**, **vWF**, and **PAI-1**, distorting factor assays and fibrinolytic tests.

#### 4.1.2 Mathematical Derivation of `RCF (x g)` vs. `RPM`
Rotational speed in Revolutions Per Minute (`RPM`) is only a measure of angular frequency—it does **not** define the sedimentation force applied to platelets unless multiplied by the horizontal rotor radius ($r$).
From Newtonian mechanics, the centripetal acceleration $a_c$ experienced by a platelet at radius $r_{\max}$ (in $\text{cm}$) rotating at angular velocity $\omega$ (in $\text{radians/second}$) is:
$$a_c = \omega^2 \cdot r_{\max\text{ (cm)}}, \quad \text{where } \omega = \frac{2\pi \cdot \text{RPM}}{60}\text{ rad/s}$$
Dividing $a_c$ by standard gravitational acceleration ($g = 980.665\text{ cm/s}^2$) yields the dimensionless **Relative Centrifugal Force (`RCF` in $\times g$)**:
$$\text{RCF} (\times g) = \frac{a_c}{g} = \left(\frac{2\pi}{60}\right)^2 \frac{1}{980.665} \times r_{\max\text{ (cm)}} \times (\text{RPM})^2 = \mathbf{1.118 \times 10^{-5} \times r_{\max\text{ (cm)}} \times (\text{RPM})^2}$$
Solving inversely for the required **`RPM`** as a function of target `RCF` and rotor radius $r_{\max}$ (in $\text{cm}$ or $\text{mm}$):
$$\mathbf{\text{RPM} = \sqrt{\frac{\text{RCF}}{1.118 \times 10^{-5} \times r_{\max\text{ (cm)}}}} = 1,000 \times \sqrt{\frac{\text{RCF}}{1.118 \times r_{\max\text{ (mm)}}}}}$$

> **[!CAUTION]**
> **Critical Red-Team Proof (`FMEA-COAG-01`):** Suppose a technician sets a compact benchtop centrifuge ($r_{\max} = 8.5\text{ cm}$) to **`1,500 RPM`** believing it equals $1,500 \times g$. Substituting into the equation:
> $$\text{RCF} = 1.118 \times 10^{-5} \times 8.5 \times (1,500)^2 = \mathbf{213.8 \times g} \quad (\text{Only } 14.2\% \text{ of required force!})$$
> To achieve the mandatory **$1,500 \times g$** on an $8.5\text{ cm}$ rotor, the centrifuge must actually spin at **$\text{RPM} = \sqrt{1,500 / (1.118 \times 10^{-5} \times 8.5)} = \mathbf{3,973\text{ RPM}}$**!

#### 4.1.3 Swing-Out Horizontal Bucket Rotor vs. Fixed-Angle Rotor ($25^\circ\text{–}45^\circ$) Mechanics
- **Swing-Out Horizontal Bucket Rotor (Preferred Reference Design):** During rotation, the buckets swing out to a horizontal $90^\circ$ plane. Red blood cells, leukocytes, and platelets sediment radially along the axis of the tube, forming a **flat, tight, horizontal buffy-coat barrier** perpendicular to the tube wall. When the rotor decelerates gently with **`Brake = OFF`**, the horizontal interface remains undisturbed, allowing safe aspiration of the top $2/3$ plasma column.
- **Fixed-Angle Rotor ($30^\circ\text{–}45^\circ$ Backup Design):** Tubes are held at a fixed diagonal angle. Platelets hit the outer wall early and slide down the inclined wall (the Boycott effect), leaving a **diagonal, sloped buffy-coat smear** extending high up the outer wall of the tube. If a fixed-angle rotor is used, tubes must be aspirated **immediately ($< 2\text{ minutes}$) after rotor stop** with the transfer pipette tip held strictly against the **inner (short) wall** at least $10\text{ mm}$ above the highest point of the diagonal buffy coat.

### 4.3 Step-by-Step Single-Spin & Double-Spin (`CLSI H21-A5` / `ISTH 2020`) `PPP` Harvesting Protocol

#### 4.3.1 Protocol 1 — Routine Single-Spin `PPP` (`1,500 x g` for `15 Minutes`, Brake OFF)
Used for immediate fresh analysis ($< 4\text{ hours}$ for `PT/INR`, `Fibrinogen`, `TT`, `D-Dimer`; $< 2\text{ hours}$ for `aPTT` on unfractionated heparin, $< 4\text{ hours}$ for non-heparinized `aPTT`):
1. **Pre-Spin Inspection:** Verify the $3.2\%$ ($0.109\text{ M}$) buffered trisodium citrate tube is filled within **$\pm 10\%$ of the etched fill mark** ($9:1$ blood-to-anticoagulant ratio; hematocrit-adjusted if $\text{PCV} > 55\%$ per `SOP 1.3.1`) and gently invert $5\text{ times}$ to check for micro-clots.
2. **Closed-Cap Centrifugation:** Keep the primary stopper **tightly capped** during centrifugation (prevents loss of dissolved $\text{CO}_2$, which otherwise raises plasma $\text{pH} > 7.8$ and artificially prolongs `PT` and `aPTT`). Balance opposing buckets within $\pm 0.5\text{ g}$ and spin at **$1,500 \times g$ ($3,096\text{ RPM}$ on $r = 14.0\text{ cm}$) for $15\text{ minutes}$ at $18\text{–}22^\circ\text{C}$ with `BRAKE = OFF`**.
3. **Visual `HIL` Inspection:** Gently remove the tube without tilting. Inspect the supernatant plasma column for **Hemolysis (`reject if pink/red`)**, **Icterus**, **Lipemia**, or a fibrin web.

#### 4.3.2 Protocol 2 — Mandatory Double-Spin `PPP` (`2,500 x g` for `15 Minutes` $\times 2$, Brake OFF)
Mandatory for **Lupus Anticoagulant (`dRVVT` / `SCT`)**, **Factor Assays (`VIII, IX, XI, XII, II, V, VII, X`)**, **Thrombophilia (`Protein C`, `Protein S`, `AT-III`)**, **Pooled Normal Plasma (`PNP`) preparation**, and **any Citrate plasma that will be frozen at $-20^\circ\text{C}$ or $-80^\circ\text{C}$**:
1. **Primary Spin:** Centrifuge the capped primary Citrate tube at **$2,000\text{–}2,500 \times g$ for $15\text{ minutes}$ (`BRAKE = OFF`)**.
2. **Supernatant Transfer to Conical Polypropylene Tube:** Using a clean, single-use **plastic (polypropylene/polyethylene) Pasteur transfer pipette** (never glass, as borosilicate glass activates Factor XII!), carefully aspirate the upper **$2/3$ of the plasma column**, stopping at least **$5\text{–}10\text{ mm}$ above the buffy coat**. Transfer into a labeled secondary **conical-bottom polypropylene tube** and cap tightly.
3. **Secondary Spin:** Centrifuge the secondary polypropylene tube at **$2,500 \times g$ ($3,997\text{ RPM}$ on $r = 14.0\text{ cm}$) for $15\text{ minutes}$ (`BRAKE = OFF`)**.
4. **Final Harvesting & Cryo-Aliquoting:** Aspirate the upper **$3/4$ of the secondary plasma column** (leaving the bottom $250\ \mu\text{L}$ pellet untouched) into labeled polypropylene cryovials (`0.5 mL` per aliquot). This double-spin procedure guarantees a residual platelet count **$< 5 \times 10^9/\text{L}$ (routinely $\le 1\text{–}2 \times 10^9/\text{L}$)**.
5. **Why $0.22\ \mu\text{m}$ Syringe Membrane Filtration Is Strictly Prohibited (`CLSI H21-A5` / `ISTH 2020`):** Never pass Citrate plasma through a $0.20\ \mu\text{m}$ or $0.22\ \mu\text{m}$ cellulose acetate/mixed-ester syringe filter to remove platelets! Filter membranes selectively adsorb high-molecular-weight **von Willebrand Factor (`vWF`)**, **Factor VIII**, **Factor V**, and **Fibrinogen**, causing severe artifactual prolongation of `aPTT` and false diagnosis of Factor VIII deficiency / von Willebrand Disease!

## Section 5: Complete `WHO TRS 889` & `CLSI H54-A` Geometric Mean `MNPT`, `MNAPTT`, and Direct `INR` / Local `ISI` Calibration & Verification Protocol

### 5.1 Biostatistical Theory: Why Arithmetic Mean & Package-Insert Midpoints Are Forbidden
In healthy human populations, individual Prothrombin Time (`PT`) and Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (`aPTT`) clotting times do **not** follow a symmetric Gaussian distribution; instead, because enzymatic reaction times are reciprocals of multi-step rate constants, they follow a **right-skewed log-normal distribution**. When individual clotting times $\text{PT}_i$ are transformed to their natural logarithms $\ln(\text{PT}_i)$ (or base-10 logarithms $\log_{10}(\text{PT}_i)$), the transformed distribution becomes strictly Gaussian.

Therefore, both **WHO Technical Report Series No. 889 (Annex 3)** and **CLSI H54-A (`Procedures for Validation of INR and Local Calibration of PT/INR Systems`)** mandate that the reference normal denominator in the `INR` formula:
$$\text{INR} = \left( \frac{\text{PT}_{\text{Patient (sec)}}}{\text{MNPT}_{\text{Local (sec)}}} \right)^{\text{ISI}}$$
must be the **Geometric Mean Normal Prothrombin Time (`MNPT`)** of **$n \ge 20$ healthy local adult donors**:
$$\mathbf{\text{MNPT} = \exp\left( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \ln(\text{PT}_i) \right) = 10^{\left( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \log_{10}(\text{PT}_i) \right)} = \left( \prod_{i=1}^{n} \text{PT}_i \right)^{\frac{1}{n}}}$$

By **Jensen's Inequality**, the Geometric Mean is always less than or equal to the Arithmetic Mean ($\text{GM} \le \text{AM}$), and using the package-insert range midpoint (which reflects extreme $2.5\text{th}\text{–}97.5\text{th}$ tails across disparate global instruments) introduces severe systematic error. Furthermore, the **Log-Normal $95\%$ Reference Interval ($2.5\text{th}\text{ to }97.5\text{th}$ percentile)** for local `PT` and `aPTT` must be computed in log-space and back-transformed:
$$\text{Reference Interval}_{95\%} = \exp\left( \overline{\ln(X)} \pm 1.96 \cdot s_{\ln(X)} \right), \quad \text{where } s_{\ln(X)} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} \left(\ln(X_i) - \overline{\ln(X)}\right)^2}{n - 1}}$$

#### 5.3.2 Protocol B — Direct `INR` Calibration / Local System `ISI` Assignment (When Verification Fails by $> 10\text{–}15\%$)
If any certified therapeutic plasma ($2.0\text{–}4.5\text{ INR}$) deviates from its certified `INR` by **$> \pm 10.0\%$** (absolute outer limit $\pm 15.0\%$ per `CLSI H54-A`)—or when pairing a generic thromboplastin without an instrument-specific `ISI` to the coagulometer—the laboratory must derive a **Local System `ISI` ($\text{ISI}_{\text{Local}}$)** via **Log-Log Linear / Orthogonal Regression (`CLSI H54-A Section 8`)**:
1. Take the base-10 logarithm of the `INR` equation:
   $$\text{INR} = \left( \frac{\text{PT}}{\text{MNPT}} \right)^{\text{ISI}_{\text{Local}}} \iff \log_{10}(\text{INR}_{\text{Cert}}) = \text{ISI}_{\text{Local}} \cdot \log_{10}(\text{PT}_{\text{Local}}) - \text{ISI}_{\text{Local}} \cdot \log_{10}(\text{MNPT})$$
2. This is a straight line $Y = bX + a$, where $Y = \log_{10}(\text{INR}_{\text{Cert}})$, $X = \log_{10}(\text{PT}_{\text{Local}})$, the **slope $b = \text{ISI}_{\text{Local}}$**, and the intercept $a = -\text{ISI}_{\text{Local}} \cdot \log_{10}(\text{MNPT}_{\text{Derived}})$:
   $$\mathbf{\text{ISI}_{\text{Local}} = b = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k} (X_i - \bar{X})(Y_i - \bar{Y})}{\sum_{i=1}^{k} (X_i - \bar{X})^2}, \quad \text{MNPT}_{\text{Derived}} = 10^{(-a / b)}}$$
   *(From the 5-calibrator table above: $\bar{X} = 1.44804$, $\bar{Y} = 0.37397$, $\sum (X_i - \bar{X})(Y_i - \bar{Y}) = 0.24212$, $\sum (X_i - \bar{X})^2 = 0.23714 \implies \mathbf{\text{ISI}_{\text{Local}} = 1.021}$, $r^2 = \mathbf{0.9999}$).*
3. **Mandatory Acceptance Criteria for Direct `INR` Calibration:**
   - Correlation coefficient **$r^2 \ge 0.990$** across at least $4$ abnormal VKA plasmas plus $1$ normal plasma.
   - Re-verified independent `INR` check control must recover within **$\pm 10.0\%$** of certified value.

#### 6.1.1 Mandatory Cuvette Pre-Warming Timer Rule (`180 Seconds` Target; `600 Seconds` Maximum)
- Every patient plasma or IQC aliquot ($50\ \mu\text{L}$ or $100\ \mu\text{L}$) dispensed into a reaction cuvette must be pre-warmed at $37.0^\circ\text{C}$ for **exactly $180\text{ seconds}$ ($3\text{ minutes}$; range $120\text{–}300\text{ s}$)** prior to starter reagent injection.

Prolonged incubation at $37.0^\circ\text{C}$ in a small open cuvette causes micro-evaporation and rapid thermal decay of Factor V and Factor VIII, artificially prolonging `aPTT` by $+3\text{ to } +8\text{ seconds}$.

### 6.3 In-House 20-Donor Pooled Normal Plasma (`PNP`) Preparation, Qualification & Cryo-Stability Protocol (`CLSI H47-A2` / `ISTH 2020`)
Because $1:1$ Immediate and $2\text{-Hour}$ Incubated Mixing Studies (`SOP 1.3.6`) and Lupus Anticoagulant Normalized Ratios (`SOP 1.3.8`) require a normal reference plasma containing **$\approx 100\text{ IU/dL}$ ($100\%$) of all clotting factors (especially heat-labile Factor VIII and Factor V) and virtually zero residual platelets ($< 1\text{–}5 \times 10^9/\text{L}$)**, commercial lyophilized normal control plasma cannot be blindly substituted unless certified for mixing studies. Sawariya Diagnostic Lab prepares and qualifies **In-House Cryo-Frozen Pooled Normal Plasma (`PNP`)** as follows:
1. **Donor Pool Composition ($n \ge 20$ Healthy Donors):** Collect fresh $3.2\%$ buffered sodium citrate tubes from **$20$ healthy, non-medicated adults ($10$ males and $10$ non-pregnant, non-OCP females, aged $18\text{–}50\text{ years}$, representing `ABO` blood groups proportionally so `O`-group lower `vWF`/`FVIII` is balanced by `A/B/AB` donors)**.
2. **Rapid Double Centrifugation Within $< 30\text{ Minutes}$ of Venipuncture:**
   - Perform **Double-Spin Protocol (`Section 4.3.2`)**: Spin #1 at $2,500 \times g$ for $15\text{ min}$ ($\text{Brake = OFF}$), transfer upper $2/3$ plasma into secondary polypropylene tubes, and perform Spin #2 at $2,500 \times g$ for $15\text{ min}$ ($\text{Brake = OFF}$).
3. **Pooling in Ice-Cooled Polypropylene Vessel & Residual Platelet Verification:**
   - Pool equal volumes ($1.5\text{ mL}$ per donor = $30\text{ mL}$ total pool) inside a pre-chilled ($4^\circ\text{C}$) sterile **polypropylene container** (never glass!).
   - Mix gently by inversion and verify on the `Nihon Kohden MEK-1503` that the pooled `PNP` has a **residual platelet count $\le 2 \times 10^9/\text{L}$ (`<= 2,000/uL`)**.
   - Verify that baseline `PNP` clotting times match the laboratory's established `MNPT` ($12.1 \pm 0.4\text{ s}$) and `MNAPTT` ($29.3 \pm 1.2\text{ s}$), defining $100\%$ normal factor activity ($\ge 80\text{–}120\text{ IU/dL}$).
4. **Cryovial Aliquoting, Snap-Freezing & Thawing Rules:**
   - Dispense **$500\ \mu\text{L}$ ($0.5\text{ mL}$) single-use aliquots** into screw-cap polypropylene cryovials labeled with `[PNP Batch # + Prep Date + Expiry Date + Baseline PT/aPTT]`.
   - Snap-freeze immediately at **$-80^\circ\text{C}$ (stable for $6\text{ months}$)** or in a dedicated non-auto-defrost **$-20^\circ\text{C}$ freezer (stable for $14\text{ days}$)**.
   - **Mandatory Rapid $37.0^\circ\text{C}$ Thawing Rule:** Never thaw frozen `PNP` (or frozen patient plasma) slowly on the benchtop at room temperature! Slow thawing between $0^\circ\text{C}$ and $4^\circ\text{C}$ precipitates **Cryoprecipitate (`Fibrinogen + Factor VIII + vWF + Factor XIII`)**, depleting the supernatant of Factor VIII! Always thaw frozen cryovials by immersing in a **$37.0^\circ\text{C}$ waterbath for exactly $4\text{–}5\text{ minutes}$**, invert gently $5\text{ times}$, and use within $< 2\text{ hours}$ (never refreeze!).

### 7.1 8-Hourly 2-Level Internal Quality Control (`IQC`), Westgard Sigma Rules & `ISO/TS 20914:2019` Measurement Uncertainty ($U$)
Per **ISO/TS 20914:2019 (`Medical laboratories — Practical guidance for the estimation of measurement uncertainty`)**, combined standard uncertainty ($u_c$) incorporates long-term intermediate precision ($u_{\text{rw}}$ from 6-month `IQC` data) and calibrator/trueness uncertainty ($u_{\text{cal}}$ from `MNPT`/`ISI` or `EQAS` bias):
$$u_c = \sqrt{u_{\text{rw}}^2 + u_{\text{cal}}^2}, \quad U_{\text{Expanded (95\%)}} = k \cdot u_c \quad (k = 1.96 \approx 2.0)$$
And the analytical **Sigma Metric ($\sigma$)** is calculated from `EFLM` / `CLIA 2024` Total Allowable Error ($\text{TE}_a\%$), observed `EQAS` bias ($\lvert\text{Bias}\%\rvert$), and long-term $CV_A\%$:
$$\sigma = \frac{\text{TE}_a(\%) - \lvert\text{Bias}(\%)\rvert}{CV_A(\%)}$$

### 7.2 Sample-to-Sample & Reagent Probe Carryover Verification Protocol (`CLSI H47-A2` / `EP10` `H1-H2-H3-L1-L2-L3`)
On automated coagulometers with reusable sample/reagent aspiration probes—or semi-automated systems where starter pipettes approach the cuvette meniscus—trace carryover of **Unfractionated Heparin (`UFH`)**, **Direct Oral Anticoagulants (`Dabigatran` / `Rivaroxaban`)**, or high-potency **Bovine Thrombin (`100 NIH U/mL` from `Clauss Fibrinogen`)** can corrupt the immediately following specimen.

#### 7.2.1 Semi-Annual Carryover Verification Procedure & Worked Benchmark
Every **6 months** (and after probe replacement or fluidic pump maintenance), execute two **6-replicate `H1–H2–H3–L1–L2–L3` sequences**:
1. **Sequence A (Sample Probe Anticoagulant Carryover):**
   - **High Sample ($H_1, H_2, H_3$):** Plasma spiked with Unfractionated Heparin (`1.2 IU/mL`, yielding `aPTT` $\approx 95\text{–}110\text{ s}$) or supratherapeutic Warfarin plasma ($\text{INR} \approx 4.8$, `PT` $\approx 56\text{ s}$).
   - **Low Sample ($L_1, L_2, L_3$):** Normal pooled citrate plasma (`aPTT` $\approx 29.0\text{ s}$, `PT` $\approx 12.1\text{ s}$).
2. **Sequence B (Reagent Probe Thrombin Carryover):**
   - Run $3$ consecutive `Clauss Fibrinogen` assays ($100\text{ NIH U/mL}$ Thrombin reagent), immediately followed by $3$ replicates ($L_1, L_2, L_3$) of Normal Control `aPTT` ($29.3\text{ s}$; if Thrombin carries over, $L_1$ will be falsely shortened compared to $L_3$).
3. **Carryover Calculation & Acceptance Criteria:**
   $$\mathbf{\text{Carryover (\%)} = \frac{L_1 - L_3}{H_3 - L_3} \times 100\%, \quad \Delta L = \lvert L_1 - L_3 \rvert}$$

### 8.2 Analytical & Hardware Troubleshooting Decision Matrix

| **1. Both `Level 1` (Normal) & `Level 2` (Abnormal) `PT` and `aPTT` Suddenly Prolonged ($> +2\text{SD} / +3\text{SD}$)** | **a.** Incubation block temperature drifted outside $37.0 \pm 0.5^\circ\text{C}$ ($< 36.0^\circ\text{C}$ or $> 38.5^\circ\text{C}$).<br>**b.** Control plasmas reconstituted with wrong diluent ($0.9\%\text{ NaCl}$ or tap water) or left at room temp $> 8\text{ h}$.<br>**c.** Starter pipette under-dispensing reagent due to loose tip or worn O-ring. | **Step 1:** Check incubation block liquid temperature with calibrated `Pt100` probe ($37.0 \pm 0.2^\circ\text{C}$).<br>**Step 2:** Reconstitute fresh vials of `Level 1` and `Level 2` control using verified Type-I `CLRW` water ($\ge 10\text{ M}\Omega\cdot\text{cm}$) and calibrated $1,000\ \mu\text{L}$ pipette.<br>**Step 3:** Inspect starter pipette seal and rerun controls. |

## Section 9: Medico-Legal, Patient Safety, `CLSI GP47` Critical Value Governance & Bio-Medical Waste (`BMW 2016/2019`) Compliance

### 9.1 Statutory Medico-Legal Archival Shield (`Consumer Protection Act 2019` & `NABL 112`)
Because anticoagulation-related bleeding or thromboembolism is a high-litigation clinical domain, Sawariya Diagnostic Lab maintains an ironclad **5-Year Medico-Legal & Metrological Evidence Trail**:
1. **Raw Instrument Printout & Clotting Curve Archival:** Every patient's raw clotting time in seconds (`0.1 s` resolution), optical reaction curve (`d(OD)/dt` / `BWA` trace) or mechanical viscosity printout, active Thromboplastin `Lot #`, programmed `MNPT`, and `ISI` are automatically bound to the patient's LIMS record (`DPDP Act 2023` encrypted) and retained for **5 years**.
2. **Prohibition of Verbal or Unverified `INR` Alterations:** The LIMS formula $\text{INR} = (\text{PT}/\text{MNPT})^{\text{ISI}}$ is hard-locked. No bench technician can manually overwrite an `INR` value or alter `MNPT`/`ISI` without dual cryptographic authentication by the Chief Hematopathologist.

### 9.2 `CLSI GP47` 30-Minute Critical Value Read-Back Governance
Whenever a validated coagulation result crosses a **Critical Life-Threatening Action Threshold**, the bench technologist or Hematopathologist must execute a **verbal telephone notification with mandatory verbatim read-back within $\le 30\text{ minutes}$** and log `[Date/Time + Receiver Name/Designation + Read-Back Verified + Caller Sign]`:
- **Prothrombin Time (`INR`):** **`INR > 4.5`** (or **`INR > 5.0`** in mechanical heart valve patients — high risk of spontaneous intracranial/GI hemorrhage!).
- **Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (`aPTT`):** **`aPTT > 70.0 seconds`** in non-heparinized patients, or **`aPTT > 100.0 seconds`** in patients on intravenous Unfractionated Heparin (`UFH`).
- **Clauss Fibrinogen:** **`Fibrinogen < 100 mg/dL`** (`< 1.0 g/L` — imminent bleeding risk in DIC, liver failure, or postpartum hemorrhage requiring cryoprecipitate!).
- **Biphasic Waveform Analysis (`BWA`):** **Positive `BWA` Sepsis/DIC Slope Alert (`Slope_1 <= -0.15%T/sec`)** in any febrile, emergency, or ICU patient.

### 9.3 Bio-Medical Waste (`BMW`) Management Rules (`2016 / 2019 Amendment`) at the Coagulation Bench
1. **Yellow Category (Non-Chlorinated Plastic Bag — Incineration / Plasma Pyrolysis):** Blood-soaked gauze, cotton swabs, and absorbent bench pads contaminated with human plasma.
2. **Red Category (Non-Chlorinated Autoclave-Safe Plastic Bag — Autoclaving & Shredding/Recycling):** Used plastic reaction cuvettes (after draining liquid into $1\%$ sodium hypochlorite), plastic polypropylene transfer pipettes, disposable plastic micropipette tips, and capped plastic $3.2\%$ Sodium Citrate vacutainer tubes.
3. **Blue Category (Puncture-Proof Cardboard/Plastic Box with Blue Marking — Disinfection & Glass/Metal Recycling):** Empty glass lyophilized reagent vials (`Thromboplastin`, `Control Plasma`, `dRVVT` glass bottles) and glass ampoules.
4. **White Category (Translucent Puncture-Proof Sharps Container with $1\%$ Sodium Hypochlorite):** Any metallic sharps, needles, or discarded stainless-steel viscosity microbeads if separated from plastic cuvettes.
5. **Liquid Effluent Disinfection:** All liquid waste aspirated into the automated coagulometer waste bottle must contain a $1:10$ volume of **freshly prepared $10\%$ Sodium Hypochlorite (achieving final $1\%$ active chlorine)** with a minimum **$30\text{ minute}$ contact time** prior to discharge into the Effluent Treatment Plant (`ETP`).
