## 2. Pre-Analytical Specimen Collection, Dietary/Drug Restrictions & Stability Matrix

### 2.1 Why `FIT` Eliminates Dietary Restrictions Required for Guaiac (`gFOBT`)
| Pre-Analytical Variable | Guaiac Peroxidase Test (`gFOBT`) | Fecal Immunochemical Test (`FIT` / `iFOBT` — Primary Standard) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Target Molecule Detected** | **Intact Heme moiety** (pseudoperoxidase activity of $\text{Fe}^{2+}/\text{Fe}^{3+}$ porphyrin ring) | **Intact Human Globin protein chains** ($\alpha/\beta$ polypeptide epitopes) |
| **Anatomical GI Bleed Specificity** | **Upper AND Lower GI Tract** (Heme resists gastric acid and pancreatic proteases, though some porphyrin degradation occurs) | **Selective for Lower GI (Colorectal) Bleeding!** *(Upper GI blood from stomach/duodenum has its globin chains enzymatically digested by gastric pepsin and pancreatic trypsin/chymotrypsin before reaching the colon!)* |
| **72-Hour Dietary Restrictions** | **MANDATORY:** Avoid **red meat, liver, poultry, fish** (dietary animal myoglobin/hemoglobin) and **peroxidase-rich raw vegetables/fruits** (turnips, horseradish, radish, broccoli, cauliflower, cantaloupe) for $72\text{ hours}$ prior! | **NONE REQUIRED:** Monoclonal antibodies are $100\%$ specific for **human** globin and do not cross-react with animal myoglobin/hemoglobin or plant peroxidases! |
| **Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid) Restriction** | **MANDATORY:** Avoid **Vitamin C $> 250\text{ mg/day}$** and citrus juices for $72\text{h}$ (Ascorbic acid reduces oxidized blue guaiaconic acid $\rightarrow$ **FALSE-NEGATIVE `gFOBT`**!). | **NONE REQUIRED:** Immunochemical antigen-antibody binding is completely unaffected by Ascorbic Acid! |
| **NSAIDs / Aspirin / Anticoagulants** | Avoid high-dose NSAIDs/Aspirin for $7\text{ days}$ (causes benign gastric mucosal micro-bleeding $> 2\text{ mL/day}$ $\rightarrow$ positive `gFOBT`). | Does not interfere analytically (and gastric micro-bleeding globin is digested before reaching stool). |

### 2.2 Specimen Collection & Stability Rules
1. **Non-Bleeding Window (Both `FIT` and `gFOBT`):** Do NOT collect stool during or within **$3\text{ days}$ after menstruation**, or during active **bleeding hemorrhoids, anal fissures, hematuria, or severe epistaxis/gum bleeding**.
2. **`FIT` Sampling Probe Protocol:** Twist the grooved sampling wand into **$3\text{ to }6\text{ different surface areas}$** of the stool specimen (where mucosal blood streaks adhere as stool passes a colonic polyp/tumor), then insert into the extraction buffer tube ($10\text{ mg stool}$ per $2.0\text{ mL}$ buffer).
   - **`FIT` Stability:** Unbuffered raw stool must be sampled within **$\le 2\text{ hours}$** at room temperature (colonic bacterial proteases degrade human globin at $> 25^\circ\text{C}$!). Once placed into the **HEPES/Azide/BSA protease-inhibitor extraction buffer tube**, globin is stable for **$5\text{ days}$ at $18\text{–}25^\circ\text{C}$** and **$14\text{ days}$ at $2\text{–}8^\circ\text{C}$**.
3. **Stool `pH` & Reducing Substances Stability (Pediatric Watery Stool):**
   - **Strict $< 1\text{ Hour}$ Freshness Rule:** Unabsorbed colonic lactose/carbohydrates are rapidly fermented by colonic bacteria into lactic/acetic/butyric short-chain fatty acids (`SCFAs`) and $\text{H}_2/\text{CO}_2$ gas within **$1\text{ to }2\text{ hours}$ at room temperature**, falsely lowering reducing sugar levels!
   - **Diaper Collection Pitfall (`CLSI`):** **NEVER scrape watery stool soaked into an absorbent disposable diaper!** The liquid whey fraction containing dissolved lactose and organic acids soaks into the diaper gel matrix, leaving only solid fecal residue and causing **false-negative reducing substances and falsely normal $\text{pH}$**! Line the infant's diaper with a **non-absorbent plastic sheet** or inverted diaper liner to collect the liquid fraction.

## 3. Hardware-Agnostic Analytical SOP: `FIT`, `gFOBT` & Stool `pH`/Reducing Substances

### 3.1 Fecal Immunochemical Test (`FIT` — Primary Method)
1. Shake the grooved fecal extraction buffer tube vigorously for $15\text{ seconds}$ and allow debris to settle for $2\text{ minutes}$.
2. Snap the dropper tip and dispense **$3\text{ drops}$ ($\sim 90\text{ }\mu\text{L}$)** into the sample well (`S`) of the anti-human hemoglobin lateral-flow cassette.
3. Read strictly at **$5\text{ to }10\text{ minutes}$**:
   - **Positive (`C + T Lines`):** Human fecal hemoglobin $\ge 50\text{ ng/mL}$ buffer ($\equiv \mathbf{10\text{ }\mu\text{g Hb / g feces}}$ — international colorectal cancer screening cutoff).
   - **High-Dose Hook Effect Override (Gross Melena / Hematochezia):** In frankly bloody or tarry stools where $\text{Hb} > 2,000\text{ }\mu\text{g/g}$, excess free hemoglobin can saturate the anti-globin antibodies and produce a **falsely weak or negative `FIT`**! If stool is visibly bloody/black with a negative `FIT`, dilute the extraction buffer **$1:20$ in normal saline** and re-test, and perform a parallel `gFOBT`.

### 3.2 Guaiac Peroxidase Test (`gFOBT` — Backup / Upper GI Heme Screen)
- **Chemical Principle:** The **Heme** moiety of hemoglobin acts as a heat-stable pseudoperoxidase that catalyzes the transfer of oxygen from **Hydrogen Peroxide ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$, $3\text{–}5\%$ in ethanol)** to **$\alpha\text{-Guaiaconic Acid}$** (impregnated in filter paper), oxidizing colorless guaiac into a **conjugated Blue Quinone Dye** within $30\text{–}60\text{ seconds}$ (Analytical threshold: **$\sim 600\text{–}1,000\text{ }\mu\text{g Hb / g stool}$**, corresponding to $> 10\text{–}20\text{ mL/day}$ GI blood loss vs normal physiological blood loss of $0.5\text{–}1.5\text{ mL/day}$).

### 3.3 Stool `pH` & Fecal Reducing Substances (Benedict's / Kerry & Anderson Modification)
1. **Stool `pH` Measurement:** Touch calibrated narrow-range indicator strip ($\text{pH } 4.0\text{–}8.0$, $\Delta 0.5$) or flat-surface digital $\text{pH}$ electrode directly to the liquid phase of fresh watery stool (or $1:2$ distilled water suspension).
2. **Fecal Reducing Substances (Kerry & Anderson Standard Method):**
   - Mix **$1\text{ volume}$ ($1.0\text{ mL}$) of liquid stool** with **$2\text{ volumes}$ ($2.0\text{ mL}$) of distilled water** in a test tube and centrifuge briefly at $500 \times g$ for $2\text{ minutes}$ to pellet coarse fecal debris.
   - Transfer **$15\text{ drops}$ ($0.75\text{ mL}$) of fecal supernatant** into **$5.0\text{ mL}$ of Benedict's Qualitative Reagent** (or add $1$ Clinitest tablet).
   - Boil in a $100^\circ\text{C}$ water bath for **$2\text{ to }3\text{ minutes}$**, allow to cool, and grade color:
     - **Blue / Cloudy Green ($< 0.25\text{ g/dL}$ [$< 0.25\%$]):** **Negative (Normal)**
     - **Yellowish-Green ($0.25\text{ – }0.50\text{ g/dL}$):** **Trace / Borderline**
     - **Yellow to Orange ($0.50\text{ – }1.0\text{ g/dL}$ [`2+`/`3+`]):** **Positive (Carbohydrate Malabsorption)**
     - **Brick-Red Precipitate ($\ge 1.0\text{–}2.0\text{ g/dL}$ [`4+`]):** **Strongly Positive**
   - *Note for Sucrase-Isomaltase Deficiency:* Because **Sucrose** is a non-reducing disaccharide, boil a duplicate fecal supernatant with **$1.0\text{ mL}$ of $1\text{ N HCl}$ for $30\text{ seconds}$** (acid hydrolysis of $\text{Sucrose} \rightarrow \text{Glucose} + \text{Fructose}$), neutralize with $1\text{ N NaOH}$, and repeat Benedict's test!

## 7. Pathophysiological Correlations & Smart LIMS Reflex Cascades

### 7.1 Why Upper GI Bleeding (Melena / Peptic Ulcer) Can Yield a Negative `FIT`
Clinicians frequently order `FIT` in patients with suspected peptic ulcer disease or gastritis. Because **gastric pepsin and pancreatic proteases cleave human globin polypeptides during small-intestinal transit**, up to $70\%\text{–}80\%$ of upper GI bleeds (<100 mL) test **Negative on `FIT`** (even while testing positive on heme-based `gFOBT`). Conversely, in **Melena (black tarry stool from $> 50\text{–}100\text{ mL}$ upper GI blood)**, high heme/globin concentrations can trigger either proteolytic globin loss OR a high-dose prozone hook effect. **Clinical Rule:** If Upper GI bleeding is suspected, **Upper GI Endoscopy (Esophagogastroduodenoscopy)**—not `FIT`—is the diagnostic test of choice!

### 7.2 Pathophysiology of Acidic Stool ($\text{pH} \le 5.5$) + Positive Reducing Substances ($\ge 0.5\%$)
In **Secondary Lactase Deficiency** (following acute rotaviral/bacterial enteritis, giardiasis, or celiac disease stripping the brush-border lactase enzyme at the villous tips), unhydrolyzed **Lactose** exerts an intraluminal osmotic pull (causing watery osmotic diarrhea). Upon reaching the colon, anaerobic bacteria ferment lactose into **Lactic, Acetic, Propionic, and Butyric acids** ($\text{pH}$ drops to $\le 5.0\text{–}5.5$, causing classic fiery-red **perianal skin excoriation**) plus $\text{H}_2$ and $\text{CO}_2$ gas (bloating/flatus), while residual unfermented lactose gives a `2+` to `4+` Benedict's reducing reaction.

## 9. Conditional Band-Specific Pathologist Sign-Out Library (LIMS Auto-Comments)

- **`[BAND-FIT-NEGATIVE]` (`FIT Negative [<10 ug Hb/g]`):**
  > *"Fecal Occult Blood Test (Immunochemical FIT for Human Globin) is NEGATIVE (<10 ug Hb/g stool). MANDATORY CLINICAL SHIELD: (1) FIT is specific for intact human globin from the LOWER gastrointestinal tract (colon/rectum); upper GI blood (stomach/duodenum) undergoes peptic/tryptic globin digestion and may not be detected by FIT. (2) Because colorectal polyps and carcinomas bleed intermittently, a single negative FIT does not exclude gastrointestinal neoplasia—if unexplained Iron Deficiency Anemia, weight loss, or altered bowel habits are present, direct endoscopic evaluation (Colonoscopy / Upper GI Endoscopy) is recommended."*
- **`[BAND-FIT-POSITIVE]` (`FIT Positive [>=10 ug Hb/g]`):**
  > *"FECAL OCCULT BLOOD TEST (FIT) IS POSITIVE for human hemoglobin/globin (>=10 ug Hb/g stool), indicating lower gastrointestinal mucosal blood loss. Differential includes colorectal adenomatous polyps, colorectal carcinoma, inflammatory bowel disease (Ulcerative Colitis / Crohn's), diverticular disease, angiodysplasia, or benign anorectal bleeding (hemorrhoids, anal fissures, or menstrual contamination). Per international gastroenterology guidelines (ACG/USPSTF), a positive FIT warrants diagnostic Colonoscopy and should NOT be repeated to 're-check' if it turns negative."*
- **`[BAND-LACTOSE-MALABSORPTION]` (`Stool pH <= 5.5` + `Fecal Reducing Substances >= 0.5% [2+ to 4+]`):**
  > *"CARBOHYDRATE / LACTOSE MALABSORPTION PATTERN: Acidic stool reaction (pH <=5.5) accompanied by positive Fecal Reducing Substances (>=0.5 g/dL) confirms colonic bacterial fermentation of unabsorbed dietary reducing sugars, classically seen in Primary or Secondary Lactase Deficiency (e.g., post-viral/rotaviral gastroenteritis in infants/children). Correlate clinically with response to a lactose-free dietary trial."*
