## Section 1: Purpose, Scope, Regulatory Framework & Metrological Traceability

### 1.1 Purpose & Clinical Scope
This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) governs the complete metrological lifecycle, operational validation, quality control engineering, and biosafety protocols for the Department of Medical Microbiology at Sawariya Diagnostic Lab, Charkhi Dadri, Haryana. It provides operational mandates for:
1. Formulation, reconstitution, sterilization, aseptic pouring, physical validation, and Growth Promotion Testing (GPT) of all dehydrated and enriched culture media in compliance with `CLSI M22-A3` and `ISO 11133:2014/Amd 2:2020`.
2. Strict two-autoclave physical segregation separating Clean Media Preparation ($121.0^\circ\text{C} \times 15\text{ min}$) from Biohazard Decontamination ($121.0^\circ\text{C} \times 45\text{ min}$), monitored by Class 5 chemical integrating indicators and weekly *Geobacillus stearothermophilus* ATCC 7953 biological spore challenges.
3. Class II Type A2 Biosafety Cabinet (BSC) aerodynamic validation, annual `NSF/ANSI 49` certification, daily airflow monitoring, and germicidal UV-C cycle governance.
4. Two-tier cryo-preservation and propagation hierarchy of primary American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) reference control strains under a strict maximum of four passages ($P \le 4$) to eliminate genetic and antimicrobial resistance drift.
5. Continuous 24/7 incubator, refrigerator, and ultra-low freezer thermal mapping with automated SMS/audible telemetry.
6. Daily internal quality control of bacteriological and mycological staining reagents (Gram, Ziehl-Neelsen, Albert's, India ink, 10% KOH) prior to analyzing and releasing patient diagnostic specimens.

## Section 2: Metrological Infrastructure, Quality Engineering & Biosafety Science

### 2.1 Culture Media Lifecycle Engineering (CLSI M22-A3 / ISO 11133)
The preparation of culture media is an analytical manufacturing process requiring rigorous physicochemical and biological verification at every step:
1. **Raw Powder Inspection & Storage:** Dehydrated media powders must be stored in an airtight, desiccated, light-protected cabinet at $15.0\text{–}25.0^\circ\text{C}$. Inspect upon delivery for container seal integrity, expiration date, and hygroscopic caking. Any powder displaying discoloration, caking, or oxidation is quarantined and condemned.
2. **Reconstitution Water Metrology:** Dissolution must strictly use Type II analytical grade purified water ($\text{resistivity} > 1.0\text{ M}\Omega\cdot\text{cm}$ at $25.0^\circ\text{C}$, $\text{conductivity} < 1.0\ \mu\text{S/cm}$, total organic carbon $< 50\ \mu\text{g/L}$, and heavy metal free). Tap water or untreated reverse osmosis permeate is strictly prohibited due to divalent cation contamination ($\text{Ca}^{2+}, \text{Mg}^{2+}$) that corrupts aminoglycoside and fluoroquinolone activity.
3. **Dissolution & Pre-Autoclave pH Adjustment:** Heat media containing agar with continuous magnetic stirring until complete clarity is achieved at $95.0\text{–}100.0^\circ\text{C}$. Never scorch agar against heating mantles. Measure initial $\text{pH}$ at $25.0^\circ\text{C}$ using a temperature-compensated, NIST-calibrated pH electrode. Account for the standard $-0.1\text{ to }-0.2\ \text{pH}$ shift induced by autoclaving by pre-adjusting with sterile $0.1\text{ M NaOH}$ or $0.1\text{ M HCl}$.
4. **Sterilization Regime & Heat-Labile Protocols:**
   - Standard Media: Sterilize at $121.0 \pm 1.0^\circ\text{C}$ ($15.0 \pm 1.0\text{ psi}$) for $15\text{ minutes}$ in the Clean Media Autoclave.
   - Heat-Sensitive Carbohydrate Broths: Sterilize at $115.0^\circ\text{C}$ ($10.0\text{ psi}$) for $10\text{ minutes}$ to prevent caramelization of maltose, lactose, or xylose.
   - Non-Autoclavable Selective Media (XLD, DCA, TCBS): Heat with agitation until boiling ($100.0^\circ\text{C}$) for $60\text{ seconds}$ to dissolve completely. **NEVER AUTOCLAVE XLD OR TCBS AGAR**; autoclaving destroys sodium deoxycholate and thiosulfate selectivity, resulting in total agar liquefaction and excessive toxicity.
5. **Post-Autoclave Equilibration & Supplement Addition:** Transfer autoclaved media flasks to a circulating water bath maintained at $45.0\text{–}50.0^\circ\text{C}$ for $30\text{–}45\text{ minutes}$ until the flask wall stabilizes at $48.0^\circ\text{C}$.
   - Defibrinated Sheep Blood ($5.0\%\ \text{v/v}$): Add aseptically only when media reaches $45.0\text{–}50.0^\circ\text{C}$. Adding blood above $50.0^\circ\text{C}$ lyses erythrocytes (turning blood agar brown and releasing catalase/NADase). Adding below $45.0^\circ\text{C}$ causes premature agar gelation and bubbles.
   - Chocolate Agar: Add defibrinated blood at $48.0^\circ\text{C}$, then heat smoothly to $75.0\text{–}80.0^\circ\text{C}$ in a water bath with gentle swirling until a uniform chocolate-brown color is achieved, inactivating RBC enzymes while liberating Factor X (hemin) and Factor V (NAD).
6. **Standardized Plate Pouring & Mueller-Hinton Agar (MHA) Depth Control:** Pour $20.0 \pm 1.0\text{ mL}$ of molten agar into sterile $90\text{ mm}$ vented petri dishes on a mechanically leveled granite pouring bench inside the laminar flow enclosure.
   - **Critical MHA Depth Rule:** Mueller-Hinton Agar depth **MUST be $4.0 \pm 0.5\text{ mm}$** (verified by calibrated digital vernier caliper across 4 orthogonal points).
   - If agar depth is $< 3.5\text{ mm}$, antibiotic molecules diffuse more rapidly laterally, producing artificially large inhibition zones (**false susceptibility**).
   - If agar depth is $> 4.5\text{ mm}$, downward diffusion dilutes the drug concentration at the surface, creating artificially small inhibition zones (**false resistance**).
7. **Sterility & Growth Promotion Verification (GPT):**
   - **Sterility Sampling:** Incubate uninoculated plates representing $3.0\%\text{–}5.0\%$ of every prepared batch ($n \ge 5\text{ plates}$) at $35.0 \pm 1.0^\circ\text{C}$ for $48\text{ hours}$ (and $25.0 \pm 1.0^\circ\text{C}$ for $120\text{ hours}$ for fungal SDA media). Zero colonies must emerge.
   - **Growth Promotion Testing:** Challenge plates with low-titer inocula ($10\text{–}100\text{ CFU}$) of designated ATCC strains. Recovery must yield $\ge 70\%$ colony count relative to reference batch with typical morphology. Selective media must demonstrate complete suppression of challenge organisms ($> 1.0 \times 10^4\text{ CFU}$).

### 2.2 Autoclave Engineering, Segregation & Sterilization Assurance
Sawariya Diagnostic Lab enforces strict physical and operational segregation across two dedicated steam sterilizers:
- **Autoclave #1 (Clean Media Preparation Suite):** Dedicated solely to clean, uninoculated media formulation and clean laboratory glassware. Cycle parameters: $121.0 \pm 1.0^\circ\text{C}$ at $15.0 \pm 1.0\text{ psi}$ for $15\text{ minutes}$.
- **Autoclave #2 (Dedicated Decontamination / Biohazard Suite):** Dedicated exclusively to pre-treatment decontamination of infectious clinical specimens, used culture plates, blood culture bottles, and biohazardous waste under `Bio-Medical Waste Rules 2016`. Cycle parameters: $121.0 \pm 1.0^\circ\text{C}$ at $15.0 \pm 1.0\text{ psi}$ for $45\text{ minutes}$ (extended duration required to penetrate dense plastic, agar volumes, and organic matter). **CLEAN MEDIA MUST NEVER ENTER AUTOCLAVE #2.**

```
Sterilization Assurance Hierarchy (NABL 112 / ISO 11133)
========================================================================================
Level 1 (Physical)   --> Chart Recorder/Data Logger: 121.0°C ± 1.0°C, 15.0 ± 1.0 psi, 15 min
Level 2 (Chemical)   --> Class 1 External Tape + Class 5 Chemical Integrator in Load Center
Level 3 (Biological) --> Weekly Geobacillus stearothermophilus ATCC 7953 (>10^6 endospores)
                         Incubation at 55.0–60.0°C for 24–48 h: Purple = PASS, Yellow = FAIL
========================================================================================
```

- **Biological Spore Monitoring Protocol:** Every Monday morning, place a self-contained biological indicator ampoule containing *Geobacillus stearothermophilus* ATCC 7953 ($> 1.0 \times 10^6$ viable endospores suspended in soy broth with bromocresol purple indicator) inside the geometric center of the densest media pack.
- Following the cycle, incubate the autoclaved ampoule alongside an un-autoclaved positive control ampoule at $55.0\text{–}60.0^\circ\text{C}$ in a calibrated dry-bath incubator for $24\text{–}48\text{ hours}$.
- **Pass Criteria:** Test ampoule remains clear purple (negative for growth; successful thermal kill). Positive control ampoule turns turbid yellow within $24\text{ hours}$ (confirming spore viability).
- **Sterilization Failure Action:** If the test ampoule turns yellow, initiate immediate emergency autoclave quarantine, lock-out/tag-out (LOTO), and complete batch recall of all media sterilized during the preceding cycle.

### 2.3 Class II Type A2 Biosafety Cabinet (BSC) Aerodynamics & Containment
The Class II Type A2 Biosafety Cabinet provides personnel, environmental, and product protection through precise aerodynamic balancing:
1. **Airflow Physics:** $70\%$ of internal cabinet air is recirculated through the supply HEPA filter, providing ISO Class 5 downward laminar flow over the work surface. $30\%$ of air is drawn through the exhaust HEPA filter and discharged into the facility.
2. **Velocity Specifications:**
   - Inflow Face Velocity: Maintained at $100 \pm 10\text{ fpm}$ ($0.51 \pm 0.05\text{ m/s}$) across the sash opening height of $200\text{ mm}$ ($8\text{ inches}$).
   - Downflow Velocity: Maintained at $55\text{–}65\text{ fpm}$ ($0.28\text{–}0.33\text{ m/s}$) uniformly across the internal horizontal grid.
3. **Magnehelic Differential Pressure Gauge:** Continuously monitors static pressure drop across the supply HEPA filter. Baseline certified range: $0.4\text{–}0.7\text{ inches of water gauge (in WG)}$ ($10\text{–}18\text{ mm WG}$). A pressure spike $> 1.0\text{ in WG}$ indicates filter loading; a pressure drop $< 0.3\text{ in WG}$ indicates filter breach or motor blower failure.
4. **Annual Third-Party NSF/ANSI 49 Certification:** Mandatory annual re-certification includes:
   - Polyalphaolefin (PAO) / Dioctyl phthalate (DOP) aerosol challenge testing across supply and exhaust HEPA filters (penetration must not exceed $0.01\%$).
   - Smoke airflow pattern visualization (inward air barrier along sash, downward laminar flow, zero vortex or turbulence over work zone).
   - Electrical safety, ground resistance, and sash alarm trigger verification.
5. **Decontamination Protocol:**
   - Disinfect work deck with $70\%$ isopropanol before and after each operational session.
   - **Chlorine Caution:** Never use sodium hypochlorite (bleach) directly on stainless steel without immediate neutralization and rinsing with sterile water followed by $70\%$ ethanol; hypochlorite ions cause severe pitting corrosion and micro-fractures in stainless steel.
   - For Mycobacteriology/AFB processing, use tuberculocidal phenolic disinfectants ($5\%$ phenol / Lysol) with a $15\text{ minute}$ contact time.
   - **Germicidal UV-C ($254\text{ nm}$) Cycle:** Operate UV lamps for $15\text{ minutes}$ prior to bench setup. UV irradiation must only occur when the cabinet is completely unoccupied with the front glass sash closed. Monitor lamp output quarterly with a calibrated UV radiometer ($> 40\ \mu\text{W/cm}^2$ required at work surface). Replace lamps upon reaching $1,500\text{ operational hours}$.

### 2.6 Daily Staining & Biochemical Reagent Quality Control
Diagnostic clinical smear reading is legally and metrologically invalid unless authenticated by a corresponding passing internal QC smear processed during the same working shift:
1. **Gram Stain Internal QC:** Prepare a dual-spot control slide carrying *S. aureus* ATCC 25923 and *E. coli* ATCC 25922. The Gram-positive control must demonstrate crisp, deep purple cocci in clusters. The Gram-negative control must demonstrate bright pinkish-red bacilli with zero retained crystal violet. Any over-decolorization or under-decolorization immediately halts slide reading.
2. **Ziehl-Neelsen (ZN) & Auramine-O Acid Fast Stain QC:** Process one dual-well control slide containing a heat-fixed smear of known acid-fast bacilli (*Mycobacterium tuberculosis* H37Ra or *M. smegmatis*) alongside a negative control smear (*E. coli* ATCC 25922). ZN positive control must show bright magenta-pink beaded bacilli against a light methylene blue background. Negative control must stain completely blue. Under fluorescence microscopy (Auramine-O), AFB must fluoresce brilliant golden-yellow/green against a dark background.
3. **Albert's Diphtheria Stain QC:** Validate using a control smear of *Corynebacterium xerosis* or *Corynebacterium diphtheriae* NCTC. The bacilli must show light green cytoplasm with prominent dark bluish-black metachromatic volutin granules positioned at the poles (producing the classical L- and V-shaped Chinese letter arrangement).
4. **India Ink & Nigrosin Cryptococcal QC:** Mix one drop of India ink with *Cryptococcus neoformans* ATCC 90112 (positive control showing wide, clear, refringent polysaccharide capsule halo against dark carbon particle background) and *Candida albicans* ATCC 90028 (negative control showing yeast cells lacking halo).
5. **Biochemical Testing IQC:**
   - Catalase ($3.0\%\ \text{H}_2\text{O}_2$): Positive (*S. aureus* ATCC 25923 $\to$ vigorous gas bubbling); Negative (*Streptococcus pyogenes* ATCC 19615 $\to$ zero bubbles).
   - Cytochrome Oxidase ($1.0\%$ Tetramethyl-$p$-phenylenediamine): Positive (*P. aeruginosa* ATCC 27853 $\to$ deep purple within $10\text{ seconds}$); Negative (*E. coli* ATCC 25922 $\to$ colorless).
   - Coagulase (Citrated Rabbit Plasma $1:4$): Positive (*S. aureus* ATCC 25923 $\to$ firm clot at $37.0^\circ\text{C}$ in $4\text{ hours}$); Negative (*S. epidermidis* ATCC 12228 $\to$ free-flowing liquid).
   - 0.5 McFarland Standard Nephelometry: Calibrate barium sulfate and latex standards monthly using a calibrated spectrophotometer at $625\text{ nm}$ wavelength ($1\text{ cm}$ light path; acceptable absorbance range: $0.080\text{ to }0.100\text{ OD}$, corresponding to $1.5 \times 10^8\text{ CFU/mL}$).

### SOP-01: Culture Media Reconstitution, Autoclaving, Pouring & Depth Verification
1. Don personal protective equipment: lab coat, heat-resistant autoclave gloves, nitrile exam gloves, and safety face shield.
2. Tare the calibrated analytical balance. Weigh dehydrated powder to within $\pm 0.05\text{ g}$ of formulation specifications.
3. Dispense half the required volume of Type II analytical grade water into an Erlenmeyer flask (maximum capacity: twice the media volume). Add media powder gradually while swirling to prevent clumping. Rinse weighing container with remaining water.
4. Place on a magnetic stirrer hotplate; heat with continuous agitation until the agar boils vigorously for $60\text{ seconds}$ and becomes completely transparent.
5. Cool to $25.0^\circ\text{C}$; measure initial $\text{pH}$ using the NIST-calibrated pH meter. Adjust if required with $0.1\text{ M NaOH}$ or $0.1\text{ M HCl}$.
6. Cap flask with non-absorbent cotton plug wrapped in gauze and aluminum foil. Affix external Class 1 process indicator tape.
7. Transfer to Clean Media Autoclave #1. Run standard cycle: $121.0 \pm 1.0^\circ\text{C}$ for $15\text{ minutes}$.
8. Upon cycle completion and chamber pressure return to $0.0\text{ psi}$, transfer flask immediately to a circulating water bath maintained at $48.0 \pm 1.0^\circ\text{C}$ for $30\text{–}45\text{ minutes}$.
9. Transfer equilibrated flask into the certified laminar flow hood. Aseptically pour $20.0 \pm 1.0\text{ mL}$ into each $90\text{ mm}$ petri dish. Use a flame to quickly burst any surface air bubbles.
10. Allow plates to solidify undisturbed on the leveled granite bench for $30\text{ minutes}$.
11. Measure agar depth across 4 equidistant quadrants on 3 random plates using a calibrated depth gauge. Value must fall within $4.0 \pm 0.5\text{ mm}$.
12. Bag plates in sterile polyethylene sleeves; label with Media Name, Lot Number, Date of Preparation, and Expiration Date. Store inverted at $2.0\text{–}8.0^\circ\text{C}$.

### SOP-02: Clean Autoclave Operation, Load Configuration & Chemical Integration
1. Perform daily pre-start inspection: verify chamber water level using distilled water; clear drain strainer of debris; check door gasket for cracks.
2. Load media flasks in perforated stainless-steel baskets. Maintain minimum $25\text{ mm}$ spacing between flasks to ensure rapid steam circulation.
3. Place a calibrated Class 5 chemical integrating indicator strip inside the geometric center of the largest flask or dense central load pack.
4. Close and seal the autoclave door; engage safety interlock mechanism.
5. Select Program 1: $121.0^\circ\text{C}$, $15\text{ minutes}$ holding time, slow exhaust (liquid cycle to prevent media boilovers).
6. Verify temperature and pressure ascent on the digital datalogger. Confirm steam exhaust valve closes at $100.0^\circ\text{C}$, displacing all air.
7. Start timing once temperature reaches $121.0^\circ\text{C}$ and pressure reaches $15.0\text{ psi}$.
8. At cycle conclusion, allow pressure to drop naturally to $0.0\text{ psi}$ and temperature to drop below $80.0^\circ\text{C}$ before cracking the door open $2\text{ cm}$.
9. Retrieve the Class 5 integrator strip. Confirm indicator bar has migrated past the "SAFE / ACCEPT" threshold. Log results in Autoclave Register.

### SOP-03: Biological Spore Indicator (*G. stearothermophilus* ATCC 7953) Protocol
1. Retrieve two *Geobacillus stearothermophilus* ATCC 7953 self-contained spore ampoules ($> 1.0 \times 10^6\text{ CFU}$) from the $2.0\text{–}8.0^\circ\text{C}$ refrigerator. Verify lot number and expiration date.
2. Label one ampoule "TEST" and the other "POSITIVE CONTROL".
3. Place the "TEST" ampoule inside the center of the largest media container during the first Monday sterilization cycle. Keep "CONTROL" ampoule at room temperature.
4. Run standard cycle ($121.0^\circ\text{C} \times 15\text{ min}$). Retrieve test ampoule after chamber cools.
5. Inspect test ampoule: crush internal glass nutrient media ampoule using manufacturer activator tool. Crush positive control ampoule in identical manner.
6. Place both ampoules in the dry-bath incubator set at $56.0 \pm 1.0^\circ\text{C}$.
7. Read ampoules at $24\text{ hours}$ and $48\text{ hours}$:
   - **Test Ampoule:** Must remain purple and clear ($\text{PASS} \to \text{Sterilization Achieved}$).
   - **Control Ampoule:** Must turn yellow and turbid ($\text{PASS} \to \text{Spore Viability Confirmed}$).
8. If test ampoule turns yellow, declare immediate sterilization failure: quarantine autoclave, tag "OUT OF ORDER", notify Quality Manager, and quarantine all media batches prepared since last passing spore test.

### SOP-04: Class II Type A2 Biosafety Cabinet Daily Operation & Disinfection
1. Power on cabinet blower and fluorescent light $15\text{ minutes}$ prior to beginning work to purge internal air volume.
2. Inspect front Magnehelic differential pressure gauge. Verify reading is within certified normal range ($0.4\text{–}0.7\text{ in WG}$).
3. Adjust front sliding sash to the designated certified working height mark ($200\text{ mm}$). Verify audible sash alarm is active.
4. Wipe work deck, back wall, and side glass with $70\%$ isopropanol using lint-free wipers. Work from clean to dirty zones.
5. Place all necessary items, reagents, and biohazard containers inside cabinet before beginning. Do not block front or rear intake grilles.
6. Segregate work flow strictly: clean items on left, working zone in center, discard bucket/waste on right.
7. Perform manipulations slowly and smoothly to avoid disrupting the protective front laminar air barrier.
8. Upon completing manipulations, wipe all container exteriors with $70\%$ IPA before removing from cabinet.
9. Disinfect work deck with $70\%$ IPA. Allow blower to operate for an additional $15\text{ minutes}$ purge cycle.
10. Lower sash completely. Activate UV-C germicidal lamp ($254\text{ nm}$) for $15\text{ minutes}$. Record UV lamp operating hours in equipment log.

### SOP-05: ATCC Reference Culture Revival, Master Cryo-Banking & Working Subculture
1. To reconstitute a primary lyophilized pellet (Passage P0), aseptically open the glass ampoule and add $0.5\text{ mL}$ of sterile Tryptic Soy Broth (TSB).
2. Swirl gently to rehydrate. Inoculate onto non-selective Blood Agar and MacConkey Agar plates. Incubate at $35.0 \pm 1.0^\circ\text{C}$ for $18\text{–}24\text{ hours}$.
3. Inspect colonies for purity, Gram stain morphology, and definitive biochemical identity (e.g. catalase, oxidase, automated ID).
4. Harvest pure colonies and suspend in sterile Cryo-Protective Broth ($15\%\text{ v/v}$ glycerol in TSB) to reach a $3.0\text{ McFarland}$ turbidity.
5. Aliquot $1.0\text{ mL}$ into 10 sterile cryo-vials containing porous ceramic beads. This represents **Tier 1 Master Stock (Passage P1)**.
6. Snap-freeze immediately in an ultra-low freezer at $-80.0 \pm 3.0^\circ\text{C}$.
7. Monthly, extract a single frozen bead under sterile conditions using a sterile needle without thawing the parent vial.
8. Roll bead over a Tryptic Soy Agar (TSA) slant; incubate at $35.0^\circ\text{C}$ for $24\text{ hours}$. Store slant at $2.0\text{–}8.0^\circ\text{C}$ as **Monthly Working Stock (Passage P2)**.
9. Weekly, streak from P2 slant onto non-selective agar plates to yield isolated colonies representing **Weekly Working Stock (Passage P3)**.
10. Working colonies used for daily AST or stain QC constitute **Passage P4**.
11. **Passage Ceiling:** Plates reaching Passage P4 must be autoclaved and discarded after 7 days. Subculturing to Passage 5 (P5) is strictly prohibited.

### SOP-06: Daily Microbiological Staining Solution QC Smear Protocol
1. Retrieve designated QC slide blanks pre-cleaned in $95\%$ ethanol.
2. In circle "A", emulsify one isolated colony of *S. aureus* ATCC 25923 in a microdrop of sterile saline. In circle "B", emulsify *E. coli* ATCC 25922.
3. Air-dry smears completely; heat-fix by passing slide smoothly through a Bunsen flame three times ($< 60.0^\circ\text{C}$ on slide underside).
4. **Gram Stain Protocol:**
   - Flood with Crystal Violet for $60\text{ seconds}$; rinse gently with slow-running tap water.
   - Flood with Gram's Iodine for $60\text{ seconds}$; rinse with water.
   - Decolorize with Acetone-Alcohol ($1:1$) dropwise for $5\text{–}10\text{ seconds}$ until runoff clears; rinse immediately with water.
   - Counterstain with Safranin ($0.5\%$) for $45\text{ seconds}$; rinse and blot dry with bibulous paper.
5. Examine under oil immersion lens ($1000\times$):
   - Circle A: Deep violet Gram-positive cocci in clusters.
   - Circle B: Crisp pinkish-red Gram-negative bacilli.
6. If decolorization is improper (e.g. *S. aureus* pink or *E. coli* blue), discard staining set, recalibrate reagents, and repeat.
7. Log "PASS" in LIMS and daily QC bench register before staining or reading any patient slides.

## Section 5: Non-Conformance Protocols & Out-of-Control Corrective Actions (CAPA)

```
CAPA Non-Conformance Management Algorithm (ISO 15189:2022 Clause 8.7)
========================================================================================
[Trigger: QC Deviation Detected]
           │
           ├─► 1. Immediate Operational Containment (Lock-Out / Tag-Out / Quarantine)
           ├─► 2. Impact Assessment & Retrospective Patient Test Auditing
           ├─► 3. Root Cause Analysis (Ishikawa 5-Why Methodology)
           ├─► 4. Formulate Corrective Action (Eliminate Current Failure)
           ├─► 5. Formulate Preventive Action (Engineering Control / Procedure Revision)
           └─► 6. Verification of Effectiveness at 30, 60 & 90 Days -> Closeout
========================================================================================
```

### Protocol 5.1: Autoclave Biological Spore Test Failure Action
1. **Immediate Quarantine:** Affix red "OUT OF ORDER — STERILIZATION FAILURE" tag to autoclave control panel. Cut electrical supply to lock-out/tag-out (LOTO).
2. **Batch Recall:** Immediately recall, quarantine, and condemn all media batches sterilized in that autoclave during the preceding 7 days.
3. **Retrospective Impact Audit:** Review all patient culture plates inoculated using suspect media lots. Any specimen showing unexpected contaminants or anomalous growth must be repeated from secondary patient specimens or confirmed using alternative validated media.
4. **Engineering Investigation:** Inspect autoclave door gaskets, temperature sensor calibration, steam trap function, and air exhaust valve. Ensure chamber reached $121.0^\circ\text{C}$ and full air removal occurred.
5. **Re-Validation Protocol:** After technical servicing, execute three (3) consecutive successful sterilization cycles containing *Geobacillus stearothermophilus* ATCC 7953 ampoules and Class 5 chemical integrators. All three spore tests must demonstrate zero growth (purple color) at $48\text{ hours}$ before the autoclave may be returned to clinical service.

### Protocol 5.2: Culture Media Sterility Check Failure Action
1. If any colony appears on the $3.0\%\text{–}5.0\%$ uninoculated sterility control plates during the $48\text{ hour}$ incubation at $35.0^\circ\text{C}$, the entire media batch is declared non-conforming.
2. Mark batch in LIMS as "REJECTED — BIO-BURDEN CONTAMINATION". Transfer all plates to Biohazard Autoclave #2 for destruction.
3. Perform root cause investigation:
   - Check biological spore and Class 5 integrator logs for that autoclave run.
   - Inspect plate pouring environment: review BSC laminar flow velocity and settle plate counts during the pouring session.
   - Investigate additive sterility (e.g. defibrinated sheep blood lot sterility).
4. Re-train technicians on aseptic plate pouring technique. Verify zero colony count on replacement batch prior to clinical release.

### Protocol 5.3: Mueller-Hinton Agar Depth / pH Deviation Action
1. If MHA agar depth falls outside $4.0 \pm 0.5\text{ mm}$ ($< 3.5\text{ mm}$ or $> 4.5\text{ mm}$), or final $\text{pH}$ falls outside $7.2\text{–}7.4$:
2. The entire batch is condemned for antimicrobial susceptibility testing. Plates may not be repurposed for AST under any circumstance.
3. Recalibrate automated peristaltic pouring pumps or serological pipettes used for volume dispensing.
4. Recalibrate benchtop pH meter with fresh NIST-traceable standard buffer solutions ($\text{pH } 4.01, 7.00, 10.01$).
5. Prepare a fresh test batch, confirm depth with calibrated digital vernier caliper across 4 points per plate, and verify with standard ATCC disk diffusion QC.

### Protocol 5.4: Biosafety Cabinet Aerodynamic / Pressure Breach Action
1. If the Magnehelic gauge drops below $0.3\text{ in WG}$ or exceeds $1.0\text{ in WG}$, or if the airflow alarm sounds:
2. Cease all pathogen manipulation immediately. Cap and seal all culture containers, tubes, and specimen cups.
3. Decontaminate work deck surface with $70\%$ isopropanol.
4. Close sliding sash completely and turn off blower. Post "BSC MALFUNCTION — DO NOT USE" placard.
5. Notify certified biosafety cabinet engineering vendor.
6. Vendor must inspect pre-filters, motor blower capacitors, and HEPA filter integrity.
7. Following servicing, the cabinet must undergo full `NSF/ANSI 49` re-certification (face velocity profiling and PAO aerosol challenge) before resumption of diagnostic work.

### Protocol 5.5: Microbiological Staining Solution QC Failure Action
1. If daily Gram stain QC smear demonstrates over-decolorized *S. aureus* (pink) or under-decolorized *E. coli* (purple), or if AFB control fails:
2. Immediately suspend all clinical slide reading for that shift.
3. Discard working staining reagents in use. Inspect stock reagent containers for expiration date, precipitation, or evaporation.
4. Prepare fresh working solutions from certified concentrated stock or open new manufacturer reagent bottles.
5. Re-stain a fresh set of ATCC positive and negative QC control slides.
6. Upon achieving verified QC results, retrieve and re-stain all clinical patient slides processed during the suspect shift. Never release reports based on unvalidated stains.

## Section 6: Six Real-World Worked Operational & QC Clinical Scenarios

### Case 1: Autoclave Biological Spore Failure Traced to Air Vent Solenoid Failure
- **Incident Description:** On Monday routine validation, *Geobacillus stearothermophilus* ATCC 7953 ampoule placed in Autoclave #1 turned turbid yellow after $24\text{ hours}$ incubation at $56.0^\circ\text{C}$ (indicating spore survival and sterilization failure).
- **Immediate Containment:** Autoclave #1 was locked out and tagged out. Two batches of Blood Agar and CLED Agar prepared during that cycle were quarantined and prevented from reaching clinical benches.
- **Root Cause Analysis (5-Why):** Autoclave chart showed temperature reached $121.0^\circ\text{C}$ and pressure was $15.0\text{ psi}$, but spore kill failed. Physical inspection revealed the automatic air exhaust bellows valve was jammed in a partially closed position. Residual trapped air formed an insulating cold pocket at the bottom of the chamber, preventing true saturated steam contact. True chamber core temperature was only $112.0^\circ\text{C}$.
- **CAPA Implementation:** Replaced air exhaust bellows valve. Re-calibrated temperature probes. Performed three consecutive cycles with *G. stearothermophilus* ampoules; all three remained purple at $48\text{ hours}$.
- **LIMS Quality Entry:** `SDL-NC-2026-AUT01-08: Autoclave #1 air vent solenoid failure resolved. 3x spore re-challenge passed. Equipment released to clinical service.`

### Case 2: Mueller-Hinton Agar Batch with Insufficient Agar Depth ($2.8\text{ mm}$)
- **Incident Description:** During routine disk diffusion QC, *E. coli* ATCC 25922 and *S. aureus* ATCC 25923 exhibited oversized inhibition zones across all antibiotic disks (zones $3\text{–}5\text{ mm}$ above CLSI upper limits).
- **Metrological Investigation:** Agar depth measured across 10 plates using a calibrated vernier caliper averaged $2.8\text{ mm}$ (CLSI specification: $4.0 \pm 0.5\text{ mm}$).
- **Root Cause Analysis:** Media preparation technician used a $15\text{ mL}$ dispensing scoop instead of calibrated $20\text{ mL}$ serological dispenser for $90\text{ mm}$ petri dishes, resulting in an excessively thin agar layer. Antibiotic molecules diffused laterally with minimal vertical resistance, generating falsely enlarged zones of inhibition.
- **CAPA Implementation:** Entire batch of 80 MHA plates was condemned and autoclaved. Automated peristaltic dispenser was recalibrated to deliver exactly $20.0\text{ mL}$ per plate. Implemented mandatory vernier depth logging on 3 random plates for every freshly poured batch prior to release.
- **LIMS Quality Entry:** `SDL-NC-2026-MHA-14: Batch MHA-2610-04 scrapped due to depth 2.8 mm. Dispenser recalibrated. Replacement batch verified at 4.1 mm.`

### Case 3: *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* ATCC 27853 Gentamicin Zone Anomaly Traced to Cation Excess
- **Incident Description:** Weekly disk diffusion QC on new MHA lot yielded a Gentamicin ($10\ \mu\text{g}$) zone of $14\text{ mm}$ against *P. aeruginosa* ATCC 27853 (CLSI M100 acceptable range: $17\text{–}23\text{ mm}$). However, *E. coli* ATCC 25922 Gentamicin zone was within limits ($21\text{ mm}$).
- **Metrological Investigation:** *P. aeruginosa* is uniquely susceptible to variations in divalent cation concentrations ($\text{Ca}^{2+}, \text{Mg}^{2+}$). Excess cations compete with aminoglycosides for outer membrane binding sites, artificially reducing zone sizes.
- **Root Cause Analysis:** Water purification system cartridge was exhausted. Water conductivity was $4.8\ \mu\text{S/cm}$ (specification: $< 1.0\ \mu\text{S/cm}$), leaching excess calcium and magnesium into the media formulation water.
- **CAPA Implementation:** De-ionizing and polishing resin cartridges on the Type II water purification system were replaced immediately. Purified water conductivity restored to $0.4\ \mu\text{S/cm}$. Fresh MHA batch prepared with verified water yielded *P. aeruginosa* ATCC 27853 Gentamicin zone of $20\text{ mm}$ (conforming).
- **LIMS Quality Entry:** `SDL-NC-2026-AST-22: MHA cation imbalance secondary to water cartridge exhaustion. Resins replaced. Water conductivity 0.4 uS/cm. QC verified.`

### Case 4: Class II Type A2 Biosafety Cabinet Magnehelic Pressure Spike
- **Incident Description:** Morning inspection revealed BSC Magnehelic differential pressure gauge spiked to $1.2\text{ in WG}$ (normal operating window: $0.4\text{–}0.7\text{ in WG}$), accompanied by an intermittent audible airflow alarm.
- **Immediate Containment:** Biosafety cabinet was immediately taken offline. Sputum AFB smear preparation and TB CBNAAT processing were transferred to the backup certified containment suite.
- **Root Cause Analysis:** Technical examination revealed the cabinet pre-filter had accumulated heavy atmospheric dust following regional storm conditions. The blocked pre-filter starved the motor blower, creating a high negative pressure differential before the HEPA intake.
- **CAPA Implementation:** Pre-filter was removed, washed, dried, and reinstalled (with spare pre-filters placed on inventory). Airflow velocities were re-measured using a calibrated hot-wire anemometer: inflow face velocity was $102\text{ fpm}$, downflow velocity was $58\text{ fpm}$, and Magnehelic pressure normalized to $0.55\text{ in WG}$. Smoke airflow test confirmed laminar containment.
- **LIMS Quality Entry:** `SDL-NC-2026-BSC-03: BSC-01 pre-filter clogged. Cleaned and reset. Magnehelic 0.55 in WG. Face velocity 102 fpm. Containment verified.`

### Case 5: Loss of TEM-1 Plasmid in *E. coli* ATCC 35218 Due to Over-Passaging ($P > 5$)
- **Incident Description:** *E. coli* ATCC 35218 (used to QC $\beta$-lactamase inhibitor combinations) produced an Amoxicillin-Clavulanic Acid ($20/10\ \mu\text{g}$) zone of $25\text{ mm}$ (CLSI M100 acceptable range: $17\text{–}22\text{ mm}$). The strain was behaving as if it were wild-type fully susceptible *E. coli*.
- **Bench Investigation:** Audit of the working culture subculture log revealed the technician had maintained the working strain by serial weekly plate-to-plate transfers for 14 consecutive weeks (Passage 16).
- **Root Cause Analysis:** Extended serial passaging without selective pressure resulted in spontaneous segregation and loss of the non-conjugative plasmid encoding the TEM-1 $\beta$-lactamase enzyme.
- **CAPA Implementation:** The over-passaged working culture was destroyed. A fresh master cryo-bead (Passage P1) was retrieved from the $-80.0^\circ\text{C}$ ultra-low freezer, revived onto TSA (Passage P2), and subcultured to working plates (Passage P3). Re-testing yielded an Amoxicillin-Clavulanic Acid zone of $19\text{ mm}$ (conforming). A strict LIMS hard-lock was instituted restricting working stock longevity to 4 passages from original stock.
- **LIMS Quality Entry:** `SDL-NC-2026-ATCC-09: Over-passaged ATCC 35218 lost TEM-1 plasmid. Restored from -80C master bank (P1). Retest zone 19 mm. SOP updated.`

### Case 6: Gram Stain Over-Decolorization Incident Caught by Daily Internal QC Smear
- **Incident Description:** Morning daily Gram stain QC slide showed *S. aureus* ATCC 25923 stained reddish-pink instead of deep violet, while *E. coli* ATCC 25922 stained bright red.
- **Immediate Containment:** Clinical slides awaiting Gram stain reading (including CSF, blood culture broth, and sputum smears) were held.
- **Root Cause Analysis:** The decolorizer bottle had been accidentally refilled with pure acetone instead of the standard acetone-alcohol ($1:1$) mixture. The pure acetone decolorized the thick peptidoglycan layer of *S. aureus* in under $3\text{ seconds}$, stripping crystal violet and allowing counterstaining with safranin.
- **CAPA Implementation:** The improperly formulated decolorizer was drained and replaced with properly prepared $1:1$ Acetone-Alcohol ($95\%$ ethanol). A fresh QC slide was stained: *S. aureus* stained deep violet and *E. coli* stained bright pink. Clinical patient slides were then processed with validated reagents.
- **LIMS Quality Entry:** `SDL-NC-2026-STN-05: Acetone decolorizer concentration error detected via daily IQC. Reagent replaced. QC passed. Patient slide processing authorized.`
