## 🧪 Section 2: Clinical Biochemistry, Coenzyme A (CoA), Acyl Carrier Protein (ACP) & Metabolic Flux

### 2.1 The Molecular Architecture of Vitamin B5 & The 5-Step Biosynthesis of Coenzyme A
Vitamin $\text{B}_5$ (D-pantothenic acid; $(R)\text{-3-(2,4-dihydroxy-3,3-dimethylbutanamido)propanoic acid}$) is an essential water-soluble micronutrient consisting of **pantoic acid** linked to **$\beta$-alanine** via an amide bond. In mammalian physiology, pantothenic acid is biologically inactive in its free state; its entire physiological function derives from its incorporation into two master metabolic prosthetic groups: **Coenzyme A (`CoA-SH`)** and the **4'-phosphopantetheine** prosthetic group of **Acyl Carrier Protein (`ACP`)**.

Intracellular de novo synthesis of Coenzyme A from circulating free pantothenate occurs via five sequential enzymatic steps:
1. **Phosphorylation (Rate-Limiting Gateway):** Pantothenate is phosphorylated at its terminal hydroxyl group by cytosolic **Pantothenate Kinase (PANK1, PANK2, PANK3, PANK4)** utilizing ATP to form **4'-Phosphopantothenate**. PANK2 is uniquely targeted to the mitochondrial intermembrane space and is allosterically feedback-inhibited by acetyl-CoA, malonyl-CoA, and free CoA.
2. **Cysteine Condensation:** 4'-Phosphopantothenate condenses with L-cysteine in an ATP/CTP-dependent reaction catalyzed by **Phosphopantothenoylcysteine Synthetase (`PPCS`)** to produce **4'-Phosphopantothenoyl-L-cysteine**.
3. **Decarboxylation:** The cysteine moiety is decarboxylated by **Phosphopantothenoylcysteine Decarboxylase (`PPCDC`)**, yielding **4'-Phosphopantetheine** and releasing $\text{CO}_2$.
4. **Adenylylation:** 4'-Phosphopantetheine is coupled to the adenosine monophosphate (AMP) moiety of ATP by **Phosphopantetheine Adenylyltransferase (`PPAT`)**, forming **Dephospho-CoA**.
5. **Final Phosphorylation:** The 3'-hydroxyl group of the ribose ring is phosphorylated by **Dephospho-CoA Kinase (`DPCK`)** using ATP to produce fully mature **Coenzyme A (`CoA-SH`)**. In human cells, PPAT and DPCK exist as a bifunctional chimeric cytosolic enzyme complex designated **CoA Synthase (`COASY`)**.

### 2.2 The High-Energy Thioester Bond & Pivotal Metabolic Intersections
Coenzyme A terminates in a chemically reactive **thiol ($-\text{SH}$)** group that forms high-energy **thioester bonds** ($\Delta G^{\circ\prime} \approx -31.5\text{ kJ/mol}$) with carboxylic acids. This thermodynamic activation powers $>100$ individual enzymatic reactions across human intermediary metabolism:
- **Tricarboxylic Acid (TCA / Krebs) Cycle:** Condensation of **Acetyl-CoA** with oxaloacetate catalyzed by *citrate synthase* initiates the cycle, generating cellular NADH, $\text{FADH}_2$, and ATP via oxidative phosphorylation.
- **Fatty Acid $\beta$-Oxidation & Carnitine Shuttle:** Long-chain fatty acids are activated into acyl-CoA thioesters by acyl-CoA synthetases at the outer mitochondrial membrane before carnitine palmitoyltransferase-1 (`CPT1`) transesterification.
- **Steroidogenesis & Adrenal Corticoid Biosynthesis:** Condensation of two acetyl-CoA molecules to acetoacetyl-CoA initiates the **mevalonate pathway** via HMG-CoA reductase, producing cholesterol—the obligatory substrate for adrenocortical steroidogenesis (cortisol, aldosterone, dehydroepiandrosterone) and gonadal steroids.
- **Neurotransmitter Biosynthesis:** Choline acetyltransferase (`ChAT`) transfers the acetyl group from mitochondrial-derived acetyl-CoA to choline, generating **Acetylcholine** in motor endplates, autonomic ganglia, and central cholinergic networks.
- **Heme & Porphyrin Biosynthesis:** Mitochondrial **Succinyl-CoA** condenses with glycine via $\delta$-aminolevulinic acid synthase 1 and 2 (`ALAS1` / `ALAS2`) to synthesize $\delta$-ALA—the rate-limiting opening step of erythroid and hepatic heme formation.
- **Epigenetic Regulation & Post-Translational Modification:** Acetyl-CoA acts as the universal acetyl donor for **Histone Acetyltransferases (`HATs`)**, balancing sirtuin-mediated deacetylation to regulate chromatin accessibility, transcription, and cellular longevity.

### 2.3 Acyl Carrier Protein (ACP) & De Novo Lipogenesis
The 4'-phosphopantetheine prosthetic arm of Coenzyme A is post-translationally transferred by **Phosphopantetheinyl Transferase (`PPTase`)** onto a conserved serine residue of apo-Acyl Carrier Protein (`apo-ACP`), converting it into functionally active **holo-ACP**:
- In the cytoplasm, ACP exists as an integrated functional domain within the mega-enzyme **Fatty Acid Synthase (`FAS` / *FASN*)**. The $20\text{ \AA}$ flexible 4'-phosphopantetheine "swinging arm" shuttles growing acyl chain intermediates between the seven catalytic centers of FAS during iterative malonyl-CoA condensation cycles.
- In mitochondria, an independent monomeric ACP isoform (**mtACP** / *NDUFAB1*) serves as an essential subunit of respiratory Complex I and supplies octanoyl moieties for the de novo biosynthesis of **Lipoic Acid**—an obligatory co-factor for pyruvate dehydrogenase and $\alpha$-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase.

### 2.4 Cellular Coenzyme A Degradation, Salvage & Turnover
Intracellular Coenzyme A is continuously turned over to maintain metabolic flexibility and avoid toxic accumulation of unesterified acyl species:
- Peroxisomal and mitochondrial nudix hydrolases (**NUDT7**, **NUDT8**, and **NUDT19**) cleave the pyrophosphate bond of CoA or acyl-CoAs, generating 4'-phosphopantetheine and 3',5'-ADP.
- Ectonucleotide pyrophosphatases (**ENPP1**, **ENPP3**) and cellular phosphatases dephosphorylate 4'-phosphopantetheine to yield **pantetheine**.
- Extracellular and vascular pantetheinase (**Vanin-1 / VNN1** and **Vanin-2 / VNN2**) hydrolyzes pantetheine into **free D-pantothenic acid** and the potent antioxidant/reducing thiol **cysteamine**.
- Free pantothenic acid is subsequently taken up by enterocytes, hepatocytes, renal tubular cells, and neurons via the sodium-dependent multivitamin transporter (**SMVT / *SLC5A6***), completing the systemic conservation cycle.

## 🩺 Section 3: Clinical Pathophysiology, Gopalan's Burning Feet Syndrome, Adrenal Exhaustion, PKAN & Differential Diagnosis Matrix

### 3.1 Gopalan's "Burning Feet Syndrome" (Nutritional Melalgia / Grierson-Gopalan Syndrome)
First systematically documented in 1946 by the eminent Indian nutritional scientist **Dr. Coluthur Gopalan** among severely impoverished populations in South India, and concurrently recognized in Allied prisoners of war in Far East Japanese internment camps (1942–1945), **Burning Feet Syndrome** represents the quintessential human manifestation of severe Vitamin $\text{B}_5$ depletion:
- **Clinical Semiology:** Patients present with bilateral, symmetrical, agonizing burning pain, hyperesthesia, electric-shock paresthesias, and throbbing distress localized primarily to the soles, heels, and metatarsal pads of both feet.
- **Circadian & Thermal Dynamics:** Symptoms exhibit marked nocturnal exacerbation, causing profound insomnia, agitated restlessness, and involuntary limb pacing. Patients obtain transient, characteristic relief by immersing their feet in buckets of cold water or walking barefoot on cold stone floors.
- **Autonomic & Vasomotor Instability:** Intense local plantar hyperhidrosis (excessive sweating), elevated skin temperature, cutaneous erythema, and vasomotor instability accompany the dysesthesias.
- **Diagnostic Clinical Hallmark:** Unlike dry beriberi (Vitamin $\text{B}_1$ deficiency) or subacute combined degeneration (Vitamin $\text{B}_{12}$ deficiency), **deep tendon reflexes (patellar and Achilles reflexes) are strictly preserved**, and objective motor weakness is conspicuously absent in isolated early Gopalan's syndrome.
- **Therapeutic Specificity:** The syndrome demonstrates zero clinical response to Thiamine ($\text{B}_1$), Riboflavin ($\text{B}_2$), or Niacin ($\text{B}_3$) alone, but exhibits dramatic, complete resolution within $7\text{–}21\text{ days}$ upon the administration of **Calcium D-Pantothenate ($200\text{–}500\text{ mg/day}$)**.

### 3.2 Adrenocortical Exhaustion & Steroidogenic Failure
Classic human metabolic chamber experiments (Hodges, Bean, and colleagues, 1950s) utilizing the synthetic metabolic antagonist **$\omega$-methylpantothenic acid** established the crucial link between Vitamin $\text{B}_5$ and adrenal gland viability:
- **Pathophysiology:** Because the synthesis of cholesterol from acetyl-CoA is an absolute prerequisite for adrenocortical hormone output, pantothenic acid deficiency precipitates rapid intracellular cholesterol depletion in the *zona fasciculata* and *zona reticularis*.
- **Clinical Features:** Patients develop profound asthenia, unremitting orthostatic hypotension, recurrent non-insulin-mediated hypoglycemia, impaired diuresis, emotional lability, somnolence, and marked vulnerability to physiological stress. Laboratory testing demonstrates an absent or severely blunted eosinopenic and cortisol response to exogenous ACTH stimulation.
- **Pathological Anatomy:** Animal models of chronic pantothenate deprivation demonstrate striking bilateral cortical lipid depletion, structural disorganization, and focal hemorrhagic necrosis of the adrenal cortex.

### 3.3 Pantothenate Kinase-Associated Neurodegeneration (`PKAN` / *PANK2* Mutation)
PKAN (formerly Hallervorden-Spatz syndrome; `OMIM #234200`) is an autosomal recessive inborn error of metabolism caused by loss-of-function mutations in the **pantothenate kinase 2 (*PANK2*)** gene located on chromosome 20p13:
- **Pathogenic Mechanism:** PANK2 encodes the mitochondrial isoform of pantothenate kinase. Deficiency paralyzes the phosphorylation of pantothenate to 4'-phosphopantothenate inside mitochondria, precipitating severe localized CoA depletion, defective mitochondrial $\beta$-oxidation, and secondary accumulation of cytotoxic L-cysteine.
- **Iron Deposition & Neurotoxicity:** Unmetabolized cysteine chelates free ferrous iron ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$) within the high-turnover basal ganglia, driving catastrophic Fenton-reaction hydroxyl radical generation ($\text{OH}^\bullet$), lipid peroxidation, and neuroaxonal dystrophy.
- **Neuroimaging Pathognomonic:** High-field T2-weighted brain MRI reveals the pathognomonic **"Eye-of-the-Tiger" sign** in the globus pallidus: a central zone of high signal intensity (hyperintensity reflecting cellular necrosis, gliosis, and edema) surrounded by a dense ring of hypointensity (reflecting profound iron accumulation).
- **Critical Laboratory Interpretation Pearl:** Circulating serum/plasma free pantothenic acid levels in PKAN patients are **completely normal**, because the primary genetic lesion is an intracellular metabolic blockade in enzymatic utilization, not systemic dietary deficiency or defective intestinal enterocyte absorption!

## 🩸 Section 4: Pre-Analytical Phlebotomy, Specimen Matrices, Stability & Quantitative HIL Matrix

### 4.1 Specimen Matrix Selection: Serum/Plasma vs Whole Blood vs 24-Hour Urine
Accurate clinical assessment of Vitamin $\text{B}_5$ requires strict alignment between the clinical indication and the biological specimen matrix:
1. **Fasting Serum or K2-EDTA Plasma (Free Pantothenic Acid):**
   - Measures unbound, free D-pantothenic acid circulating in extracellular fluid ($35\text{–}150\text{ ng/mL}$).
   - Reflects acute dietary intake, short-term nutritional adequacy, parenteral nutrition monitoring, and oral supplement compliance.
2. **K2-EDTA Whole Blood (Total Pantothenate):**
   - Over $85\%\text{–}90\%$ of total body blood pantothenate resides inside erythrocytes, covalently bonded within intracellular Coenzyme A, 4'-phosphopantetheine, and acyl-CoA esters.
   - Measures total pantothenate following complete dual-enzymatic cleavage ($345\text{–}1,050\text{ ng/mL}$).
   - Serves as the authoritative gold standard for long-term tissue store quantification and cellular CoA status.
3. **24-Hour Aliquoted Urine (Renal Excretion):**
   - Free pantothenate filtered at the glomerulus is excreted without significant tubular reabsorption when plasma levels exceed renal threshold ($1.0\text{–}7.0\text{ mg/24h}$).
   - Daily excretion $< 1.0\text{ mg/24h}$ ($< 4.56\text{ }\mu\text{mol/24h}$) confirms severe total-body pantothenate depletion.

### 4.2 Phlebotomy Protocol & Patient Preparation Rules (`CLSI GP41`)
- **Fasting Requirement:** Mandatory **12-hour overnight fast**. High-fat meals alter matrix viscosity, and postprandial dietary absorption induces transient $2\text{–}5\times$ plasma spikes.
- **Supplementation Hold:** Complete cessation of oral B-complex vitamins, multivitamins, nutritional energy beverages, hair/skin/nail formulas, and dexpanthenol-containing nutraceuticals for **$24\text{–}48\text{ hours}$** prior to phlebotomy.
- **CLSI GP41 Order of Draw:**
  - Fasting Serum: Red-top plain glass/plastic tube without gel separator, or gold-top SST tube (Draw Order #4).
  - Plasma / Whole Blood: Lavender-top K2-EDTA tube (Draw Order #6). Invert gently $8\text{–}10\text{ times}$ immediately upon venipuncture to prevent micro-clot formation.
- **Light Protection:** Pantothenic acid demonstrates moderate stability under ambient light, but samples must be collected in amber vials or immediately wrapped in aluminum foil to protect concurrent light-labile B-vitamers ($\text{B}_2$, $\text{B}_6$) routinely co-ordered.
- **Thermal Processing:** Centrifuge serum/plasma within **$60\text{ minutes}$** of venipuncture at **$1,500\text{–}2,000 \times g$ for $10\text{ minutes}$ at $4^\circ\text{C}$**. Immediately separate supernatant into sterile polypropylene transfer tubes and freeze.

### 5.3 Step-by-Step Sample Preparation Protocols
#### Protocol A: Fasting Serum / EDTA Plasma (Free Pantothenic Acid)
1. Pipette $100\text{ }\mu\text{L}$ of patient serum/plasma, calibrator, or quality control material into a $1.5\text{ mL}$ Eppendorf tube.
2. Add $20\text{ }\mu\text{L}$ of working Internal Standard solution ($500\text{ ng/mL}$ $^{13}\text{C}_3,\text{^{15}N}\text{-Pantothenate}$).
3. Add $300\text{ }\mu\text{L}$ of ice-cold LC-MS grade Acetonitrile containing $0.1\%$ formic acid to induce protein precipitation.
4. Vortex vigorously at $2,500\text{ rpm}$ for $60\text{ seconds}$. Incubate at $-20^\circ\text{C}$ for $15\text{ minutes}$ to maximize globulin precipitation.
5. Centrifuge at $14,000 \times g$ for $10\text{ minutes}$ at $4^\circ\text{C}$.
6. Transfer $200\text{ }\mu\text{L}$ of clear supernatant into an autosampler vial with micro-insert. Inject $5.0\text{ }\mu\text{L}$ into the LC-MS/MS system.

#### Protocol B: EDTA Whole Blood (Total Pantothenate Dual-Enzyme Digestion Protocol)
1. Pipette $100\text{ }\mu\text{L}$ of thoroughly mixed K2-EDTA whole blood into a $1.5\text{ mL}$ tube; add $400\text{ }\mu\text{L}$ of deionized water to induce complete osmotic hemolysis; freeze-thaw once.
2. Add $100\text{ }\mu\text{L}$ of Tris-HCl buffer ($0.2\text{ mol/L}$, $\text{pH } 8.0$) containing $5.0\text{ mmol/L } \text{MgCl}_2$.
3. Add $20\text{ }\mu\text{L}$ of **Alkaline Phosphatase** ($10\text{ U/mL}$; dephosphorylates CoA and 4'-phosphopantetheine) and $20\text{ }\mu\text{L}$ of recombinant **Pantetheinase / Vanin-1** ($5\text{ U/mL}$; hydrolyzes pantetheine into D-pantothenate and cysteamine).
4. Incubate the digestion mixture in a shaking water bath at **$37^\circ\text{C}$ for $3\text{ hours}$**.
5. Terminate the enzymatic reaction by heating at $80^\circ\text{C}$ for $5\text{ minutes}$ in a dry heating block.
6. Cool to room temperature; spike with $20\text{ }\mu\text{L}$ Internal Standard; add $600\text{ }\mu\text{L}$ acidified acetonitrile; centrifuge at $14,000 \times g$ for $10\text{ minutes}$. Analyze supernatant by LC-MS/MS.

### 6.1 EFLM Biological Variation Specifications & Reference Change Values

- **Between-Subject Biological Variation ($CV_G$):** $24.0\%$

  *Clinical Impact:* An Index of Individuality of $0.44$ ($< 0.60$) indicates **very high individuality**. Population-based reference intervals are relatively insensitive for detecting subtle personal shifts; **serial tracking of individual patient delta values using RCV is clinically superior** for monitoring dietary intervention and repletion.
- **Analytical Performance Specifications:**
  - **Desirable Imprecision ($CV_{A,des}$):** $CV_{A,des} \le 0.5 \times CV_I = 0.5 \times 10.5\% = \mathbf{\le 5.25\%}$

  $$\text{RCV}_{95\%} = 2^{1/2} \times 1.96 \times \sqrt{CV_A^2 + CV_I^2} = 1.414 \times 1.96 \times \sqrt{3.6^2 + 10.5^2} = \mathbf{\pm 30.8\%}$$

### 6.3 Six Sigma Metric & Westgard Multi-Rule Statistical Architecture
Using the regulatory Total Allowable Error ($\text{TE}_a = 20.0\%$), observed intermediate imprecision ($CV_A = 3.6\%$), and verified analytical bias ($\text{Bias} = 2.1\%$):
$$\text{Sigma Metric } (\sigma) = \frac{\text{TE}_a - \lvert\text{Bias}\rvert}{CV_A} = \frac{20.0\% - 2.1\%}{3.6\%} = \frac{17.9\%}{3.6\%} = \mathbf{4.97\sigma} \approx \mathbf{5.0\sigma}$$
A performance rating of **$5.0\sigma$ (World Class / High Performance Quality)** mandates the following optimized Westgard statistical control schema:
- **Daily Quality Control Schema:** Run 2 levels of QC material once every $12\text{ hours}$ or at the beginning of each patient analytical batch.
- **Active Multi-Rules:**
  - **$1_{3s}$ Rule:** Reject run when a single QC value exceeds the mean $\pm 3\text{ SD}$ (sensitive to random error).
  - **$2_{2s}$ Rule:** Reject run when two consecutive QC values exceed the same mean $+ 2\text{ SD}$ or $- 2\text{ SD}$ (sensitive to systematic bias).
  - **$R_{4s}$ Rule:** Reject run when one QC value exceeds $+2\text{ SD}$ and another exceeds $-2\text{ SD}$ in the same analytical run (random error).
  - **$4_{1s}$ Rule:** Warning alert when four consecutive QC results exceed $+1\text{ SD}$ or $-1\text{ SD}$ on the same side of the mean (systematic drift).

## 💊 Section 8: Clinical Pharmacology, Drug-Nutrient Interferences, SMVT Competition & Toxicity

### 8.1 The Sodium-Dependent Multivitamin Transporter (`SMVT` / *SLC5A6*) Competition
Intestinal uptake of dietary pantothenic acid across the brush border membrane of enterocytes and its transport across the blood-brain barrier occur primarily via the **Sodium-Dependent Multivitamin Transporter (`SMVT`)**, encoded by the *SLC5A6* gene:
- **Competitive Shared Ligands:** SMVT transports three chemically distinct, biologically essential micronutrients: **D-Pantothenic Acid (Vitamin $\text{B}_5$)**, **Biotin (Vitamin $\text{B}_7$)**, and **$\alpha$-Lipoic Acid**.
- **Cross-Substrate Competitive Inhibition:**
  - Megadose Biotin therapy (e.g., $100\text{–}300\text{ mg/day}$ administered experimentally in progressive multiple sclerosis or hair/skin supplements) competitively inhibits SMVT, suppressing intestinal pantothenic acid absorption by up to $60\%\text{–}75\%$.
  - Conversely, massive pantothenic acid supplementation ($> 5\text{ g/day}$) competitively impairs Biotin uptake, inducing secondary subclinical biotin deficiency.
  - *Clinical Directive:* When administering megadoses of Biotin or $\alpha$-Lipoic Acid, monitor serum pantothenic acid levels and space supplement administration by at least $4\text{ hours}$.

### 8.2 Pharmacological Agents, Provitamins & Analytical/In-Vivo Interferences
1. **Dexpanthenol (D-Pantothenyl Alcohol / Provitamin $\text{B}_5$):**
   - Widely utilized in surgical gastrointestinal atony (to stimulate postoperative peristalsis via acetylcholine synthesis), wound-healing topicals, and intravenous multivitamin cocktails.
   - Rapidly oxidized in vivo by alcohol dehydrogenase to free D-pantothenic acid, generating massive, transient serum concentrations ($> 500\text{ ng/mL}$) within $1\text{–}4\text{ hours}$.
2. **Pantethine (Bis-Pantethine / Coenzyme A Precursor):**
   - Utilized as an over-the-counter lipid-lowering nutraceutical ($600\text{–}1,200\text{ mg/day}$) to reduce total cholesterol, LDL-C, and triglycerides by inhibiting HMG-CoA reductase and acetyl-CoA carboxylase.
   - In vivo, pantethine is cleaved into two molecules of pantetheine, which are hydrolyzed by pantetheinase into free pantothenic acid and cysteamine. Produces sustained $5\text{–}10\times$ elevations in measured serum pantothenate.
3. **Valproic Acid & The "CoA Trapping" Phenomenon:**
   - Valproic acid (2-propylpentanoic acid) is converted inside mitochondrial matrix into valproyl-CoA.
   - Valproyl-CoA acts as a dead-end metabolite that sequesters cellular Coenzyme A pools ("CoA trapping"), competitively inhibiting mitochondrial pyruvate dehydrogenase, $\alpha$-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, and carnitine palmitoyltransferase-1. This precipitates secondary carnitine deficiency and valproate-induced hyperammonemic encephalopathy.
4. **Hemodialysis & Peritoneal Dialysis Losses:**
   - Free pantothenic acid has a low molecular weight ($219.23\text{ Da}$) and negligible circulating plasma protein binding ($< 10\%$).
   - Consequently, it undergoes rapid diffusive clearance during hemodialysis, with losses of $3.0\text{–}5.0\text{ mg}$ per $4\text{-hour}$ dialysis session. Chronic dialysis patients are at high risk of unmeasured subclinical B5 deficiency predisposing to uremic/nutritional burning feet symptoms.
5. **$\omega$-Methylpantothenic Acid:**
   - Synthetic biochemical antagonist used in experimental human studies. Competitively inhibits pantothenate kinase, inducing rapid adrenocortical failure, motor incoordination, and burning feet sensations within 4 weeks.
6. **Antibiotic Interference in Microbiological Bioassays:**
   - Aminoglycosides, penicillins, cephalosporins, and tetracyclines present in patient serum severely inhibit the replication of *Lactobacillus plantarum* (ATCC 8014), causing **falsely catastrophic deficiency results** in turbidimetric bioassays! ID-LC-MS/MS is completely immune to this pharmacological interference.

### 8.3 Toxicity Profile & Tolerable Upper Intake Level (`UL`)
- **Systemic Toxicity:** Pantothenic acid has an exceptionally low order of toxicity. It is not known to be teratogenic, mutagenic, or carcinogenic. Because it is a water-soluble vitamin with efficient saturable active transport and rapid glomerular filtration, excess amounts are rapidly cleared into urine.
- **Tolerable Upper Limit:** The US Institute of Medicine (`IOM`) and the European Food Safety Authority (`EFSA`) have **not established a Tolerable Upper Intake Level (`UL`)** due to the absence of demonstrated adverse toxicological endpoints.
- **Megadose Adverse Effects:** Extremely high oral intakes ($10\text{ to }20\text{ g/day}$) overwhelm intestinal absorptive capacity, causing mild osmotic water retention in the colon, presenting with transient **osmotic diarrhea, abdominal cramping, and nausea**, which resolve promptly upon dose cessation.
