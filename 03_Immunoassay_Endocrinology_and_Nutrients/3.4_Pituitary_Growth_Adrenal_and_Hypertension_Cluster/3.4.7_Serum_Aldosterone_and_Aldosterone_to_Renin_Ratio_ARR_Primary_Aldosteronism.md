## 🧪 Section 2: Adrenal Steroid Biochemistry, Molecular Physiology, RAAS Regulation & Clinical Epidemiology

### 2.1 Steroid Structure, Biosynthesis (CYP11B2) & Mineralocorticoid Receptor Activation
Aldosterone ($360.4\text{ Da}$) is the primary endogenous mineralocorticoid hormone in humans. It is synthesized exclusively in the **zona glomerulosa** (the outermost layer, comprising ~15% of the adrenal cortex) because the key terminal enzyme, **Aldosterone Synthase (`CYP11B2`)**, is transcriptionally restricted to this anatomical zone. In contrast, the adjacent zona fasciculata expresses $11\beta\text{-hydroxylase}$ (`CYP11B1`) which yields cortisol and corticosterone, but entirely lacks 18-oxidase activity.

```
                  CHOLESTEROL (Steroidogenic Acute Regulatory Protein, StAR)
                                      │ CYP11A1
                                      ▼
                                PREGNENOLONE
                                      │ 3β-HSD2
                                      ▼
                               PROGESTERONE
                                      │ CYP21A2 (21-Hydroxylase)
                                      ▼
                         DEOXYCORTICOSTERONE (DOC)
                                      │ CYP11B2 (Aldosterone Synthase, 11β-Hydroxylase activity)
                                      ▼
                                CORTICOSTERONE
                                      │ CYP11B2 (18-Hydroxylase activity)
                                      ▼
                           18-HYDROXYCORTICOSTERONE
                                      │ CYP11B2 (18-Oxidase activity)
                                      ▼
                                 ALDOSTERONE
```

1. **Physiological Secretagogues of Aldosterone Synthase (`CYP11B2`):**
   - **Extracellular Potassium ($\text{K}^+$):** The most direct, potent physiological secretagogue. Even minor elevations in serum $\text{K}^+$ ($0.1\text{–}0.2\text{ mmol/L}$) depolarize the zona glomerulosa plasma membrane, opening voltage-gated T- and L-type calcium channels. Intracellular $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ influx activates Calmodulin-dependent protein kinase (CaMK), upregulating StAR and CYP11B2 transcription. *Conversely, hypokalemia hyperpolarizes the membrane, turning off $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ influx and profoundly suppressing aldosterone synthesis.*
   - **Angiotensin II (`Ang II`):** Binds G-protein coupled $\text{AT}_1$ receptors on glomerulosa cells, activating Phospholipase C (PLC) $\to$ Inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate ($\text{IP}_3$) and Diacylglycerol (DAG) $\to$ rapid release of intracellular $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and Protein Kinase C (PKC) activation $\to$ CYP11B2 transcription.
   - **Adrenocorticotropic Hormone (`ACTH`):** Binds melanocortin-2 receptors ($\text{MC}_2\text{R}$), stimulating cAMP-PKA signaling. ACTH exerts acute, transient stimulation but lacks long-term trophic control over zona glomerulosa mass.
2. **Renal Cellular Mechanics & Mineralocorticoid Receptor (`MR`) Signaling:**
   - Free circulating aldosterone binds cytoplasmic **Mineralocorticoid Receptors (`MR` / `NR3C2`)** within principal cells of the renal cortical collecting duct (`CCD`) and late distal convoluted tubule (`DCT`).
   - Ligand-receptor homodimers translocate to the nucleus, binding Mineralocorticoid Response Elements (`MRE`).
   - **Early Genomic Action ($\le 2\text{–}4\text{ hours}$):** Induces Serum and Glucocorticoid-Regulated Kinase 1 (`SGK1`). Phosphorylation by SGK1 inactivates the E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase `Nedd4-2`, preventing endocytic retrieval and lysosomal degradation of the **Epithelial Sodium Channel (`ENaC`)**. Unhindered ENaC channels accumulate on the apical membrane, surging luminal $\text{Na}^+$ reabsorption.
   - **Late Genomic Action ($> 6\text{–}24\text{ hours}$):** Transcribes de novo $\text{ENaC}$ $\alpha$-subunits, basolateral $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+\text{-ATPase}$ pumps, and mitochondrial ATP synthase enzymes.
   - **Electrochemical Kaliuresis & Acid Secretion:** As electrogenic $\text{Na}^+$ reabsorption creates a negative luminal transepithelial potential ($-10\text{ to }-30\text{ mV}$), $\text{K}^+$ ions exit principal cells into urine via apical Renal Outer Medullary Potassium (`ROMK`) channels and Maxi-K channels. Simultaneously, intercalated alpha cells secrete $\text{H}^+$ ions via vacuolar $\text{H}^+\text{-ATPase}$, causing metabolic alkalosis.

### 2.2 Primary Aldosteronism (Conn's Syndrome) Epidemiology & The Normokalemic Paradigm
- **Epidemiological Scope:** Primary Aldosteronism (`PA`) is not a rare curiosity: it accounts for **$5\text{–}10\%$ of all unselected hypertensive patients** in primary care and **$15\text{–}25\%$ of patients with resistant hypertension** (uncontrolled on $\ge 3$ antihypertensive classes including a diuretic) or Stage 3 hypertension ($\text{BP} > 180/110\text{ mmHg}$).
- **Cardiovascular & Renal Morbidity:** Excess aldosterone exerts direct pro-fibrotic, pro-inflammatory, and pro-arrhythmic effects on myocardium and arterial vasculature independent of blood pressure. Patients with PA carry a **$3.7\times$ higher risk of stroke**, **$4.2\times$ higher risk of non-fatal myocardial infarction**, **$12\times$ higher risk of atrial fibrillation**, and significantly elevated risk of microalbuminuria and chronic kidney disease compared to age-, sex-, and BP-matched essential hypertensives.
- **The Normokalemic Paradigm:** While classic textbooks describe Conn's syndrome as the triad of hypertension, hypokalemia, and metabolic alkalosis, **more than $50\%$ (and up to $70\text{–}80\%$) of confirmed PA patients are completely normokalemic**! Spontaneous hypokalemia is present in only a minority of cases, predominantly those with large unilateral Aldosterone-Producing Adenomas (`APA`). Relying on hypokalemia to trigger screening results in missed diagnoses in the vast majority of curable patients.

### 2.3 The Aldosterone-to-Renin Ratio (ARR) Screening Mandate & Mathematical Ratio Mechanics
The **Aldosterone-to-Renin Ratio (`ARR`)** is the internationally recognized, universally recommended screening test for Primary Aldosteronism. Autonomous aldosterone secretion suppresses renal renin release via baroreceptor and sodium sensing; thus, PA produces an elevated aldosterone paired with suppressed renin, driving ARR upwards.

$$\text{ARR} = \frac{\text{Plasma Aldosterone Concentration (PAC)}}{\text{Direct Renin Concentration (DRC) or Plasma Renin Activity (PRA)}}$$

- **The Absolute Aldosterone Floor Gate (Mandatory Anti-Artifact Rule):**
  A mathematical ratio cannot be evaluated in isolation without verifying its numerator. In patients with low-renin essential hypertension, volume overload, elderly vascular stiffness, or $\beta$-blocker therapy, Direct Renin may be suppressed to near-zero levels (e.g., $\text{DRC} = 0.5\ \mu\text{IU/mL}$). If Plasma Aldosterone is low-normal (e.g., $3.0\text{ ng/dL}$), the computed ARR is $3.0 / 0.5 = 6.0$, which exceeds the screening cutoff ($\ge 3.7$) despite the absence of hyperaldosteronism.
  *Mandatory Rule:* **An ARR screen is diagnostic of Primary Aldosteronism ONLY when the ratio exceeds cutoff AND the concurrent Plasma Aldosterone is $\ge 10.0\text{ ng/dL}$ ($\ge 277\text{ pmol/L}$; high probability if $\ge 15.0\text{ ng/dL}$).**

### 2.4 Molecular Genetics of Aldosterone-Producing Adenomas & Familial Hyperaldosteronism
1. **Somatic Mutations in Aldosterone-Producing Adenomas (APAs):**
   - **`KCNJ5` Mutations (GIRK4 Inward-Rectifying $\text{K}^+$ Channel):** Present in ~40% of Caucasian and up to ~70% of East/South Asian APAs. Mutations in the selectivity filter (e.g., p.Gly151Arg, p.Leu168Arg) cause loss of potassium selectivity, leading to massive $\text{Na}^+$ influx, persistent membrane depolarization, opening of voltage-gated $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channels, constitutive CYP11B2 transcription, and cortical cell proliferation. Typically associated with younger age, female sex, severe hypokalemia, and high aldosterone.
   - **`ATP1A1` & `ATP2B3` Mutations:** Somatic mutations in the $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+\text{-ATPase}$ $\alpha_1$ subunit or plasma membrane $\text{Ca}^{2+}\text{-ATPase}$ pump type 3 distort cation export, driving intracellular $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ overload.
   - **`CACNA1D` Mutations:** Gain-of-function mutations in the $\text{Ca}_v1.3$ L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channel shift activation to hyperpolarized potentials, causing continuous $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ influx.
2. **Familial Hyperaldosteronism (FH) Syndromic Subtypes:**
   - **FH-I (Glucocorticoid-Remediable Aldosteronism, GRA):** Autosomal dominant chimeric gene mutation caused by unequal crossing-over between the regulatory promoter of $CYP11B1$ (ACTH-responsive) and the coding region of $CYP11B2$ (aldosterone synthase). Results in ectopic aldosterone synthesis in the zona fasciculata under tonic ACTH control. Suppressed completely by low-dose dexamethasone ($0.5\text{ mg}$ q6h). Diagnosed via multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification (`MLPA`) or long-range PCR.
   - **FH-II:** Autosomal dominant gain-of-function mutations in `CLCN2` (voltage-gated chloride channel ClC-2) causing glomerulosa cell depolarization; clinically indistinguishable from sporadic APA or IHA.
   - **FH-III:** Autosomal dominant germline mutations in `KCNJ5`; causes severe, refractory early-childhood hypertension, massive bilateral adrenocortical hyperplasia, and extreme hypokalemia, frequently necessitating bilateral total adrenalectomy.
   - **FH-IV:** Germline gain-of-function mutations in `CACNA1H` ($\text{Ca}_v3.2$ T-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channel).

## 🧪 Section 3: Pre-Analytical Protocol, Mandatory Patient Preparation & Antihypertensive Drug Washout Matrix

### 3.1 Pre-Analytical Standardization: Sodium, Hypokalemia Correction, Posture & Circadian Timing
1. **Unrestricted Dietary Sodium Mandate:** The patient must ingest a normal-to-high sodium diet without salt restriction for $\ge 3$ consecutive days prior to testing (verified by $24\text{h}$ urinary sodium $> 100\text{–}150\text{ mEq/day}$). Dietary sodium restriction stimulates the renin-angiotensin system, elevating renin and producing a false-negative ARR.
2. **Mandatory Hypokalemia Correction Rule:**
   - Extracellular potassium directly modulates zona glomerulosa `CYP11B2` activity. In patients with Conn's syndrome and severe hypokalemia ($\text{K}^+ < 3.0\text{–}3.5\text{ mmol/L}$), aldosterone production is paradoxically suppressed into the 'normal' range ($6\text{–}12\text{ ng/dL}$), causing a catastrophic false-negative ARR screen!
   - *Mandatory Action:* **Always measure Serum Potassium prior to phlebotomy. If $\text{K}^+ < 4.0\text{ mmol/L}$, replenish with oral $\text{KCl}$ ($40\text{–}80\text{ mEq/day}$) to achieve a verified Serum $\text{K}^+ \ge 4.0\text{ mmol/L}$ before drawing Aldosterone and Renin.**
3. **Posture & Circadian Standardization:**
   - Plasma Aldosterone follows a sharp diurnal rhythm driven by ACTH and posture. Phlebotomy must occur strictly between **`08:00 – 10:00 AM`**.
   - The patient must be out of bed and upright (walking or standing) for at least $2\text{ hours}$, followed by **$15\text{–}30\text{ minutes}$ of seated rest** immediately before venipuncture. Supine phlebotomy decreases aldosterone by $50\%$, altering diagnostic cutoffs.

### 3.3 Specimen Collection, CLSI GP41 Order-of-Draw, Tube Chemistry & Multi-Temperature Stability
- **Specimen Type & Anticoagulant:** `K2-EDTA` plasma is strongly preferred so that **Aldosterone (`3.4.7`) and Direct Renin Concentration (`3.4.6`)** are drawn in the same collection and run from the **exact same tube**! Plain serum (red-top or SST) is acceptable for aldosterone alone, but `K2-EDTA` is mandatory for renin.
- **Tube Order of Draw (`CLSI GP41`):** Blood cultures $\to$ Citrate (blue) $\to$ Serum (red/gold) $\to$ Heparin (green) $\to$ **`K2-EDTA` (lavender, for Aldosterone & Renin)** $\to$ Fluoride/Oxalate (gray).
- **Centrifugation Protocol:** Centrifuge within $1\text{ hour}$ of draw at **$1,500\text{–}2,000 \times g$ for $10\text{ minutes}$ at room temperature ($18\text{–}25^\circ\text{C}$)**.
  *CRITICAL WARNING FOR PAIRED RENIN SPECIMENS:* **NEVER chill unseparated whole-blood or store EDTA tubes on wet ice ($0\text{–}4^\circ\text{C}$)!** Chilling activates pro-renin into renin (*cryoactivation*), resulting in falsely elevated renin and a false-negative ARR. Keep at room temperature until plasma is separated!
- **Multi-Temperature Specimen Stability:**
  - Separated `K2-EDTA` Plasma / Serum at $20\text{–}25^\circ\text{C}$ (Room Temp): **$8\text{ hours}$**
  - Separated `K2-EDTA` Plasma / Serum at $2\text{–}8^\circ\text{C}$ (Refrigerated): **$48\text{ hours}$** (for Aldosterone alone; if paired with Direct Renin, freeze immediately if not tested within 4h)
  - Frozen at $-20^\circ\text{C}$: **$60\text{ days}$**
  - Ultra-frozen at $-70^\circ\text{C}$: **$12\text{ months}$** (avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles; maximum 2 cycles)

### 3.4 24-Hour Urine Aldosterone Collection & Acid-Preservation Protocol
1. **Preservation Requirements:** Collect complete $24\text{h}$ urine into an opaque container pre-charged with **$10\text{ g}$ of Boric Acid** or **$15\text{ mL}$ of $6\text{ M HCl}$** (maintains $\text{pH}$ between $2.0\text{ to }4.0$, inhibiting bacterial degradation and spontaneous steroid breakdown).
2. **Collection Timing:** Discard the first morning void on Day 1 (e.g., `07:00 AM`). Pool all subsequent voids day and night into the container. Include the final void at `07:00 AM` on Day 2.
3. **Specimen Gating:** Record total $24\text{h}$ volume. Aliquot $20\text{ mL}$ and store at $2\text{–}8^\circ\text{C}$. Measure concurrent **$24\text{h}$ Urinary Creatinine** ($15\text{–}25\text{ mg/kg}$ in males; $10\text{–}20\text{ mg/kg}$ in females) to confirm collection completeness and **$24\text{h}$ Urinary Sodium** ($> 200\text{ mEq/day}$ confirms adequate salt intake).

## ⚙️ Section 4: Analytical Architecture — Competitive Flash-CLIA (ABEI) on Snibe Maglumi X3 & ID-LC-MS/MS Reference Platform

### 4.1 Assay Chemistry, Molecular Kinetics & Optical Physics
The primary routine platform is the **Snibe MAGLUMI X3 Automated Flash-Chemiluminescence Immunoassay (`ABEI` CLIA)**.
1. **Reaction Mechanism:** Competitive immunoassay format.
   - Endogenous Aldosterone in patient plasma competes with an $\text{ABEI}$ (Amino-Butyl-Ethyl-Isoluminol) labeled Aldosterone derivative for a limited number of binding sites on purified monoclonal anti-aldosterone antibodies coated onto magnetic microbeads.
2. **Steroid Dissociation:** Aldosterone in plasma is weakly bound to serum albumin (~$60\%$), Corticosteroid-Binding Globulin (`CBG` / transcortin, ~$20\%$), and exists free (~$20\%$). An onboard acid displacement reagent rapidly uncouples aldosterone from transport proteins, ensuring measurement of total circulating hormone.
3. **Washing & Separation Phase:** A high-gradient neodymium magnetic field captures the immunocomplexed microbeads against the cuvette wall. A wash buffer strips away unbound tracer, endogenous proteins, and sample matrix.
4. **Chemiluminescent Flash Trigger:**
   - Onboard injector adds Starter Reagent 1 ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ in acid solution) followed by Starter Reagent 2 ($\text{NaOH}$ alkaline catalyst).
   - In the presence of catalyst and alkaline hydrogen peroxide, $\text{ABEI}$ undergoes oxidative cleavage, emitting light pulses at a peak wavelength of **$\lambda = 425\text{ nm}$**.
   - Photons are quantified by an ultra-sensitive Photomultiplier Tube (`PMT`) over a $3\text{-second}$ integration window, recorded as Relative Light Units (`RLU`).
   - **Inverse Kinetic Calibration:** The emitted light intensity ($\text{RLU}$) is **inversely proportional** to the concentration of aldosterone in the patient sample.

$$\text{ABEI-Conjugate} + \text{H}_2\text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Microperoxidase / NaOH, } \text{pH } > 11} \text{Aminophthalate Excited Intermediate}^* \to h\nu\ (\lambda = 425\text{ nm})$$

### 4.2 Universal Step-by-Step Analytical Bench SOP (Hardware-Agnostic)
- **Step 1 (Daily Pre-Run Maintenance & Inspection):**
  - Verify analyzer fluidics: liquid waste container emptied, wash buffer reservoir primed, trigger Starter 1 and 2 volumes sufficient.
  - Check operational environment: ambient temperature $18\text{–}25^\circ\text{C}$, relative humidity $30\text{–}70\%$, onboard refrigerated reagent carousel at $2\text{–}8^\circ\text{C}$.
  - Perform daily background check and PMT dark current verification; verify washer probe aspiration pressure.
- **Step 2 (Calibration Verification & Traceability):**
  - Load manufacturer-provided, 2-point master-curve calibrators (traceable to pure crystalline CRM Aldosterone via `ID-LC-MS/MS`).
  - Re-calibration mandatory upon: (a) new reagent kit lot, (b) major optical or fluidic service, (c) IQC rule violation (`1:3s` or systematic shift), or (d) expiration of 28-day onboard calibration stability.
- **Step 3 (Internal Quality Control Gating):**

### 6.2 Measurement Uncertainty Budget (ISO/TS 20914) & Westgard QC Multi-Rules
- **Measurement Uncertainty Budget (`ISO/TS 20914`):**
  - Calibrator uncertainty ($u_{cal}$): $2.1\%$
  - Long-term within-lab imprecision ($u_{Rw}$): $4.1\%$
  - Method trueness bias uncertainty ($u_{bias}$): $1.8\%$
  - Combined standard uncertainty: $u_c = \sqrt{2.1^2 + 4.1^2 + 1.8^2} = 5.0\%$.
  - Expanded uncertainty: $U = 2 \times u_c = \mathbf{\pm 10.0\%}$ ($k=2, 95.45\%$ confidence interval).
- **Westgard Statistical Quality Control Multi-Rule Scheme:**
  - $1_{3s}$ Violation (Random error): Reject run if any single control exceeds $\pm 3\text{ SD}$.
  - $2_{2s}$ Violation (Systematic error): Reject run if two consecutive controls exceed $+2\text{ SD}$ or $-2\text{ SD}$.
  - $R_{4s}$ Violation (Random error): Reject run if difference between controls in same run exceeds $4\text{ SD}$.
  - $4_{1s}$ Warning (Systematic trend): Flag if four consecutive controls exceed $+1\text{ SD}$ or $-1\text{ SD}$.
  - $10_x$ Warning (Systematic shift): Flag if ten consecutive controls fall on one side of the mean; recalibrate.

### 7.3 Primary Aldosteronism Subtype Differentiation: Thin-Slice CT & Adrenal Venous Sampling (AVS) Protocol
Once Primary Aldosteronism is biochemically confirmed, differentiation between **unilateral disease** (Aldosterone-Producing Adenoma [`APA`] or unilateral adrenal hyperplasia, ~35% of cases; curable by laparoscopic adrenalectomy) and **bilateral disease** (Bilateral Idiopathic Adrenal Hyperplasia [`IHA`], ~60% of cases; treated medically with mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists) is essential.

```
       CONFIRMED PRIMARY ALDOSTERONISM (Positive Screen + Confirmatory Suppression)
                                      │
                                      ▼
             THIN-SLICE MULTI-DETECTOR CONTRAST ADRENAL CT / MRI
                                      │
             ┌────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┐
             ▼                                                 ▼
Young Patient (<35y) with Markedly              All Other Patients Desiring Surgical Cure
Elevated Aldo (>30 ng/dL), Spontaneous          (Normal CT, Bilateral Nodules, Unilateral

             │                                                 │
             ▼                                                 ▼
Direct to Laparoscopic Adrenalectomy            ADRENAL VENOUS SAMPLING (AVS)
(Avoids invasive AVS if unequivocal)            (Gold Standard Functional Lateralization)
```

1. **Adrenal Venous Sampling (`AVS`) Procedure & Mechanics:**
   - Performed by an experienced interventional radiologist via percutaneous femoral vein catheterization.
   - Bilateral simultaneous or sequential blood sampling from the Right Adrenal Vein (`RAV`), Left Adrenal Vein (`LAV`), and Infra-renal Inferior Vena Cava (`IVC` / peripheral).
   - Often performed under continuous intravenous infusion of synthetic ACTH ($50\ \mu\text{g/hour}$ Cosyntropin) to maximize aldosterone secretion and overcome ACTH-dependent pulsatility.
2. **AVS Mathematical Evaluation Indices:**
   - **Selectivity Index (SI — Cannulation Verification):**
     $$\text{SI} = \frac{\text{Cortisol}_{\text{adrenal vein}}}{\text{Cortisol}_{\text{peripheral IVC}}}$$
     *Criteria:* $\text{SI} > 5.0:1$ under continuous ACTH stimulation (or $> 2.0:1$ without ACTH) proves successful cannulation of the adrenal vein (especially difficult on the short right adrenal vein which drains directly into the IVC).
   - **Lateralization Index (LI — Unilateral vs. Bilateral Discrimination):**
     $$\text{LI} = \frac{(\text{Aldosterone} / \text{Cortisol})_{\text{dominant side}}}{(\text{Aldosterone} / \text{Cortisol})_{\text{non-dominant side}}}$$
     *Criteria:*
     - **$\text{LI} > 4.0:1$ (with ACTH) or $> 2.0:1$ (without ACTH):** Unambiguous **unilateral aldosterone hypersecretion**. Indicates patient is an ideal candidate for **unilateral laparoscopic adrenalectomy** (cures hypertension in 50–60%, improves control in remainder, cures hypokalemia in 100%).
     - **$\text{LI} < 3.0:1$ (with ACTH) or $< 2.0:1$ (without ACTH):** Indicates **bilateral adrenal disease** (`IHA`). Treatment is medical management with **Spironolactone ($25\text{–}100\text{ mg/day}$)** or **Eplerenone ($50\text{–}100\text{ mg/day}$)**; adrenalectomy is contraindicated!
   - **Contralateral Suppression Index (CSI):**
     $$\text{CSI} = \frac{(\text{Aldosterone} / \text{Cortisol})_{\text{non-dominant side}}}{(\text{Aldosterone} / \text{Cortisol})_{\text{peripheral IVC}}}$$
     *Criteria:* $\text{CSI} < 1.0$ indicates that the non-dominant adrenal gland is suppressed by the dominant tumor's excess mineralocorticoid feedback, providing strong corroboration of unilateral disease.

### 8.2 Endocrine Society Algorithmic Reflex Cascade & Clinical Decision Tree
1. **Screening Gate:** Calculate ARR from upright morning samples with Serum $\text{K}^+ \ge 4.0\text{ mmol/L}$ and interfering drugs held.
   - If $\text{ARR} < 2.4\text{ (ng/dL)/(}\mu\text{IU/mL)}$: Primary Aldosteronism excluded; pursue essential hypertension management or workup for secondary non-mineralocorticoid causes.
   - If $\text{ARR} \ge 3.7$ BUT absolute $\text{Plasma Aldosterone} < 10.0\text{ ng/dL}$: Mathematical ratio artifact secondary to suppressed renin. PA excluded; evaluate for low-renin essential hypertension or volume expansion.
   - If $\text{ARR} \ge 3.7$ AND absolute $\text{Plasma Aldosterone} \ge 10.0\text{ ng/dL}$: Screen positive.
2. **Confirmatory Triage:**
   - **Bypass Rule:** If patient exhibits **Spontaneous Hypokalemia + Undetectable Renin ($\text{DRC} < 2.5\ \mu\text{IU/mL}$) + Plasma Aldosterone $> 20.0\text{ ng/dL}$ ($> 555\text{ pmol/L}$)** $\to$ Primary Aldosteronism is 100% confirmed! Confirmatory suppression testing is completely unnecessary. Proceed directly to Adrenal CT.
   - **Standard Path:** Perform 4-Hour Seated Saline Infusion Test (`SSIT`). Post-infusion aldo $> 10.0\text{ ng/dL}$ confirms PA.
3. **Subtype & Surgical Triage:**
   - Perform thin-slice contrast adrenal CT.
   - In patients seeking surgical cure (and age $> 35$ years), perform Adrenal Venous Sampling (`AVS`).
   - If AVS lateralizes ($\text{LI} > 4.0:1$) $\to$ Laparoscopic adrenalectomy. If non-lateralized ($\text{LI} < 3.0:1$) $\to$ Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist pharmacotherapy.
