## 🧪 Section 2: Adrenal Steroidogenesis, HPA Axis, Circadian Dynamics & Four Core Diagnostic Matrices / Protocols

### 2.1 Adrenal Steroidogenesis, Transport Kinetics & Molecular Mechanisms
Cortisol (Hydrocortisone, $362.5\text{ Da}$) is the primary endogenous glucocorticoid synthesized in the zona fasciculata of the human adrenal cortex ($75\%$ of adrenocortical volume). Biosynthesis proceeds under pulsatile ACTH stimulation from cholesterol through the steroidogenic cascade:
$$\text{Cholesterol} \xrightarrow{\text{CYP11A1}} \text{Pregnenolone} \xrightarrow{\text{CYP17A1}} \text{17}\alpha\text{-OH-Pregnenolone} \xrightarrow{\text{3}\beta\text{-HSD2}} \text{17}\alpha\text{-OH-Progesterone} \xrightarrow{\text{CYP21A2}} \text{11-Deoxycortisol} \xrightarrow{\text{CYP11B1}} \text{Cortisol}$$

1. **Intravascular Transport & The Free Hormone Hypothesis:**
   - **Corticosteroid-Binding Globulin (`CBG` / Transcortin, $52\text{ kDa}$):** Binds $80\text{–}90\%$ of circulating cortisol with high affinity ($K_d \approx 10^{-8}\text{ M}$). Synthesized in the liver; hepatic production is markedly upregulated by estrogens (pregnancy, oral contraceptives) and downregulated by severe liver disease, nephrotic syndrome, and critical illness.
   - **Serum Albumin:** Binds $10\text{–}15\%$ of cortisol with low affinity ($K_d \approx 10^{-4}\text{ M}$) but high capacity.
   - **Free (Bioactive) Cortisol:** Only $2\text{–}5\%$ circulates in an unbound, biologically active state. In accordance with the Free Hormone Hypothesis, only unbound cortisol diffuses across capillary barriers, enters target cells, binds intracellular Glucocorticoid Receptors (`GR` / `NR3C1`), homodimerizes, and translocates into the nucleus to transactivate Glucocorticoid Response Elements (`GRE`) or transrepress proinflammatory transcription factors (`NF-κB`, `AP-1`).
2. **The Tissue Enzymatic Shuttle: $11\beta\text{-HSD2}$ vs. $11\beta\text{-HSD1}$:**
   - **$11\beta\text{-Hydroxysteroid Dehydrogenase Type 2 (11}\beta\text{-HSD2):}$** Located in mineralocorticoid-target tissues (aldosterone-sensitive distal nephron, salivary gland ductal epithelium, colon, and placenta). It rapidly converts active cortisol to inactive cortisone (oxidizing the $11\beta\text{-hydroxyl}$ group to an $11\text{-keto}$ group). Because circulating cortisol concentrations exceed aldosterone by 100- to 1000-fold and both bind the Mineralocorticoid Receptor (`MR`) with equal affinity, $11\beta\text{-HSD2}$ protects the MR from inappropriate cortisol flooding. Genetic deficiency or pharmacological inhibition (by glycyrrhizin in licorice or carbenoxolone) allows cortisol to flood the MR, inducing the syndrome of **Apparent Mineralocorticoid Excess (`AME`)** with severe hypertension, hypokalemia, and suppressed renin/aldosterone.
   - **$11\beta\text{-Hydroxysteroid Dehydrogenase Type 1 (11}\beta\text{-HSD1):}$** Located in liver, visceral adipose tissue, skeletal muscle, and CNS. Acting predominantly as an $\text{NADPH}$-dependent reductase in vivo, it regenerates active cortisol from circulating inactive cortisone, amplifying local glucocorticoid signaling in visceral obesity and metabolic syndrome.

### 2.2 Neuroendocrine HPA Axis & Circadian Dynamics
The hypothalamic suprachiasmatic nucleus (`SCN`) dictates a profound diurnal rhythm in cortisol secretion:
- **Nocturnal Nadir (`23:00 – 01:00 AM`):** Cortisol secretion drops to its lowest baseline ($< 1.8\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ in serum; $< 0.145\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ in saliva). Disruption or loss of this nocturnal nadir is the earliest, most sensitive biochemical hallmark of **Cushing's Syndrome**.
- **Cortisol Awakening Response (`CAR`) & Morning Peak (`07:00 – 09:00 AM`):** Secretion surges sharply prior to awakening, reaching $6.2\text{–}19.4\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ in serum, mobilizing glucose and vascular tone for daytime activity.
- **Afternoon Trough (`16:00 – 18:00 PM`):** Cortisol levels fall by approximately $50\%$ compared to morning levels ($2.3\text{–}11.9\ \mu\text{g/dL}$).

## ⚙️ Section 3: Analytical Architecture — Competitive Flash-CLIA (ABEI) on Snibe Maglumi X3 & ID-LC-MS/MS Reference Platform

### 3.1 Reaction Chemistry & Optical Physics of Competitive Flash-CLIA
The Snibe MAGLUMI X3 Cortisol assay is a competitive solid-phase flash-chemiluminescence immunoassay (`ABEI` CLIA):
1. **Sample Dispensing & Steroid Displacement:** $20\ \mu\text{L}$ of patient serum (or processed saliva extract) is dispensed into the reaction cuvette. An acid displacement reagent dissociates endogenous cortisol completely from endogenous binding proteins (`CBG` and albumin).
2. **Competitive Immunological Incubation:** Paramagnetic microbeads coated with high-affinity monoclonal anti-cortisol antibodies and $N\text{-(4-aminobutyl)-}N\text{-ethylisoluminol}$ (`ABEI`)-labeled cortisol derivative tracer are added. Endogenous patient cortisol competes stoichiometrically with ABEI-cortisol tracer for a limited number of monoclonal antibody binding sites:
   $$\text{Bead-Ab} + \text{Cortisol}_{\text{Patient}} + \text{Cortisol-ABEI} \rightleftharpoons [\text{Bead-Ab-Cortisol}] + [\text{Bead-Ab-Cortisol-ABEI}]$$
3. **Magnetic Separation & Washing:** Reaction cuvettes enter the magnetic separation rack where rare-earth neodymium magnets immobilize the microbead complexes. Unbound tracer, serum proteins, and displacement reagents are aspirated to liquid waste, followed by 3 automated cycles of high-pressure wash buffer.
4. **Flash Light Trigger & Chemiluminescent Emission:** Cuvettes enter the optical read chamber. Starter Reagent 1 ($\text{NaOH}$, establishing $\text{pH} > 12.0$) and Starter Reagent 2 ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$) are injected. The alkaline peroxide environment catalytically cleaves the ABEI label, producing an excited aminophthalate radical that emits a flash of chemiluminescent light at **$\lambda = 425\text{ nm}$** within $3.0\text{ seconds}$.
5. **Inverse Photometric Relationship:** Emitted Relative Light Units (`RLU`) measured by the Photomultiplier Tube (`PMT`) are **inversely proportional** to the cortisol concentration in the patient specimen. The concentration is interpolated from a master 10-point calibration curve.

### 3.3 Analytical Measurement Range (`AMR`), Limits of Detection & Linearity
- **Limit of Blank (`LoB`, `CLSI EP17-A2`):** $0.08\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ ($2.2\text{ nmol/L}$).
- **Limit of Detection (`LoD`, `CLSI EP17-A2`):** $0.15\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ ($4.1\text{ nmol/L}$).
- **Limit of Quantitation (`LoQ`, functional sensitivity at $CV \le 10\%$, `CLSI EP17-A2`):** **$0.20\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ ($5.5\text{ nmol/L}$)**. Robustly quantifies post-dexamethasone suppression below the $1.8\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ cutoff and late-night salivary cortisol down to $0.145\ \mu\text{g/dL}$.
- **Analytical Measurement Range (`AMR`):** $0.20\text{ to }60.0\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ ($5.5\text{ to }1,655\text{ nmol/L}$).

## 📊 Section 4: EFLM Biological Variation, Six Sigma, MU Budget (ISO/TS 20914) & Quality Control

### 4.1 EFLM European Biological Variation Study (`EuBIVAS`) Quality Specifications
- **Within-Subject Biological Variation ($CV_I$):** **$15.2\%$** (standardized morning resting draw; unstandardized diurnal variation across $24\text{h}$ exceeds $50\%$).
- **Between-Subject Biological Variation ($CV_G$):** **$28.1\%$**.
- **Index of Individuality ($\text{II} = CV_I / CV_G$):**
  $$\text{II} = \frac{15.2\%}{28.1\%} = \mathbf{0.54}$$
  *Clinical & Metrological Significance:* An index of individuality $< 0.6$ indicates **high individuality**. Single population-based reference intervals are relatively insensitive for detecting subtle individual abnormalities. Serial delta monitoring via Reference Change Value (`RCV`) and dynamic suppression/stimulation tests are mandatory for definitive clinical diagnosis.

### 4.2 Analytical Performance Goals & Six Sigma Quality Metrics
| Quality Metric | Specification Formula | EFLM Target | Sawariya Lab Maglumi X3 Achieved Metric | Six Sigma Status |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |

### 4.3 Measurement Uncertainty Budget (`ISO/TS 20914:2019`)
Measurement uncertainty is modeled across the operational range:
- Within-laboratory intermediate precision uncertainty ($u_{Rw}$): $3.20\%$
- Calibrator assigned value uncertainty ($u_{cal}$, NIST SRM 921): $1.80\%$
- Bias and recovery uncertainty ($u_{bias}$): $1.20\%$
- **Combined Standard Uncertainty ($u_c$):**
  $$u_c = \sqrt{u_{Rw}^2 + u_{cal}^2 + u_{bias}^2} = \sqrt{3.20^2 + 1.80^2 + 1.20^2} = \sqrt{10.24 + 3.24 + 1.44} = \sqrt{14.92} = 3.86\%$$
- **Expanded Measurement Uncertainty ($U$ at $95.45\%$ confidence, $k=2$):**
  $$U = 2 \times u_c = 2 \times 3.86\% = \mathbf{7.72\% \approx 7.7\%}$$
  *Clinical Example:* An $08:00\text{ AM}$ Serum Cortisol reported as $10.0\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ carries an expanded uncertainty interval of $10.0 \pm 0.77\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ ($9.23\text{ to }10.77\ \mu\text{g/dL}$).

### 4.4 Asymmetric Log-Normal Reference Change Value (`RCV`)
Because within-subject biological variation ($CV_I = 15.2\%$) exceeds $12\%$, the asymmetric Log-Normal RCV is applied:
$$S = \sqrt{\ln(1 + CV_I^2) + \ln(1 + CV_A^2)} = \sqrt{\ln(1 + 0.152^2) + \ln(1 + 0.032^2)} = \sqrt{0.02283 + 0.00102} = 0.1544$$
- **Positive Reference Change Value ($\text{RCV}_{pos}$, $95\%$ confidence, $Z = +1.96$):**
  $$\text{RCV}_{pos} = (\exp(+1.96 \times 0.1544 \times \sqrt{2}) - 1) \times 100\% = \mathbf{+53.4\%}$$
- **Negative Reference Change Value ($\text{RCV}_{neg}$, $95\%$ confidence, $Z = -1.96$):**
  $$\text{RCV}_{neg} = (\exp(-1.96 \times 0.1544 \times \sqrt{2}) - 1) \times 100\% = \mathbf{-34.8\%}$$
- *LIMS Delta-Check Rule:* In time-matched morning draws, an increase exceeding $+53.4\%$ or a decrease exceeding $-34.8\%$ represents a true biological change ($p < 0.05$) rather than analytical or physiological fluctuation.

### 4.5 Westgard Six Sigma Quality Control Rules
- Run 3 levels of commutable IQC (Low: $2.5\ \mu\text{g/dL}$, Mid: $14.0\ \mu\text{g/dL}$, High: $35.0\ \mu\text{g/dL}$) every 8-hour shift and post-maintenance.
- Westgard Multirule Cascade:
  * Reject run: $1:3s$ (random error) or $2:2s$ (systematic error across two consecutive runs or both levels).
  * Warning rule: $1:2s$ (initiates review of reagent blank, lamp intensity, and temperature).
  * Systemic shift: $4:1s$ or $10:\bar{x}$ (requires recalibration and optical probe cleaning).

## 🔬 Section 7: In-Vivo Pharmacokinetics, Differential Diagnosis Matrix & 6 Worked Clinical Cases + Reflex Cascades

### 7.1 Pharmacokinetics & Clearance Dynamics
- **Plasma Elimination Half-Life ($t_{1/2}$):** **$60\text{ to }90\text{ minutes}$** for free and total circulating cortisol.
- **Biological / Anti-Inflammatory Tissue Half-Life:** **$8\text{ to }12\text{ hours}$** (reflecting persistent nuclear gene expression changes).
- **Hepatic Metabolism & Clearance:** Cortisol is metabolized primarily in the liver via ring-A reduction by $5\alpha\text{-}$ and $5\beta\text{-reductases}$ and $3\alpha\text{-HSD}$ into dihydrocortisol and tetrahydrocortisol (`THF`), allotetrahydrocortisol (`allo-THF`), and tetrahydrocortisone (`THE`). These metabolites are glucuronidated and excreted in urine.
- **The Acute Secondary Adrenal Deficiency Diagnostic Window:** Adrenocortical zona fasciculata cells require **$4\text{ to }6\text{ weeks}$ to undergo disuse atrophy** following acute pituitary ACTH loss. Therefore, within the first $4\text{ weeks}$ after pituitary surgery or apoplexy, the $250\ \mu\text{g}$ ACTH Synacthen test will yield a **falsely normal stimulated peak ($\ge 18\ \mu\text{g/dL}$)**! In acute sellar injury, rely exclusively on **$08:00\text{ AM}$ basal cortisol** ($< 3.0\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ = AI; $> 15.0\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ = intact).

### 7.3 Six Detailed Worked Clinical Cases

#### Case 1: Primary Addison's Disease Secondary to Adrenal Tuberculosis
- **Patient:** 38-year-old male farmer from Bhiwani district, Haryana.
- **Clinical Presentation:** 6-month history of progressive asthenia, $9\text{ kg}$ weight loss, postural dizziness (orthostatic blood pressure $85/55\text{ mmHg}$ supine to $70/40\text{ mmHg}$ standing), and intense hyperpigmentation over knuckles, palmar creases, and buccal mucosa.
- **Laboratory Investigation:**
  * Serum Sodium: $124\text{ mmol/L}$ (Severe hyponatremia); Serum Potassium: $5.9\text{ mmol/L}$ (Hyperkalemia).
  * $08:00\text{ AM}$ Serum Cortisol: **$1.8\ \mu\text{g/dL}$** (Diagnostic cutoff $< 3.0\ \mu\text{g/dL}$).
  * Morning Chilled Plasma ACTH (`3.4.5`): **$860.0\text{ pg/mL}$** (Reference: $7.2\text{–}63.3\text{ pg/mL}$; markedly elevated).
  * $250\ \mu\text{g}$ ACTH Synacthen Stimulation: $T_0 = 1.8\ \mu\text{g/dL}$; $T_{30} = 2.1\ \mu\text{g/dL}$; $T_{60} = 2.2\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ (Flat, failed adrenocortical reserve).
- **Imaging & Conclusion:** Contrast-enhanced abdominal CT demonstrated bilateral enlarged adrenal glands with peripheral rim enhancement and central calcifications. Sputum GeneXpert positive for *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*. Diagnosed with **Primary Adrenal Insufficiency (Addison's Disease) secondary to Disseminated Tuberculosis**. Resuscitation with IV normal saline, hydrocortisone ($100\text{ mg}$ stat bolus then $200\text{ mg/day}$), fludrocortisone, and 4-drug ATT instituted with complete clinical recovery.

#### Case 2: Classic Pituitary Cushing's Disease in a Young Female
- **Patient:** 32-year-old female schoolteacher from Rohtak, Haryana.
- **Clinical Presentation:** 18-month progressive facial rounding ("moon facies"), dorsocervical fat pad ("buffalo hump"), central adiposity, wide ($> 1\text{ cm}$) violaceous abdominal striae, secondary oligomenorrhea, and new-onset hypertension ($160/100\text{ mmHg}$).
- **Laboratory Investigation:**
  * $08:00\text{ AM}$ Basal Cortisol: $24.8\ \mu\text{g/dL}$; $16:00\text{ PM}$ Cortisol: $22.5\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ (Complete loss of diurnal decline).
  * Overnight $1\text{ mg}$ Dexamethasone Suppression (`ODST`): $08:00\text{ AM}$ Cortisol $= \mathbf{9.4\ \mu\text{g/dL}}$ (Severe failure to suppress $< 1.8\ \mu\text{g/dL}$).
  * Late-Night Salivary Cortisol (`23:00` $\times 2$ nights): $0.48\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ and $0.55\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ (Both markedly elevated, normal $< 0.145\ \mu\text{g/dL}$).
  * $24\text{-Hour}$ Urine Free Cortisol (`UFC`): $340\ \mu\text{g/24h}$ (Normal $< 45\ \mu\text{g/24h}$; $> 7\times\text{ ULN}$).
  * Morning Plasma ACTH: $68.0\text{ pg/mL}$ (Inappropriately normal/elevated; ACTH-dependent hypercortisolism).
  * $8\text{ mg}$ High-Dose Dexamethasone Suppression (`HDDST`): Cortisol dropped from $24.8\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ to $7.2\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ ($71\%$ suppression, $> 50\%$ pass).
- **Imaging & Conclusion:** Dynamic contrast-enhanced pituitary MRI revealed a $5.5\text{ mm}$ hypoenhancing microadenoma in the left adenohypophysis. Diagnosed with **Pituitary Cushing's Disease**. Transsphenoidal selective adenomectomy achieved biochemical cure (post-op day 3 cortisol $1.1\ \mu\text{g/dL}$).

#### Case 3: Ectopic ACTH Syndrome Secondary to Bronchial Neuroendocrine Tumor
- **Patient:** 56-year-old male heavy smoker presenting to the emergency department in Charkhi Dadri.
- **Clinical Presentation:** Rapid-onset severe proximal myopathy (unable to climb stairs or stand from chair), dark cutaneous hyperpigmentation, severe peripheral edema, and new-onset brittle diabetes with blood glucose $380\text{ mg/dL}$.
- **Laboratory Investigation:**
  * Serum Potassium: **$2.2\text{ mmol/L}$** (Profound refractory hypokalemia); Serum Bicarbonate: $38\text{ mmol/L}$ (Severe metabolic alkalosis).
  * $08:00\text{ AM}$ Serum Cortisol: **$58.0\ \mu\text{g/dL}$**; Midnight Sleeping Cortisol: $54.0\ \mu\text{g/dL}$.
  * Overnight $1\text{ mg}$ ODST: Cortisol was **$52.0\ \mu\text{g/dL}$** (No suppression).
  * Morning Plasma ACTH (`3.4.5`): **$340.0\text{ pg/mL}$** (Markedly elevated).
  * $8\text{ mg}$ High-Dose Dexamethasone Suppression (`HDDST`): Baseline $58.0\ \mu\text{g/dL} \to$ Post-Dex Cortisol $= \mathbf{55.0\ \mu\text{g/dL}}$ ($< 5\%$ suppression, complete failure).
- **Imaging & Conclusion:** Sellar MRI was normal. High-resolution CT chest revealed a $2.2\text{ cm}$ well-circumscribed lesion in the right middle lobe with mediastinal lymphadenopathy. 68Ga-DOTATATE PET-CT confirmed intense somatostatin receptor uptake. Diagnosed with **Ectopic ACTH Syndrome secondary to Bronchial Neuroendocrine Tumor (Carcinoid)**. Metyrapone and ketoconazole initiated to block steroidogenesis, followed by curative right middle lobectomy.

#### Case 4: Clandestine Ayurvedic "Desi Pudiya" Steroid Ingestion & Acute HPA Withdrawal
- **Patient:** 52-year-old female with long-standing rheumatoid arthritis from a rural village near Dadri.
- **Clinical Presentation:** Presented in an acute hypotensive crisis ($80/50\text{ mmHg}$), nausea, abdominal pain, and profound weakness. History revealed taking unlabelled herbal packets ("Desi Joint Pain Pudiya") for 2 years, which she abruptly stopped 3 days prior due to running out of stock. She exhibited classic cushingoid facies and centripetal fat distribution.
- **Laboratory Investigation:**
  * $08:00\text{ AM}$ Serum Cortisol: **$0.7\ \mu\text{g/dL}$** (Profoundly low, $< 3.0\ \mu\text{g/dL}$).
  * Morning Plasma ACTH (`3.4.5`): **$< 5.0\text{ pg/mL}$** (Completely suppressed).
  * $250\ \mu\text{g}$ ACTH Synacthen Test: Peak Cortisol was $2.8\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ (Adrenocortical atrophy from prolonged tertiary suppression).
- **Toxicology & Conclusion:** Chemical LC-MS/MS screen of the herbal powder confirmed high concentrations of adulterated Dexamethasone and Betamethasone ($2.5\text{ mg}$ per packet). Diagnosed with **Iatrogenic / Clandestine Cushing's Syndrome with Acute Secondary Adrenal Crisis upon Abrupt Withdrawal**. Resuscitated with IV Hydrocortisone, transitioned to physiological oral steroid tapering over 9 months until HPA axis recovered.

#### Case 5: Oral Contraceptive Pill (OCP) Induced CBG Surge Mimicking Hypercortisolism
- **Patient:** 24-year-old female software engineer referred for evaluation of an "elevated cortisol".
- **Clinical Presentation:** Asymptomatic; general health checkup at a corporate clinic showed an $08:00\text{ AM}$ total cortisol of $34.0\ \mu\text{g/dL}$. Referring physician performed an overnight $1\text{ mg}$ ODST, which yielded an $08:00\text{ AM}$ cortisol of $3.8\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ (failing the $< 1.8\ \mu\text{g/dL}$ suppression cutoff), prompting an alarming provisional diagnosis of Cushing's syndrome.
- **Laboratory Investigation & Trap Resolution:**
  * Review of medication history confirmed she was taking a combined oral contraceptive pill containing $30\ \mu\text{g}$ Ethinyl Estradiol and Levonorgestrel for 8 months.
  * Late-Night Salivary Cortisol (`LNSC` $\times 2$ nights): **$0.06\ \mu\text{g/dL}$** and **$0.05\ \mu\text{g/dL}$** (Completely normal nocturnal nadir, $< 0.145\ \mu\text{g/dL}$).
  * $24\text{-Hour}$ Urine Free Cortisol (`UFC`): **$22.0\ \mu\text{g/24h}$** (Completely normal, reference $10\text{–}45\ \mu\text{g/24h}$).
- **Pathologist Interpretation:** Oral ethinyl estradiol stimulates massive hepatic CBG transcription, doubling total serum cortisol and causing false non-suppression on $1\text{ mg}$ ODST. Free bioactive cortisol is strictly normal. Cushing's syndrome was ruled out; patient was reassured and spared unnecessary pituitary/adrenal imaging.

#### Case 6: Acute Pituitary Apoplexy with Falsely Normal Synacthen Test
- **Patient:** 44-year-old male admitted with sudden severe headache, cranial nerve III palsy, and acute bitemporal visual field loss.
- **Clinical Presentation:** Urgent transsphenoidal decompression of a hemorrhagic pituitary macroadenoma was successfully performed. On Post-Operative Day 8, ward resident ordered a $250\ \mu\text{g}$ ACTH Synacthen stimulation test to assess adrenal sufficiency.
- **Laboratory Investigation & Physiological Pitfall Avoidance:**
  * $250\ \mu\text{g}$ Synacthen Test (Day 8 post-op): $T_0 = 2.4\ \mu\text{g/dL}$; $T_{30} = 17.5\ \mu\text{g/dL}$; $T_{60} = \mathbf{19.8\ \mu\text{g/dL}}$ ($\ge 18.0\ \mu\text{g/dL}$, apparently "passed").
  * Pathologist Intervention: The adrenal cortex takes $4\text{–}6\text{ weeks}$ to atrophy following acute pituitary ACTH deprivation. At Day 8, the adrenal glands retain transient responsiveness to massive pharmacological ACTH ($250\ \mu\text{g}$), yielding a dangerously misleading false-normal result!
  * Morning Basal Evaluation: $08:00\text{ AM}$ Basal Cortisol was **$2.1\ \mu\text{g/dL}$** (diagnostic of AI), and concurrent Plasma ACTH was **$< 5.0\text{ pg/mL}$**.
- **Conclusion:** Diagnosed with **Acute Central / Secondary Adrenal Insufficiency**. Discharged safely on maintenance oral hydrocortisone ($20\text{ mg}$ morning, $10\text{ mg}$ evening) and an emergency steroid card, preventing post-discharge fatal adrenal collapse.
