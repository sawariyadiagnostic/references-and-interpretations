## 📋 0.1a — Universal Master Report Footer (Place at the Bottom of Every Lab Report Page)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **LIMS Configuration Note:** Configure this block in your LIMS global report template footer so every printed and PDF report automatically carries statutory protection under the Indian Consumer Protection Act (CPA) 2019 and NABL ISO 15189:2022.

### **[LIMS COPY-PASTE BLOCK — STANDARD MASTER FOOTER]**

> **IMPORTANT CLINICAL & MEDICO-LEGAL DISCLAIMER (ISO 15189:2022 & CPA 2019 COMPLIANT):**
> 1. **Clinical Correlation Mandatory:** Laboratory test results are dependent on biological, physiological, diurnal, nutritional, and pre-analytical variables. Results must be interpreted strictly by a qualified Registered Medical Practitioner (RMP) in conjunction with the patient’s complete clinical history, physical examination, medication profile, and imaging/other diagnostic findings. A single laboratory value does not constitute a standalone clinical diagnosis.
> 2. **Analytical & Methodological Limitations:** All assays are performed using validated standard operating procedures and calibrated equipment backed by Internal Quality Control (IQC) and External Quality Assurance Schemes (EQAS). However, every diagnostic technology (Photometry, Ion-Selective Electrode, CLIA/ECLIA, ELISA, Nephelometry, Cell Impedance/Flow Cytometry, Rapid Immunochromatography) has inherent analytical sensitivity, specificity, Measurement Uncertainty (`MU`), and interference limitations (including heterophile antibodies, biotin/supplements, paraproteins, hook effect, hemolysis, lipemia, or icterus).
> 3. **Inter-Laboratory Variance & Reference Change Value (`RCV`):** Test values obtained from different laboratories or at different time points cannot be directly compared due to differences in assay methodology, reagent clone/calibrator traceability, instrumentation, and population-specific biological reference intervals. Serial changes smaller than the analyte's combined biological and analytical Reference Change Value (`RCV %`) may reflect physiological fluctuation rather than true clinical progression.
> 4. **Discordant Results Protocol (48-Hour Window):** In the event of an unexpected test result or a value that does not correlate with the clinical picture, the referring clinician/patient is requested to contact the laboratory within **48 hours** of report release for sample archival verification and complimentary repeat/reflex evaluation prior to initiating irreversible therapeutic or surgical intervention.
> 5. **Scope of Liability & Medico-Legal Restriction:** This report relates exclusively to the specimen tested as received at the laboratory. In case of any technical or clerical discrepancy, the maximum liability of the laboratory shall remain strictly limited to a refund or free repeat of the specific test charged. **This report is NOT valid for medico-legal purposes.**

## ⚖️ Why This Protects Your Laboratory in Haryana Consumer Courts (NCDRC / SCDRC Precedents)

1. **Defeats "Negligence per se" on Inter-Lab Discrepancies (*Bolam* & *Jacob Mathew v. State of Punjab* Standards):** Under Supreme Court of India and National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (NCDRC) rulings, a diagnostic lab is **not negligent** merely because a second lab reports a different numerical value—*provided* the first lab documents its **assay methodology**, **instrument calibration/QC**, **Measurement Uncertainty (`MU`)**, **biological/diurnal variability (`RCV %`)**, and **inter-laboratory non-comparability** on the report face.
2. **Shifts Diagnostic & Therapeutic Responsibility to the Clinician (RMP):** Explicitly stating that laboratory findings are **ancillary aids** and must be correlated clinically before starting treatment or surgery prevents claims where a patient self-medicates or a clinician acts on an isolated borderline/false-positive screening test without confirmatory testing.
3. **48-Hour Archival Verification Window:** Offering a formal **48-hour sample verification / complimentary repeat test window** demonstrates statutory good faith under the Consumer Protection Act 2019 and strict adherence to NABL ISO 15189:2022 Clause 7.9 complaint-resolution standards.
