## 📐 Section 4: Master Mathematical Equations & Algorithmic Derivations for Lipidology & ASCVD Risk

To prevent black-box LIMS calculation errors during NABL (`ISO 15189:2022 Clause 5.5 / 7.3`) and CAP inspections, every derived lipid parameter in Cluster `2.4` is governed by the following verified mathematical formulas, domain constraints, and unit conversions:

### 4.1 Classical Friedewald (1972) Equation & Error Propagation

In conventional mass units ($\text{mg/dL}$) and SI molar units ($\text{mmol/L}$):

$$\text{LDL-C}_{\text{Friedewald (mg/dL)}} = \text{TC} - \text{HDL-C} - \left(\frac{\text{TG}}{5}\right) \qquad \text{where } \text{VLDL-C}_{\text{Friedewald}} = \frac{\text{TG}}{5}$$

$$\text{LDL-C}_{\text{Friedewald (mmol/L)}} = \text{TC} - \text{HDL-C} - \left(\frac{\text{TG}}{2.2}\right) \qquad \text{where } \text{VLDL-C}_{\text{Friedewald}} = \frac{\text{TG}}{2.2}$$

- **Strict Validity Constraints (Mandatory LIMS Guardrails):**
  1. **Invalid when $\text{TG} > 400\text{ mg/dL}$ ($> 4.52\text{ mmol/L}$):** Chylomicrons and large triglyceride-rich VLDL particles have a $\text{TG}:\text{Cholesterol}$ ratio approaching $8:1$ to $20:1$; dividing by $5$ massively overestimates $\text{VLDL-C}$ and causes severe negative bias or mathematically negative `LDL-C`.
  2. **Clinically Unreliable when $\text{TG} = 150\text{–}400\text{ mg/dL}$ OR when $\text{LDL-C} < 70\text{ mg/dL}$:** On high-intensity statin + ezetimibe / PCSK9i therapy, Friedewald under-estimates true `LDL-C` by $-8\text{ to }-35\text{ mg/dL}$ in $> 25\%$ of Indian patients, leading to false achievement of `< 50` or `< 70 mg/dL` `LAI` goals.
  3. **Invalid in Fredrickson Type III Dysbetalipoproteinemia (`APOE2/E2`):** Remnant $\beta\text{-VLDL}$ is cholesterol-enriched ($\text{TG}:\text{VLDL-C}$ ratio $\approx 2.5:1$ to $3.0:1$ instead of $5:1$).
- **Combined Analytical Uncertainty Propagation (`ISO/TS 20914`):**
  $$u_c(\text{LDL-C}_{\text{Friedewald}}) = \sqrt{u^2(\text{TC}) + u^2(\text{HDL-C}) + \left(0.20 \times u(\text{TG})\right)^2}$$

### 4.2 NIH Sampson–NIH (2020) Bivariate Quadratic Equation (Primary Calculated Equation up to $\text{TG} \le 800\text{ mg/dL}$)

Derived by **Sampson et al. (*JAMA Cardiology*, 2020;5(5):540–548)** at the National Institutes of Health against **CDC Beta-Quantification Ultracentrifugation** ($n = 18,715$ lipid profiles), the **Sampson–NIH equation** models the changing cholesterol content of VLDL as a bivariate function of both **$\text{TG}$** and **$\text{Non-HDL-C}$**, plus a quadratic correction term ($-\text{TG}^2/16100$) for chylomicron/large-VLDL triglyceride enrichment:

$$\text{VLDL-C}_{\text{Sampson (mg/dL)}} = \frac{\text{TG}}{8.59} + \frac{\text{TG} \times \text{Non-HDL-C}}{2250} - \frac{\text{TG}^2}{16500}$$

$$\text{LDL-C}_{\text{Sampson (mg/dL)}} = \frac{\text{TC}}{0.948} - \frac{\text{HDL-C}}{0.971} - \left(\frac{\text{TG}}{8.56} + \frac{\text{TG} \times \text{Non-HDL-C}}{2140} - \frac{\text{TG}^2}{16100}\right) - 9.44$$

- **Superior Clinical Performance:** Validated for **$\text{TG}$ up to $800\text{ mg/dL}$ ($9.0\text{ mmol/L}$)** and **low $\text{LDL-C} < 50\text{–}70\text{ mg/dL}$**, reducing misclassification of `LAI` / `ESC` statin treatment categories by $> 65\%$ compared to Friedewald.
- **Worked Bench Verification Example (North Indian Diabetic Male, Age 46y):**
  - Measured on Bonavera Chem 400: $\text{TC} = 192\text{ mg/dL}$, $\text{HDL-C} = 33\text{ mg/dL}$, $\text{TG} = 335\text{ mg/dL} \implies \text{Non-HDL-C} = 192 - 33 = 159\text{ mg/dL}$.
  - **Legacy Friedewald:** $\text{LDL-C}_{\text{Friedewald}} = 192 - 33 - (335 / 5) = 159 - 67.0 = \mathbf{92.0\text{ mg/dL}}$ *(Falsely appears $< 100\text{ mg/dL}$!)*.
  - **NIH Sampson (2020):**
    $$\text{LDL-C}_{\text{Sampson}} = \frac{192}{0.948} - \frac{33}{0.971} - \left(\frac{335}{8.56} + \frac{335 \times 159}{2140} - \frac{335^2}{16100}\right) - 9.44$$
    $$\text{LDL-C}_{\text{Sampson}} = 202.53 - 33.99 - (39.14 + 24.89 - 6.97) - 9.44 = 168.54 - 57.06 - 9.44 = \mathbf{102.0\text{ mg/dL}}$$
  - **Direct Homogeneous `LDL-C` (`2.4.5`):** $\mathbf{104.5\text{ mg/dL}}$ *(Friedewald underestimated true `LDL-C` by $-12.5\text{ mg/dL}$, whereas Sampson agreed within $-2.5\text{ mg/dL}$!)*.

### 4.3 Martin–Hopkins Adjustable-Factor Equation ($\text{TG}:\text{VLDL-C}$ Factor Matrix $f_{\text{adj}} \in [3.1, 11.9]$)

Developed by **Martin et al. (*JAMA*, 2013;310(19):2061–2068)** and endorsed by the **2018/2024 AHA/ACC**, **2019/2023 ESC/EAS**, and **LAI 2023–2024 Guidelines** particularly when **$\text{LDL-C} < 70\text{ mg/dL}$**, the Martin–Hopkins equation replaces the fixed denominator $5$ with an empirically derived adjustable factor $f_{\text{adj}}$ based on the patient's $\text{TG}$ and $\text{Non-HDL-C}$ strata:

$$\text{LDL-C}_{\text{Martin–Hopkins (mg/dL)}} = \text{TC} - \text{HDL-C} - \left(\frac{\text{TG}}{f_{\text{adj}}(\text{TG}, \text{Non-HDL-C})}\right) \qquad \text{where } \text{VLDL-C}_{\text{Martin}} = \frac{\text{TG}}{f_{\text{adj}}}$$

### 4.4 Non-HDL Cholesterol (`Non-HDL-C`) & Remnant Cholesterol (`Remnant-C`)

$$\text{Non-HDL-C (mg/dL)} = \text{TC} - \text{HDL-C} = \text{LDL-C} + \text{VLDL-C} + \text{IDL-C} + \text{Chylomicron Remnants} + \text{Lp(a)-C}$$

$$\text{Remnant Cholesterol (mg/dL)} = \text{TC} - \text{HDL-C} - \text{LDL-C}_{\text{Direct or Sampson}}$$

- **Clinical Advantage of `Non-HDL-C`:** Totally independent of fasting status and triglyceride variability; co-primary target in **LAI 2023–2024** (always set **$+30\text{ mg/dL}$ above the `LDL-C` target**).
- **Clinical Significance of `Remnant-C`:** Quantifies cholesterol carried in triglyceride-rich lipoprotein remnants ($\text{VLDL}_2/\text{VLDL}_3$, $\text{IDL}$, and chylomicron remnants), which penetrate the arterial intima directly and are engulfed by macrophages **without requiring oxidative modification**.
  - **Optimal Fasting `Remnant-C`:** $< 24\text{ mg/dL}$ ($< 0.62\text{ mmol/L}$)
  - **High Residual ASCVD Risk Cutoff (`ESC/EAS` & Copenhagen General Population Study):** **Fasting $\ge 30\text{ mg/dL}$ ($\ge 0.78\text{ mmol/L}$)** or **Non-Fasting $\ge 35\text{ mg/dL}$ ($\ge 0.90\text{ mmol/L}$)**.

### 4.5 Castelli Risk Indices I & II, `Apo-B:Apo-A1` Ratio, and Atherogenic Index of Plasma (`AIP`)

1. **Castelli Risk Index I (`Total Cholesterol / HDL-C` Ratio):**
   $$\text{Castelli Index I} = \frac{\text{TC (mg/dL)}}{\text{HDL-C (mg/dL)}} \qquad (\text{Optimal: } < 3.5; \text{ Borderline: } 3.5\text{–}4.9; \text{ High Risk: } \ge 5.0)$$
2. **Castelli Risk Index II (`LDL-C / HDL-C` Ratio):**
   $$\text{Castelli Index II} = \frac{\text{LDL-C (mg/dL)}}{\text{HDL-C (mg/dL)}} \qquad (\text{Optimal: } < 2.5; \text{ Borderline: } 2.5\text{–}3.4; \text{ High Risk: } \ge 3.5)$$
3. **Atherogenic Index of Plasma (`AIP` — Dobiášová & Frohlich, 2001; Surrogate for Small-Dense LDL [`sdLDL`] Particle Diameter):**
   - **CRITICAL UNIT RULE:** `AIP` **MUST** be calculated using **SI Molar Units ($\text{mmol/L}$)** for both $\text{TG}$ and $\text{HDL-C}$! Calculating $\log_{10}(\text{TG}_{\text{mg/dL}}/\text{HDL-C}_{\text{mg/dL}})$ introduces a constant mathematical error of $+\log_{10}(88.57/38.67) = +0.360$!
   $$\text{AIP} = \log_{10}\left(\frac{\text{TG (mmol/L)}}{\text{HDL-C (mmol/L)}}\right) = \log_{10}\left(\frac{\text{TG (mg/dL)} \times 0.01129}{\text{HDL-C (mg/dL)} \times 0.02586}\right) = \log_{10}\left(\frac{\text{TG (mg/dL)}}{\text{HDL-C (mg/dL)}}\right) - 0.360$$
   - **`AIP` Risk Stratification & Lipoprotein Subfraction Correlation (`FER_HDL` & `sdLDL`):**
     - **Low Risk ($\text{AIP} < 0.11$):** Predominance of large, buoyant Pattern A $\text{LDL}$ particles (diameter $> 25.5\text{ nm}$).
     - **Intermediate Risk ($\text{AIP} = 0.11 \text{ – } 0.21$):** Mixed intermediate $\text{LDL}$ phenotype.
     - **High Atherogenic Risk ($\text{AIP} > 0.21$; Severe $> 0.40$):** Predominance of **Small, Dense Pattern B `sdLDL` particles (diameter $< 25.5\text{ nm}$)** with prolonged plasma half-life, reduced hepatic LDL-R affinity, high arterial proteoglycan binding, and accelerated oxidation (hallmark of **Indian Atherogenic Diabetic Dyslipidemia**).

### 4.6 `Lp(a)`-Corrected `LDL-C` (Unmasking Pseudo-Statin Resistance)

Both Direct Homogeneous `LDL-C` assays and Calculated `LDL-C` equations (`Friedewald`, `Sampson`, `Martin-Hopkins`) inadvertently include the cholesterol core carried inside **`Lipoprotein(a)` [`Lp(a)-C`]** (which accounts for $\sim 30\%$ of $\text{Lp(a)}$ total particle mass). Because statins do **not** lower $\text{Lp(a)}$ (and may increase it by $5\text{–}15\%$), a patient with markedly elevated $\text{Lp(a)}$ ($\ge 50\text{ mg/dL}$ / $\ge 125\text{ nmol/L}$) may appear "statin-resistant" when significant fraction of their measured `LDL-C` is actually statin-unresponsive `Lp(a)-C`!

$$\text{LDL-C}_{\text{Lp(a)-Corrected (mg/dL)}} = \text{LDL-C}_{\text{measured/calculated}} - \left(0.30 \times \text{Lp(a) [mg/dL]}\right)$$

$$\text{LDL-C}_{\text{Lp(a)-Corrected (mg/dL, from Molar Lp(a))}} \approx \text{LDL-C}_{\text{measured/calculated}} - \left(0.125 \times \text{Lp(a) [nmol/L]}\right)$$

- **Worked Clinical Example:** A post-CABG patient on Rosuvastatin $40\text{ mg}$ + Ezetimibe $10\text{ mg}$ has measured $\text{LDL-C} = 68\text{ mg/dL}$ (above the `< 50 mg/dL` Very High Risk target) and $\text{Lp(a)} = 90\text{ mg/dL}$ ($216\text{ nmol/L}$).
  - $\text{Lp(a)-C contribution} = 0.30 \times 90 = 27.0\text{ mg/dL}$.
  - $\text{True Non-Lp(a) LDL-C} = 68 - 27 = \mathbf{41.0\text{ mg/dL}}$ *(proving maximal statin/ezetimibe efficacy on true `LDL` receptors and indicating need for PCSK9i / Lp(a)-targeted therapy rather than blaming patient non-adherence!)*.

### 6.1 Fredrickson / WHO Hyperlipoproteinemia Phenotype Classification & `4°C` Overnight Fridge Standing Test

When severe dyslipidemia is encountered ($\text{TC} > 300\text{ mg/dL}$ or $\text{TG} > 500\text{ mg/dL}$), the laboratory correlates the quantitative lipid profile, `Apo-B` concentration, and the **16-Hour $4^\circ\text{C}$ Overnight Refrigerator Standing Test** (where low-density Chylomicrons [$d < 0.95\text{ g/mL}$] float to form a **creamy supernatant layer**, while smaller $\text{VLDL}$ particles [$d = 0.95\text{–}1.006\text{ g/mL}$] remain dispersed to create a **turbid/lactescent infranatant**):

### 6.2 Secondary Dyslipidemia & Competing Clinical Conditions Differential Diagnosis Matrix (8 Conditions)

Per **ESC/EAS**, **ACC/AHA**, and **LAI 2023–2024 Guidelines**, **secondary causes of dyslipidemia MUST be excluded by targeted biochemical testing before diagnosing a primary genetic lipid disorder or committing a patient to lifelong high-dose statin/fibrate therapy** (for example, administering a high-intensity statin or fibrate to an undiagnosed severely hypothyroid patient precipitates **statin-induced necrotizing myopathy and acute rhabdomyolysis**!):

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1.
