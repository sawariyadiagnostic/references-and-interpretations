### 1.2 Physiological Compartments of Circulating Plasma Calcium

Total Serum Calcium represents the sum of three distinct physicochemical pools in dynamic equilibrium governed by the Law of Mass Action, blood $\text{pH}$, and plasma protein concentration:

$$\text{Total Serum Calcium }\left(100\% \approx 8.6\text{–}10.2\text{ mg/dL}\text{ or }2.15\text{–}2.55\text{ mmol/L}\right) = \text{Free Ionized }\text{Ca}^{2+} + \text{Protein-Bound }\text{Ca} + \text{Diffusible Anion-Complexed }\text{Ca}$$

| Calcium Compartment | Fraction of Total Calcium | Approximate Concentration (Adult) | Physicochemical & Physiological Characteristics |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **1. Free Ionized Calcium ($\text{iCa}^{2+}$ — SOP `2.5.2`)** | **$45\text{–}50\%$** ($\sim 48\%$) | **$4.64\text{–}5.28\text{ mg/dL}$** ($1.16\text{–}1.32\text{ mmol/L}$) | **Only biologically active fraction!** Directly sensed by the seven-transmembrane G-protein coupled **Calcium-Sensing Receptor (`CaSR`)** on parathyroid chief cells, thyroid C-cells, and renal thick ascending limb (`TAL`) of Henle; mediates myocardial excitation-contraction coupling, neurotransmitter exocytosis, axonal voltage-gated $\text{Na}^+$ channel gating, and coagulation Factor IV activity. Ultrafiltrable across renal glomeruli. |
| **2. Protein-Bound Calcium (Non-Diffusible)** | **$40\text{–}45\%$** ($\sim 42\%$) | **$3.60\text{–}4.40\text{ mg/dL}$** ($0.90\text{–}1.10\text{ mmol/L}$) | **$\sim 80\%$ bound to Serum Albumin** ($12\text{–}16$ negatively charged carboxylate and imidazole/histidine residues per albumin molecule; association constant $K_a \approx 100\text{ L/mol}$ at $\text{pH } 7.40$) and **$\sim 20\%$ bound to $\alpha/\beta/\gamma$-Globulins**. **Non-ultrafiltrable** at the glomerulus; biologically inactive buffer reservoir. Strongly **$\text{pH}$-dependent** ($\text{H}^+$ protons compete directly with $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ for imidazole nitrogen sites on albumin). |
| **3. Diffusible Anion-Complexed Calcium** | **$8\text{–}10\%$** ($\sim 10\%$) | **$0.70\text{–}1.00\text{ mg/dL}$** ($0.17\text{–}0.25\text{ mmol/L}$) | Complexed to low-molecular-weight polyvalent anions: **Bicarbonate ($\text{CaHCO}_3^+$, $\sim 3.5\%$), Citrate ($\text{CaCit}^-$, $\sim 2.5\%$), Inorganic Phosphate ($\text{CaHPO}_4^0$, $\sim 2.0\%$), Lactate ($\sim 1.0\%$), and Sulfate ($\sim 1.0\%$)**. Ultrafiltrable across glomeruli and dialysis membranes, but **biologically inactive** at the `CaSR` and neuromuscular junction. Expands dramatically in `CKD Stage G4–G5`, lactic acidosis, and massive citrated blood transfusion! |

## ⚙️ Section 3: Hardware-Agnostic Analytical SOP, Spectrophotometric Chemistry (`Arsenazo III` vs `NM-BAPTA` vs `o-CPC`), Payne Equations & `CLSI` Verification

### 3.1 Spectrophotometric Chemistry & Comparative Method Architecture

1. **Primary Analytical Method on `Bonavera Chem 400` / `Chem 200` — `Arsenazo III` End-Point Colorimetry ($\lambda_1 = 650\text{ nm}$, $\lambda_2 = 700\text{ nm}$):**
   - **Reagent Composition (Single-Reagent Ready-to-Use Liquid Stable Formulation, `R1`):**
     - `MES` (`2-(N-morpholino)ethanesulfonic acid`) or `Imidazole` Buffer: $100\text{ mmol/L}$, **$\text{pH } 6.50 \pm 0.05$** at $37^\circ\text{C}$
     - **`Arsenazo III`** (`2,2'-[1,8-dihydroxy-3,6-disulfonaphthylene-2,7-bisazo]bisbenzenearsonic acid`, sodium salt): **$120\text{ }\mu\text{mol/L}$**
     - Non-ionic surfactant (`Brij-35` / `Polyoxyethylene lauryl ether`): $0.1\%\text{ w/v}$ (prevents protein precipitation and cuvette fouling)
   - **Stoichiometric Reaction & Detection Physics:**
     $$\text{Protein-Bound Ca} + \text{H}^+ \xrightarrow{\text{MES Buffer, pH } 6.50} \text{Free }\text{Ca}^{2+} + \text{Protonated Albumin}$$
     $$\text{Ca}^{2+} + \text{Arsenazo III}^{4-} \xrightarrow{\text{pH } 6.50,\; 37^\circ\text{C}} \left[\text{Ca–Arsenazo III}\right]^{2-}\text{ Purple-Blue Complex }\left(1:1\text{ Stoichiometry, }\lambda_{\max} = 650\text{ nm}\right)$$
   - **Why `Arsenazo III` ($\text{pH } 6.5$) Outperforms Legacy `o-CPC` ($\text{pH } 10.7$):**
     - At mildly acidic $\text{pH } 6.50$, `Arsenazo III` releases all protein-bound and anion-complexed $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and binds $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ with high selectivity ($K_d \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-5}\text{ M}$) while exhibiting **negligible affinity for Magnesium ($\text{Mg}^{2+}$ cross-reactivity $< 0.5\%$ up to $\text{Mg} = 10\text{ mg/dL}$)**.
     - Because the reaction occurs at $\text{pH } 6.50$, the reagent does **not** absorb atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ (unlike strongly alkaline `o-CPC` at $\text{pH } 10.7$, where $\text{CO}_2$ absorption causes downward pH drift and requires calibration every $24\text{–}48\text{ hours}$), delivering **30-day on-board reagent stability**!
     - Reading at long red wavelength ($\lambda_1 = 650\text{ nm}$, blanking at $\lambda_2 = 700\text{ nm}$) minimizes spectral interference from hemoglobin ($415/540/576\text{ nm}$), bilirubin ($450\text{ nm}$), and lipemic Rayleigh scattering ($\propto \lambda^{-4}$).
   - **Environmental & Disposal Precaution:** `Arsenazo III` contains organically bound arsenic ($\sim 18\text{ mg/L}$ elemental $\text{As}$ in working reagent). Spent reaction waste must be routed into the dedicated chemical-waste carboy under `Bio-Medical & Hazardous Waste Rules 2016/2019` (never discharged into municipal greywater).

2. **Alternative / Future-Upgrade Two-Reagent Method — `5-Nitro-5'-Methyl-BAPTA` (`NM-BAPTA`, $340/376\text{ nm}$):**
   - **Reaction Principle:**
     $$\text{Step 1 (R1: Alkaline Buffer pH } 10.3 + \text{NM-BAPTA):}\quad \text{Ca}^{2+} + \text{NM-BAPTA} \longrightarrow \text{Ca–NM-BAPTA Complex }\left(\Delta A\text{ measured at }340/376\text{ nm}\right)$$
     $$\text{Step 2 (R2: EDTA Chelator):}\quad \text{Ca–NM-BAPTA} + \text{EDTA} \longrightarrow \text{Ca–EDTA} + \text{Free NM-BAPTA }\left(\text{Specific }\Delta A\text{ subtraction}\right)$$
   - **Metrological Advantage:** Because `R2` (`EDTA`) specifically strips $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ from the `NM-BAPTA` chromophore and the analyzer measures the *difference* in absorbance before and after `EDTA` addition, sample blanking is internal, eliminating paraprotein turbidity, hemolysis, icterus, and linear gadolinium (`Gadodiamide`) interference.

3. **Legacy Comparative Method — `o-Cresolphthalein Complexone` (`o-CPC`, $570/660\text{ nm}$):**
   - Uses `R1` ($0.25\text{ mol/L}$ $\text{HCl}$ + `8-Hydroxyquinoline` [$5\text{ mmol/L}$] to release protein-bound $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and chelate/mask $\text{Mg}^{2+}$) followed by `R2` (`o-CPC` + corrosive alkaline `2-Amino-2-Methyl-1-Propanol [AMP]` or `Diethylamine` buffer at $\text{pH } 10.7$) to form a purple $2:1$ $\text{Ca–o-CPC}$ complex at $570\text{ nm}$. Highly susceptible to atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ absorption (reagent calibration stability $< 24\text{–}72\text{ hours}$), non-linear S-shaped calibration curves at low calcium, and gadolinium interference.

| Analytical Feature | `Arsenazo III` (Primary — `Bonavera Chem 400`) | `NM-BAPTA` (High-Throughput Modular Alternative) | `o-Cresolphthalein Complexone` (`o-CPC` Legacy) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reagent Format & Reaction $\text{pH}$** | Single-Reagent (`R1`), Mildly Acidic ($\text{pH } 6.50$) | Two-Reagent (`R1` $\text{pH } 10.3$ + `R2` `EDTA`) | Two-Reagent (`R1` $\text{HCl/8-HQ}$ + `R2` Alkaline $\text{pH } 10.7$) |

### 3.2 Payne's Albumin-Corrected Calcium Equations, `BCG` vs `BCP` Method Offset & Clinical Failure States

1. **Standard Payne Equation (Payne RB et al., *BMJ* 1973; Validated for Bromocresol Green [`BCG`] Albumin with Normal Mean $= 4.0\text{ g/dL}$ [$40\text{ g/L}$]):**
   - **Conventional Units ($\text{mg/dL}$ Calcium, $\text{g/dL}$ Albumin):**
     $$\text{Corrected Calcium (mg/dL)} = \text{Measured Total Calcium (mg/dL)} + 0.8 \times \left[4.0 - \text{Measured Serum Albumin}_{\text{BCG}}\text{ (g/dL)}\right]$$
   - **SI Units ($\text{mmol/L}$ Calcium, $\text{g/L}$ Albumin):**
     $$\text{Corrected Calcium (mmol/L)} = \text{Measured Total Calcium (mmol/L)} + 0.020 \times \left[40 - \text{Measured Serum Albumin}_{\text{BCG}}\text{ (g/L)}\right]$$
   - **LIMS Application Rule at Sawariya Diagnostic Lab:** Because our primary Serum Albumin assay (`SOP 2.2.8` on `Bonavera Chem 400`) utilizes the **early-reading Bromocresol Green (`BCG`) method**, the classic Payne equation above is programmed into the LIMS and applied whenever $\text{Serum Albumin} < 4.0\text{ g/dL}$ (and optionally reported across all paired orders). When $\text{Serum Albumin} > 4.5\text{ g/dL}$ (e.g., severe dehydration/tourniquet stasis), the formula subtracts $0.8 \times (\text{Albumin} - 4.0)$.

2. **Why `BCG` vs `BCP` Albumin Methodology Matters (`KDIGO 2017/2024` & `IFCC` Warning):**
   - **Bromocresol Green (`BCG`) Non-Specificity in Hypoalbuminemia:** In patients with chronic inflammation, nephrotic syndrome, cirrhosis, or `CKD`, acute-phase $\alpha_1$- and $\alpha_2$-globulins cross-react with `BCG` dye, causing `BCG` to **overestimate true serum albumin by $+0.3\text{ to }+0.6\text{ g/dL}$ ($+3\text{ to }+6\text{ g/L}$)** when true albumin is $< 3.0\text{ g/dL}$.
   - **Bromocresol Purple (`BCP`) / Immunoturbidimetric Modified Equation:** Laboratories using `BCP` or nephelometric albumin (which do not cross-react with $\alpha$-globulins and have a lower reference mean of $\sim 3.5\text{–}3.7\text{ g/dL}$) cannot use the standard `BCG` Payne equation without inducing systematic false hypercalcemia ($+0.3\text{ to }+0.5\text{ mg/dL}$). For `BCP` albumin, the modified slope/intercept equation is:
     $$\text{Corrected Calcium}_{\text{BCP}}\text{ (mg/dL)} = \text{Measured Total Calcium (mg/dL)} + 0.8 \times \left[3.5 - \text{Measured Serum Albumin}_{\text{BCP}}\text{ (g/dL)}\right]$$

3. **Clinical Scenarios Where Payne's Albumin-Corrected Calcium Fails (`25–35%` Diagnostic Discordance vs Direct Ionized Calcium `2.5.2`):**

| Clinical Scenario / Pathological State | Direction of Payne Correction Error | Biophysical Mechanism & Mandatory Laboratory Action (`SOP 2.5.2` Reflex) |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **1. Acute Acid-Base Disorders (Metabolic / Respiratory Alkalosis or Acidosis)** | **Alkalosis: Masks True $\text{iCa}^{2+}$ Tetany**<br>**Acidosis: False Low/Normal** | Protons ($\text{H}^+$) compete directly with $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ for negative histidine/carboxylate binding sites on albumin. **Every $+0.10\text{ pH}$ unit rise (alkalosis) decreases free $\text{iCa}^{2+}$ by $-0.05\text{ mmol/L}$ ($-0.20\text{ mg/dL}$, $\sim 4\text{–}5\%$)** without changing Total Calcium or Albumin! Conversely, every $-0.10\text{ pH}$ drop (acidosis) increases free $\text{iCa}^{2+}$ by $+0.05\text{ mmol/L}$ ($+0.20\text{ mg/dL}$). **Action:** Order **Direct $\text{iCa}^{2+}$ (`2.5.2`)**. |
| **2. Stage G4–G5 Chronic Kidney Disease (`CKD-MBD`) & Dialysis (`KDIGO 2017/2024`)** | **Up to $30\%$ Misclassification (Usually Overestimates Calcium Status)** | Retention of inorganic phosphate, sulfate, citrate, and uremic toxins expands the **diffusible anion-complexed calcium pool from $10\%$ to $18\text{–}22\%$**, while metabolic acidosis and carbamylated albumin alter binding affinity ($K_a$). `KDIGO` explicitly recommends abandoning Albumin-Corrected Calcium in `CKD G4–G5D` in favor of uncorrected Total Calcium + **Direct $\text{iCa}^{2+}$**. |
| **3. Critical Illness, Sepsis & Severe Hypoalbuminemia ($\text{Albumin} < 2.5\text{ g/dL}$)** | **Overestimates Free $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ by $+0.4\text{ to }+1.1\text{ mg/dL}$ (Masks True Hypocalcemia)** | At $\text{Albumin} < 2.5\text{ g/dL}$, the remaining albumin molecules increase their $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ binding affinity ($> 0.8\text{ mg Ca/g Albumin}$), while `BCG` overestimates albumin due to elevated acute-phase $\alpha$-globulins. Payne-corrected calcium falsely reassures clinicians ("normal $9.1\text{ mg/dL}$") when true $\text{iCa}^{2+}$ is critically low ($< 0.95\text{ mmol/L}$). |
| **4. Massive Blood Transfusion, Plasmapheresis & Citrate `CRRT` Anticoagulation** | **Completely Blind to Acute Citrate Chelation!** | Citrate chelates free $\text{iCa}^{2+}$ into diffusible $\text{CaCit}^-$ complexes. Because `Arsenazo III` at $\text{pH } 6.5$ dissociates $\text{CaCit}^-$ and measures **all** calcium (including citrate-bound calcium), **Measured Total Calcium is normal or elevated ($9.5\text{–}11.5\text{ mg/dL}$) while the patient is in lethal ionized hypocalcemic cardiac arrest ($\text{iCa}^{2+} < 0.70\text{ mmol/L}$)**! |
| **5. Multiple Myeloma / Waldenström Paraproteinemia (`Pseudohypercalcemia`)** | **False Hypercalcemia ($\text{Total Ca } 13.0\text{–}17.5\text{ mg/dL}$)** | Monoclonal $\text{IgG}/\text{IgA}/\text{IgM}$ paraproteins either bind $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ via mutated Fab/Fc hypervariable domains OR precipitate at $\text{pH } 6.50$. Payne correction only adjusts for albumin, not globulins. **Action:** Always verify unexpected asymptomatic hypercalcemia in myeloma with **Direct $\text{iCa}^{2+}$ (`2.5.2`)** before giving IV Zoledronate! |
| **6. Elevated Free Fatty Acids (`FFA`), Intralipid `TPN`, or Unfractionated Heparin** | **Variable Discordance** | Intravenous lipid emulsions (`TPN`) and heparin-activated lipoprotein lipase generate non-esterified fatty acids (`NEFA`) that bind albumin and increase its negative charge/affinity for $\text{Ca}^{2+}$, lowering free $\text{iCa}^{2+}$ while Total Calcium remains unchanged. |

### 4.2 `ISO/TS 20914:2019` 2-Variable Uncertainty Propagation for Payne Albumin-Corrected Calcium

Because Payne's Albumin-Corrected Calcium is a **derived two-parameter equation** combining Measured Total Calcium ($\text{Ca}_{\text{Total}}$) and Measured Serum Albumin ($\text{Alb}$), its combined standard measurement uncertainty $u_c(\text{Ca}_{\text{corr}})$ must be derived using the `GUM` (`JCGM 100:2008`) & `ISO/TS 20914:2019` partial-derivative law of propagation of uncertainty for independent variables:

$$\text{Ca}_{\text{corr}} = \text{Ca}_{\text{Total}} + 0.8 \times \left(4.0 - \text{Alb}\right) = \text{Ca}_{\text{Total}} - 0.8\,\text{Alb} + 3.2$$

Taking the partial derivatives (sensitivity coefficients $c_1 = \frac{\partial \text{Ca}_{\text{corr}}}{\partial \text{Ca}_{\text{Total}}} = +1.0$ and $c_2 = \frac{\partial \text{Ca}_{\text{corr}}}{\partial \text{Alb}} = -0.8\text{ mg Ca/g Alb}$):

$$u_c\left(\text{Ca}_{\text{corr}}\right) = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial \text{Ca}_{\text{corr}}}{\partial \text{Ca}_{\text{Total}}}\right)^2 u^2\left(\text{Ca}_{\text{Total}}\right) + \left(\frac{\partial \text{Ca}_{\text{corr}}}{\partial \text{Alb}}\right)^2 u^2\left(\text{Alb}\right)} = \sqrt{u^2\left(\text{Ca}_{\text{Total}}\right) + \left[0.8 \times u\left(\text{Alb}\right)\right]^2}$$

- **At Normal Physiological Concentrations ($\text{Ca}_{\text{Total}} = 9.50\text{ mg/dL}$, $\text{Albumin} = 4.00\text{ g/dL}$):**
  - Standard uncertainty of Total Calcium: $u(\text{Ca}_{\text{Total}}) = 1.21\% \times 9.50\text{ mg/dL} = \mathbf{0.115\text{ mg/dL}}$ (Expanded $U_{\text{Ca}} = 2 \times 0.115 = \pm 0.23\text{ mg/dL}$ or $\pm 2.42\%$).
  - Standard uncertainty of Serum Albumin (`SOP 2.2.8` `BCG`, $u(\text{Alb})\% = 2.50\%$): $u(\text{Alb}) = 2.50\% \times 4.00\text{ g/dL} = \mathbf{0.100\text{ g/dL}}$.
  - Combined Standard Uncertainty of Payne Corrected Calcium:
    $$u_c\left(\text{Ca}_{\text{corr}}\right) = \sqrt{(0.115)^2 + (0.8 \times 0.100)^2} = \sqrt{0.013225 + 0.006400} = \sqrt{0.019625} = \mathbf{0.140\text{ mg/dL}}\quad\left(\mathbf{1.47\%}\right)$$
  - **Expanded Uncertainty ($U_{\text{Ca,corr}}$ at $k=2$, $95.45\%$ confidence):**
    $$U\left(\text{Ca}_{\text{corr}}\right) = 2 \times 0.140\text{ mg/dL} = \mathbf{\pm 0.28\text{ mg/dL}}\quad\left(\mathbf{\pm 0.07\text{ mmol/L}}\text{ or }\mathbf{\pm 2.95\%}\right)$$

#### Case 1: Differentiation of Familial Hypocalciuric Hypercalcemia (`FHH`) from Primary Hyperparathyroidism (`PHPT`) Avoiding Unnecessary Parathyroidectomy
- **Clinical Presentation:** A $29\text{-year-old}$ asymptomatic male schoolteacher from Charkhi Dadri is referred to an endocrine surgeon for "Primary Hyperparathyroidism" after two routine health checkups showed persistent mild hypercalcemia ($\text{Total Ca} = 11.1\text{ mg/dL}$ and $11.2\text{ mg/dL}$). A neck ultrasound shows normal thyroid/parathyroid glands, and a $^{99\text{m}}\text{Tc-Sestamibi}$ scan is negative. Before proceeding with bilateral 4-gland surgical neck exploration, a complete metabolic & 24-hour acidified urine panel is performed at Sawariya Diagnostic Lab:
  - **Serum Total Calcium:** $11.20\text{ mg/dL}$ ($2.80\text{ mmol/L}$; Reference: $8.6\text{–}10.2\text{ mg/dL}$)
  - **Serum Albumin (`BCG`):** $4.20\text{ g/dL}$ $\rightarrow$ **Payne Corrected Calcium:** $11.20 + 0.8 \times (4.0 - 4.20) = \mathbf{11.04\text{ mg/dL}}$
  - **Serum Creatinine (`IDMS`):** $0.90\text{ mg/dL}$ ($\text{eGFR} = 108\text{ mL/min/1.73 m}^2$); **Serum Phosphorus:** $2.9\text{ mg/dL}$; **Serum Magnesium:** $2.45\text{ mg/dL}$ (Mildly elevated!)
  - **Intact `PTH` (`3.2.17`):** $58.0\text{ pg/mL}$ (Inappropriately non-suppressed; Reference: $15\text{–}65\text{ pg/mL}$); **$25\text{-OH Vitamin D}$:** $38.4\text{ ng/mL}$ (Sufficient $\ge 30\text{ ng/mL}$)
  - **24-Hour Acidified Urine ($\text{Volume} = 1,800\text{ mL}$, $\text{pH} = 1.6$):** $\text{Urine Calcium} = 3.20\text{ mg/dL}$ ($57.6\text{ mg/24h}$ — marked hypocalciuria $< 100\text{ mg/24h}$!); $\text{Urine Creatinine} = 85.7\text{ mg/dL}$ ($1,543\text{ mg/24h}$).
- **Step-by-Step `CCCR` (`FECa`) Calculation:**
  $$\text{CCCR (FECa)} = \frac{\text{Urine Ca (mg/dL)} \times \text{Serum Cr (mg/dL)}}{\text{Serum Total Ca (mg/dL)} \times \text{Urine Cr (mg/dL)}} = \frac{3.20 \times 0.90}{11.20 \times 85.7} = \frac{2.88}{959.84} = \mathbf{0.0030}\quad\left(\mathbf{0.30\%}\right)\quad\left[\text{or if }\text{U}_{\text{Ca}} = 6.4\text{ mg/dL}, \text{CCCR} = \mathbf{0.0060}\; (0.60\%)\right]$$
- **Diagnostic Conclusion & Surgical Impact:** Whether $\text{CCCR} = 0.0030$ or $0.0060$, it is far below the **$< 0.010$ ($< 1.0\%$) diagnostic cutoff for Familial Hypocalciuric Hypercalcemia (`FHH`)**, accompanied by classic mild hypermagnesemia ($2.45\text{ mg/dL}$). Screening the patient's father reveals asymptomatic hypercalcemia ($10.9\text{ mg/dL}$), and `CASR` gene sequencing confirms a heterozygous loss-of-function mutation (`FHH Type 1`). **Unnecessary bilateral neck exploration and permanent iatrogenic hypoparathyroidism are completely averted!**

#### Case 5: Full `ISO/TS 20914:2019` `MU` & `RCV` Calculation for Total Calcium and 2-Parameter Payne Albumin-Corrected Calcium

      $$U(\text{Ca})\% = 2 \times 1.211\% = \mathbf{\pm 2.42\%}\quad\left(\text{At }\text{Ca} = 10.20\text{ mg/dL}: \mathbf{10.20 \pm 0.25\text{ mg/dL}}\text{ or }9.95\text{–}10.45\text{ mg/dL}\right)$$

    - A cirrhosis patient has **$\text{Measured Total Ca} = 7.80\text{ mg/dL}$** and **$\text{Measured Serum Albumin} = 2.20\text{ g/dL}$** (`SOP 2.2.8` `BCG`, where $u_c(\text{Alb})\% = 2.50\%$).
    - **Step 1 — Calculate Payne Corrected Calcium:**
      $$\text{Ca}_{\text{corr}} = 7.80 + 0.8 \times \left(4.00 - 2.20\right) = 7.80 + 1.44 = \mathbf{9.24\text{ mg/dL}}$$
    - **Step 2 — Convert Relative Standard Uncertainties to Absolute Units ($\text{mg/dL}$ and $\text{g/dL}$):**
      $$u\left(\text{Ca}_{\text{Total}}\right) = 1.211\% \times 7.80\text{ mg/dL} = \mathbf{0.0945\text{ mg/dL}}$$
      $$u\left(\text{Alb}\right) = 2.50\% \times 2.20\text{ g/dL} = \mathbf{0.0550\text{ g/dL}}$$
    - **Step 3 — Apply 2-Variable `ISO/TS 20914:2019` Propagation Formula:**
      $$u_c\left(\text{Ca}_{\text{corr}}\right) = \sqrt{u^2\left(\text{Ca}_{\text{Total}}\right) + \left[0.8 \times u\left(\text{Alb}\right)\right]^2} = \sqrt{(0.0945)^2 + (0.8 \times 0.0550)^2} = \sqrt{0.00893 + 0.001936} = \sqrt{0.010866} = \mathbf{0.1042\text{ mg/dL}}$$
    - **Step 4 — Expanded Uncertainty ($k=2$):**
      $$U\left(\text{Ca}_{\text{corr}}\right) = 2 \times 0.1042 = \mathbf{\pm 0.21\text{ mg/dL}}\quad\left(\text{Reported as }\mathbf{9.24 \pm 0.21\text{ mg/dL}}\text{ or }9.03\text{–}9.45\text{ mg/dL}\right)$$
  - **Step 5 — Serial Reference Change Value (`RCV`) Application:**
    - If a patient's baseline Total Calcium is $9.10\text{ mg/dL}$ and increases $6\text{ months}$ later to $9.90\text{ mg/dL}$ (still inside the population reference interval of $8.6\text{–}10.2\text{ mg/dL}$!), the percentage change is:
      $$\Delta\% = \frac{9.90 - 9.10}{9.10} \times 100 = \mathbf{+8.79\%}$$
    - Because $+8.79\%$ exceeds both **$\text{RCV}_{95\%}$ ($\pm 5.61\%$)** and **$\text{RCV}_{99\%}$ ($\pm 7.38\%$)**, this $+0.80\text{ mg/dL}$ rise represents a **statistically significant ($p < 0.01$) true physiological shift** (`II = 0.67`) warranting `iPTH` evaluation for early `PHPT`!
