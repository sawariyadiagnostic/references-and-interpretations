### 0.2 — Quantitative `ISO 22367:2020` Failure Mode, Effects, and Criticality Analysis (`FMECA`) Matrix

> **Risk Scoring Methodology (`ISO 22367:2020` & `CLSI EP18-A2`):**
> - **Severity ($S$, $1\text{–}10$):** $10 =$ Fatal iatrogenic cardiac arrest / irreversible central pontine myelinolysis (`ODS`); $8\text{–}9 =$ Unnecessary emergency hemodialysis, missed life-threatening AKI/toxic alcohol poisoning, or major therapeutic mistreatment.
> - **Occurrence ($O$, $1\text{–}10$):** $1 =$ $< 1\text{ in }10,000$; $5 =$ $1\text{ in }400$; $8 =$ $> 1\text{ in }50$ in unmitigated routine laboratory practice.
> - **Detection ($D$, $1\text{–}10$):** $10 =$ Undetectable without automated LIMS multi-parameter cross-checks; $1 =$ $100\%$ intercepted by automated LIMS hard-lock before release.
> - **Risk Priority Number ($\text{RPN} = S \times O \times D$):** Any pre-mitigation $\text{RPN} \ge 100$ or Severity $S \ge 9$ mandates an engineered LIMS hard-lock and bench verification protocol reducing residual $\text{RPN} < 45$.

### 2.1 — Glomerular Filtration Rate (`eGFR`) & Creatinine Clearance Equations

#### 1. 2021 NKF-ASN Race-Free `CKD-EPI` Creatinine Equation (`eGFRcr`, Adults $\ge 18\text{ Years}$) [Inker et al., *NEJM* 2021; KDIGO 2024 Mandatory Standard]
$$\text{eGFR}_{\text{cr}}\text{ (mL/min/1.73 m}^2\text{)} = 142 \times \min\left(\frac{S_{\text{cr}}}{\kappa}, 1\right)^\alpha \times \max\left(\frac{S_{\text{cr}}}{\kappa}, 1\right)^{-1.200} \times 0.9938^{\text{Age (years)}} \times 1.012\text{ [if Female]}$$

| Biological Sex | Spline Knot Constant $\kappa$ ($\text{mg/dL}$ / $\mu\text{mol/L}$) | Exponent $\alpha$ (when $S_{\text{cr}} \le \kappa$) | Exponent (when $S_{\text{cr}} > \kappa$) | Sex Multiplier | Explicit Piecewise LIMS Formula ($S_{\text{cr}}$ in $\text{mg/dL}$) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |

$$\text{eGFR}_{\text{cr-cys}} = 135 \times \min\left(\frac{S_{\text{cr}}}{\kappa}, 1\right)^\alpha \times \max\left(\frac{S_{\text{cr}}}{\kappa}, 1\right)^{-0.544} \times \min\left(\frac{S_{\text{cys}}}{0.8}, 1\right)^{-0.323} \times \max\left(\frac{S_{\text{cys}}}{0.8}, 1\right)^{-0.778} \times 0.9961^{\text{Age}} \times 0.963\text{ [if Female]}$$

  - **Female:** $\kappa = 0.7\text{ mg/dL}$, $\alpha = -0.219$, Sex multiplier $= 0.963$, Cystatin C knot $= 0.8\text{ mg/L}$.

*Used when severe sarcopenia, bilateral amputation, quadriplegia, cirrhosis, bodybuilding/creatine supplementation, or `OCT2/MATE1` drug inhibition (`Trimethoprim`, `Cobicistat`, `Dolutegravir`) causes severe discordance ($\lvert \text{eGFR}_{\text{cys}} - \text{eGFR}_{\text{cr}} \rvert \ge 15\text{ mL/min/1.73 m}^2$):*

#### 4. Pediatric & Young Adult `CKiD U25` Equations (Age $1\text{ to }25\text{ Years}$, Pierce et al., *Kidney Int* 2021) & Bedside Schwartz Equation
$$\text{eGFR}_{\text{Bedside Schwartz}}\text{ (mL/min/1.73 m}^2\text{)} = 0.413 \times \frac{\text{Height (cm)}}{S_{\text{cr}}\text{ (mg/dL)}} \quad ; \quad \text{eGFR}_{\text{CKiD U25}} = k_{\text{cr}} \times \frac{\text{Height (m)}}{S_{\text{cr}}\text{ (mg/dL)}}$$

#### 5. De-Indexed Absolute GFR ($\text{mL/min}$) for Chemotherapy / Narrow-Therapeutic-Index Drug Dosing & `Cockcroft-Gault` (`CrCl_CG`)
- **BSA De-Indexing (Mandatory for patients with $\text{BMI} < 18.5$ or $> 30\text{ kg/m}^2$ receiving Carboplatin [Calvert Formula], Methotrexate, Vancomycin, DOACs, or Aminoglycosides):**
  $$\text{BSA}_{\text{Du Bois}}\text{ (m}^2\text{)} = 0.007184 \times \text{Weight (kg)}^{0.425} \times \text{Height (cm)}^{0.725} \quad ; \quad \text{eGFR}_{\text{Absolute (mL/min)}} = \text{eGFR}_{\text{Indexed}} \times \frac{\text{BSA (m}^2\text{)}}{1.73}$$
- **Cockcroft-Gault Equation (`CrCl_CG`, Legacy FDA Package-Insert Drug Dosing Standard):**
  $$\text{CrCl}_{\text{CG}}\text{ (mL/min)} = \frac{(140 - \text{Age [years]}) \times \text{Weight}_{\text{dosing}}\text{ (kg)}}{72 \times S_{\text{cr}}\text{ (mg/dL)}} \times 0.85\text{ [if Female]}$$
  - *Weight Selection Rules (`Winter Criteria`):* If Actual Body Weight ($\text{ABW}$) $< \text{IBW}$, use $\text{ABW}$. If normal weight, use Ideal Body Weight ($\text{IBW}_{\text{Male}} = 50 + 2.3 \times [\text{Height (in)} - 60]$; $\text{IBW}_{\text{Female}} = 45.5 + 2.3 \times [\text{Height (in)} - 60]$). If obese ($\text{ABW} > 1.2 \times \text{IBW}$), use **Adjusted Body Weight**: $\text{AdjBW} = \text{IBW} + 0.40 \times (\text{ABW} - \text{IBW})$.
- **Measured 24-Hour Endogenous Creatinine Clearance (`SOP 2.3.11`):**
  $$\text{CrCl}_{\text{measured}}\text{ (mL/min/1.73 m}^2\text{)} = \frac{U_{\text{cr}}\text{ (mg/dL)} \times V_{24\text{h}}\text{ (mL)}}{S_{\text{cr}}\text{ (mg/dL)} \times 1440\text{ (min)}} \times \frac{1.73}{\text{BSA (m}^2\text{)}}$$

#### 6. 4-Variable Kidney Failure Risk Equation (`KFRE`, Tangri Formula — KDIGO 2024 Nephrology Referral & Fistula Planning Benchmark)
*Computes the 2-Year and 5-Year probability ($\%$) of progression to End-Stage Kidney Disease (`ESKD` requiring dialysis or transplantation) in patients with CKD Stages `G3a–G5` ($\text{eGFR} < 60\text{ mL/min/1.73 m}^2$):*
$$\text{Linear Predictor } (LP) = -0.2201 \times \left(\frac{\text{Age}}{10} - 7.036\right) + 0.2467 \times (\text{Male} - 0.5642) - 0.5567 \times \left(\frac{\text{eGFR}}{5} - 7.222\right) + 0.4510 \times \left(\ln[\text{UACR (mg/g)}] - 5.137\right)$$
$$\text{2-Year ESKD Risk (\%)} = \left[1 - 0.9832^{\exp(LP)}\right] \times 100 \quad ; \quad \text{5-Year ESKD Risk (\%)} = \left[1 - 0.9365^{\exp(LP)}\right] \times 100$$
- **KDIGO 2024 Clinical Action Thresholds:**
  - **5-Year `KFRE` $\ge 3\text{–}5\%$:** Mandatory specialist nephrology referral.
  - **2-Year `KFRE` $\ge 10\%$:** Initiate multidisciplinary CKD clinic care (anemia, CKD-MBD, metabolic acidosis, SGLT2i/finerenone optimization).
  - **2-Year `KFRE` $\ge 40\%$:** Initiate vascular access (`AV fistula`) creation, peritoneal dialysis planning, or preemptive transplant evaluation.

### 2.2 — Azotemia Stoichiometry, Fractional Excretion Indices (`FE_Na`, `FE_Urea`), Acid-Base (`Figge AG`, `Delta-Delta`) & Osmolality Equations

#### 1. `Blood Urea` $\leftrightarrow$ `Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN)` Stoichiometric Conversion
The molecular weight of Urea ($\text{CH}_4\text{N}_2\text{O}$) is $60.06\text{ g/mol}$, containing two Nitrogen atoms ($2 \times 14.007 = 28.014\text{ g/mol}$):
$$\frac{\text{MW}_{\text{Urea}}}{\text{MW}_{\text{2N}}} = \frac{60.06}{28.014} = \mathbf{2.1439} \approx \mathbf{2.14} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \text{Blood Urea (mg/dL)} = \text{BUN (mg/dL)} \times 2.1428 \quad ; \quad \text{BUN (mg/dL)} = \text{Blood Urea (mg/dL)} \times 0.4667$$

#### 2. Fractional Excretion of Sodium (`FE_Na`), Fractional Excretion of Urea (`FE_Urea`) & Renal Failure Index (`RFI`)
$$\text{FE}_{\text{Na}}\text{ (\%)} = \frac{U_{\text{Na}}\text{ (mmol/L)} \times S_{\text{cr}}\text{ (mg/dL)}}{S_{\text{Na}}\text{ (mmol/L)} \times U_{\text{cr}}\text{ (mg/dL)}} \times 100 \quad ; \quad \text{RFI} = \frac{U_{\text{Na}}\text{ (mmol/L)} \times S_{\text{cr}}\text{ (mg/dL)}}{U_{\text{cr}}\text{ (mg/dL)}}$$
$$\text{FE}_{\text{Urea}}\text{ (\%)} = \frac{U_{\text{Urea}}\text{ (mg/dL)} \times S_{\text{cr}}\text{ (mg/dL)}}{S_{\text{Urea}}\text{ (mg/dL)} \times U_{\text{cr}}\text{ (mg/dL)}} \times 100$$
- **Clinical Superiority of `FE_Urea` in Diuretic-Treated Patients:** Loop diuretics (`Furosemide`, `Torsemide`) and thiazides inhibit distal tubular $\text{Na}^+$ reabsorption, causing natriuresis ($\text{FE}_{\text{Na}} > 1\text{–}2\%$) even in severe Pre-Renal hypovolemia. Because proximal tubular urea reabsorption is driven by angiotensin II and ADH upstream of the loop of Henle/distal convoluted tubule, **$\text{FE}_{\text{Urea}} < 35\%$ remains diagnostic of Pre-Renal Azotemia even during active diuretic therapy** (whereas $\text{FE}_{\text{Urea}} > 50\%$ confirms Intrinsic `ATN`).

#### 3. Serum Anion Gap (`AG`), Figge Albumin-Corrected Anion Gap (`AG_corr`) & Delta-Delta Ratio ($\Delta\text{AG} / \Delta\text{HCO}_3^-$)
$$\text{Anion Gap (AG)} = \text{Na}^+ - \left(\text{Cl}^- + \text{HCO}_3^-\right) \quad [\text{Reference Interval: } 8 \text{ – } 16\text{ mmol/L; Mean } = 12\text{ mmol/L}]$$
$$\text{Figge Albumin-Corrected AG (AG}_{\text{corr}}\text{)} = \text{Measured AG} + 2.5 \times \left(4.0 - \text{Serum Albumin [g/dL]}\right)$$
*(Why Figge Correction is Critical in ICU: Every $1.0\text{ g/dL}$ fall in Serum Albumin below $4.0\text{ g/dL}$ reduces unmeasured negative protein charges by $2.5\text{ mmol/L}$. In a septic ICU patient with $\text{Albumin} = 1.6\text{ g/dL}$ and $\text{Measured AG} = 12\text{ mmol/L}$, $\text{AG}_{\text{corr}} = 12 + 2.5(2.4) = \mathbf{18.0\text{ mmol/L}}$ — unmasking an occult High Anion Gap Lactic Acidosis!)*

$$\text{Delta-Delta Ratio }\left(\frac{\Delta\text{AG}}{\Delta\text{HCO}_3^-}\right) = \frac{\text{AG}_{\text{corr}} - 12}{24 - \text{HCO}_3^-}$$
- **Interpretation of Delta-Delta Ratio in `HAGMA` ($\text{AG}_{\text{corr}} > 16\text{ mmol/L}$):**
  - **$< 0.4$:** Hyperchloremic Normal Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (`NAGMA`).
  - **$0.4 \text{ – } 0.8$:** Combined **High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (`HAGMA`) + Normal Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (`NAGMA`)** (e.g., DKA during rapid $0.9\%\text{ NaCl}$ resuscitation or diarrhea + lactic acidosis).
  - **$1.0 \text{ – } 2.0$:** Pure **Uncomplicated High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (`HAGMA`)** (Lactic acidosis typically averages $\approx 1.6$; DKA averages $\approx 1.0$ due to urinary ketone loss).
  - **$> 2.0$:** Combined **High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (`HAGMA`) + Concurrent Metabolic Alkalosis** (e.g., DKA/uremia with severe vomiting/NG suction) OR **Pre-existing Compensated Chronic Respiratory Acidosis** (`COPD` baseline $\text{HCO}_3^- > 30\text{ mmol/L}$).

#### 4. Urine Anion Gap (`UAG`) & Urine Osmolal Gap (`UOG`) for Differentiating `NAGMA` (`GI Loss` vs `Renal Tubular Acidosis`)
$$\text{Urine Anion Gap (UAG)} = \left(U_{\text{Na}} + U_{\text{K}}\right) - U_{\text{Cl}}$$
- **Negative `UAG` ($-20\text{ to }-50\text{ mmol/L}$, $U_{\text{Cl}} > U_{\text{Na}} + U_{\text{K}}$):** Intact renal distal acidification excreting abundant $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ (**"NeGUTive"** $\rightarrow$ Extra-renal GI bicarbonate loss / diarrhea / proximal RTA).
- **Positive `UAG` ($+20\text{ to }+50\text{ mmol/L}$, $U_{\text{Na}} + U_{\text{K}} > U_{\text{Cl}}$):** Impaired distal tubular $\text{NH}_4^+$ excretion $\rightarrow$ **Distal (`Type 1`) RTA** (Urine $\text{pH} > 5.5$, hypokalemia) or **Hyperkalemic (`Type 4`) Hypoaldosteronism RTA** (Urine $\text{pH} < 5.5$, hyperkalemia).
- **Urine Osmolal Gap (`UOG`, used when urinary $\text{HCO}_3^-$ or $\beta\text{-OHB}^-$ anions invalidate `UAG`):**
  $$\text{UOG} = \text{Urine Osm}_{\text{measured}} - \left[2 \times (U_{\text{Na}} + U_{\text{K}}) + \frac{U_{\text{Urea (mg/dL)}}}{6.0} + \frac{U_{\text{Glucose (mg/dL)}}}{18}\right] \quad \left(\text{Urinary } [\text{NH}_4^+] \approx \frac{\text{UOG}}{2}\right)$$
  - $\text{UOG} > 100\text{ mOsm/kg}$ ($\text{Urinary NH}_4^+ > 50\text{ mmol/L}$): Intact renal $\text{NH}_4^+$ excretion (GI diarrhea).
  - $\text{UOG} < 40\text{ mOsm/kg}$ ($\text{Urinary NH}_4^+ < 20\text{ mmol/L}$): Impaired distal acidification (**Type 1 or Type 4 RTA**).

#### 5. Hyperglycemic Sodium Correction (`Katz` & `Hillier`), Free Water Deficit, Plasma Water Fraction (`Waugh`) & Serum Osmolal Gap (`OG`)
- **Hyperglycemic Translocational Hyponatremia Correction (Extracellular hypertonicity draws intracellular water into plasma):**
  $$\text{Corrected Na}^+_{\text{Katz (Glucose } \le 400\text{ mg/dL)}} = \text{Measured Na}^+ + 0.016 \times \left(\text{Serum Glucose [mg/dL]} - 100\right)$$
  $$\text{Corrected Na}^+_{\text{Hillier (Glucose } > 400\text{ mg/dL)}} = \text{Measured Na}^+ + 0.024 \times \left(\text{Serum Glucose [mg/dL]} - 100\right)$$
- **Free Water Deficit (`FWD`) in Hypernatremia ($\text{Na}^+ > 145\text{ mmol/L}$) & Safe Rate of Correction ($\le 8\text{–}10\text{ mmol/L per 24h}$):**
  $$\text{Free Water Deficit (Liters)} = \text{TBW} \times \left(\frac{\text{Serum Na}^+}{140} - 1\right) \quad [\text{where } \text{TBW} = \text{Weight (kg)} \times 0.60\text{ (Male), } 0.50\text{ (Female/Elderly)}]$$
- **Waugh Plasma Water Fraction (`W_p`) & Indirect ISE Exclusion Error Estimation:**
  $$W_p\text{ (\%)} = 99.1 - (0.73 \times \text{Total Protein [g/dL]}) - (0.103 \times \text{Total Lipids [g/dL]}) \quad ; \quad \text{Na}^+_{\text{Direct ISE}} \approx \text{Na}^+_{\text{Indirect ISE}} \times \frac{93.0}{W_p\text{ (\%)}} $$
- **Calculated Serum Osmolality (`Worthley Equation`) & Serum Osmolal Gap (`OG`):**
  $$\text{Osm}_{\text{calc}}\text{ (mOsm/kg)} = 2 \times \text{Na}^+\text{ (mmol/L)} + \frac{\text{Glucose (mg/dL)}}{18.0} + \frac{\text{BUN (mg/dL)}}{2.8} \quad \left[\text{or } + \frac{\text{Blood Urea (mg/dL)}}{6.0}\right]$$
  $$\text{Osmolal Gap (OG)} = \text{Osm}_{\text{measured (Freezing Point)}} - \text{Osm}_{\text{calc}} \quad [\text{Normal: } -10 \text{ to } +10\text{ mOsm/kg; Critical Toxic Alert: } > 15\text{ mOsm/kg}]$$
  - **Estimating Toxic Alcohol / Ethanol Concentration ($\text{mg/dL}$) from Osmolal Gap:**
    - **Ethanol ($\text{MW} = 46\text{ g/mol}$):** $\Delta\text{Osm} = \frac{[\text{Ethanol (mg/dL)}]}{4.6}$ (or $3.7$ empirically due to non-ideal osmotic behavior at high levels).
    - **Methanol ($\text{MW} = 32\text{ g/mol}$):** $\Delta\text{Osm} = \frac{[\text{Methanol (mg/dL)}]}{3.2}$ ($50\text{ mg/dL}$ Methanol increases `OG` by $+15.6\text{ mOsm/kg}$).
    - **Ethylene Glycol ($\text{MW} = 62\text{ g/mol}$):** $\Delta\text{Osm} = \frac{[\text{Ethylene Glycol (mg/dL)}]}{6.2}$ ($50\text{ mg/dL}$ Ethylene Glycol increases `OG` by $+8.1\text{ mOsm/kg}$).
    - **Isopropanol ($\text{MW} = 60\text{ g/mol}$):** $\Delta\text{Osm} = \frac{[\text{Isopropanol (mg/dL)}]}{6.0}$ (Causes elevated `OG` + Acetone ketonemia **without** metabolic acidosis!).

## 🔬 Section 3: Analytical Chemistry, Electrochemistry (`Direct` vs `Indirect ISE`), Method Comparison & Drug-Laboratory Interference Matrices

### 3.1 — Direct ISE (**Medica EasyLyte**) vs Indirect ISE (**Bonavera Chem 400**) Electrochemistry & Plasma Water Exclusion Protocol (`CLSI C29-A2`)

#### 1. Potentiometric Nernstian Physics
Both Direct and Indirect Ion-Selective Electrode (`ISE`) modules measure the electrical potential difference ($E$, in millivolts) developed across a selective membrane between the sample test solution and an internal filling solution of fixed ion activity, referenced against a stable $\text{Ag/AgCl}$ reference electrode with a saturated $\text{KCl}$ liquid junction:
$$E = E_0 + \frac{2.303 \cdot R \cdot T}{z \cdot F} \log_{10}\left(a_i\right) = E_0 + S \cdot \log_{10}\left(\gamma_i \cdot c_i\right)$$
where $R = 8.314\text{ J/(mol}\cdot\text{K)}$, $T = 310.15\text{ K}$ ($37^\circ\text{C}$), $F = 96,485\text{ C/mol}$, $z = \pm 1$ (valency for $\text{Na}^+, \text{K}^+, \text{Cl}^-$), yielding a theoretical **Nernstian Slope $S = 61.5\text{ mV/decade}$ at $37^\circ\text{C}$** ($59.16\text{ mV/decade}$ at $25^\circ\text{C}$). Note that an ISE responds strictly to **ion activity ($a_i = \gamma_i \cdot m_i$) in the aqueous phase (molality, $\text{mmol/kg H}_2\text{O}$)**, which is converted to molar concentration ($\text{mmol/L}$ of normal plasma) by calibrating against **NIST SRM 956d** human serum standards (`CLSI C29-A2`).

### 3.5 — Method Verification (`CLSI EP15-A3`, `EP06-Ed2`, `EP17-A2`, `EP28-A3c`), Top-Down `ISO/TS 20914:2019` Measurement Uncertainty & Westgard Six-Sigma QC Governance (`NABL 129 / 131 / 163`)

1. **`CLSI EP15-A3` ($5 \times 5$ Precision & Trueness Verification):**
   - Prior to placing any new reagent lot or recalibrated channel into service on **Bonavera Chem 400**, **Bonavera Chem 200**, or **Medica EasyLyte**, $5$ replicates per day across $5$ consecutive days ($N = 25$ per level at Level 1 Normal and Level 2/3 Pathological) are analyzed using One-Way ANOVA to verify Repeatability ($s_r \le \text{Manufacturer Claim}$ or Upper Verification Limit $\text{UVL}_r$) and Within-Laboratory Intermediate Precision ($s_{\text{WL}} \le 0.5 \times CV_I$).
2. **`CLSI EP06-Ed2` (Polynomial Linearity & $1:10$ Auto-Dilution Verification):**
   - Verified semi-annually using $5\text{ to }7$ equidistant linearity levels spanning the full `AMR`. Nonlinearity deviation must remain $\le 0.5 \times \text{TE}_a$ at all points. Maximum validated auto-dilution (`CRR`) is $1:10$ with $0.9\%\text{ NaCl}$ (for photometric assays) or manufacturer Urine Diluent (for ISE urine assays), achieving $95\%\text{–}105\%$ recovery.
3. **`CLSI EP17-A2` (`LoB`, `LoD`, and Functional `LoQ` Verification):**
   - Functional Limit of Quantitation (`LoQ`) is verified at total $\text{CV} \le 10\%$ (specifically $\text{LoQ} \le 0.10\text{ mg/dL}$ for Enzymatic Creatinine to guarantee neonatal accuracy, $\le 0.20\text{ mg/L}$ for Cystatin C, and $\le 2.0\text{ mg/dL}$ for Urine Protein).
4. **Top-Down `ISO/TS 20914:2019` & `NABL 129` Measurement Uncertainty (`MU`) Calculation:**
   $$u_c(y) = \sqrt{u_{\text{rw}}^2 + u_{\text{cal}}^2 + u_{\text{bias}}^2} \quad ; \quad U = k \times u_c(y) \quad (\text{where } k = 2 \text{ for } 95.45\%\text{ confidence})$$
   - $u_{\text{rw}}$: Long-term within-laboratory intermediate imprecision from $\ge 6\text{ months}$ ($N \ge 180$ data points across reagent/calibrator lots) of third-party IQC.
   - $u_{\text{cal}}$: Calibrator uncertainty provided on the manufacturer's `ISO 17511` Traceability Certificate ($U_{\text{cal}} / 2$).
   - $u_{\text{bias}}$: Root-mean-square bias from $\ge 6\text{–}12$ monthly **CMC Vellore / Bio-Rad EQAS** challenges ($\sqrt{\text{Bias}_{\text{mean}}^2 + (s_{\text{EQAS}}/\sqrt{n})^2}$).

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Pre-Renal Azotemia**<br>*(Hypovolemia, GI Bleeding, Heart Failure, Early Sepsis)* | **$> 20 : 1$**<br>($> 40 : 1$) | $U_{\text{Na}} < 20\text{ mmol/L}$<br>$U_{\text{Osm}} > 500\text{ mOsm/kg}$<br>*(Specific Gravity $> 1.020$)* | **`FE_Na` $< 1.0\%$**<br>**`FE_Urea` $< 35\%$**<br>*(If on Furosemide: `FE_Na` may be $> 1\%$, but **`FE_Urea` stays $< 35\%$**!)* | Bland sediment (occasional transparent **hyaline casts**);<br>`UPCR` $< 0.20\text{ g/g}$ | **`NGAL` $< 100\text{ ng/mL}$**<br>(Intact tubular epithelium) | Intact tubules avidly reabsorb $\text{Na}^+$, water, and urea in response to RAAS, sympathetic tone, and ADH.<br>**Action:** Isotonic crystalloid resuscitation (or inotrope/diuretic optimization in Cardiorenal Syndrome). |

| **4. ### 4.5 — Integrated Dysnatremia ($\text{Na}^+$), Dyskalemia ($\text{K}^+$) & Acid-Base Diagnostic Algorithm Matrix

| Electrolyte / Acid-Base Disorder | Primary Biochemical Gate | Sub-Classification by Urine Chemistry (`Urine Osmolality`, `Urine Na+`, `Urine K+`, `TTKG`, `UAG`) | Definitive Etiologies & Therapeutic Safety Guardrails |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hyponatremia**<br>($\text{Na}^+ < 135\text{ mmol/L}$) | **Step 1:** Exclude **Pseudohyponatremia** (`Direct ISE`, `Osm = 275–295 mOsm/kg`) & **Hypertonic Translocational Hyponatremia** (`Osm > 295`, Hyperglycemia / Mannitol).<br>**Step 2:** Confirm **True Hypotonic Hyponatremia (`Serum Osm < 275 mOsm/kg`)**. | • **Urine Osm $< 100\text{ mOsm/kg}$ (ADH Appropriately Suppressed):** Primary Polydipsia, Beer Potomania, "Tea & Toast" low-solute diet.<br>• **Urine Osm $> 100\text{ mOsm/kg}$ + `Urine Na+ < 20 mmol/L`:**<br>  - *Hypovolemic:* Vomiting, diarrhea, third-spacing, burns.<br>  - *Hypervolemic:* Congestive Heart Failure, Cirrhosis, Nephrotic Syndrome.<br>• **Urine Osm $> 100\text{ mOsm/kg}$ + `Urine Na+ > 30 mmol/L`:**<br>  - *Euvolemic:* **SIADH** (check low Serum Uric Acid $< 4.0\text{ mg/dL}$ & `FE_UA` $> 12\%$), Secondary Adrenal Insufficiency (Cortisol), Severe Hypothyroidism.<br>  - *Hypovolemic:* Diuretics (Thiazides), Primary Adrenal Insufficiency (Addison's), Cerebral Salt Wasting (`CSW`), Salt-Losing Nephropathy. | **CRITICAL SAFETY GUARDRAIL (`ODS Prevention`):**<br>In chronic hyponatremia ($> 48\text{h}$), **NEVER correct Serum $\text{Na}^+$ by $> 8\text{ mmol/L}$ in any $24\text{h}$ period** ($\le 6\text{ mmol/L/24h}$ in cirrhosis, alcoholism, hypokalemia, or malnutrition) to prevent catastrophic **Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (`ODS`)**. Monitor $\text{Na}^+$ every $2\text{–}4\text{h}$ during active correction. |
| **Hypernatremia**<br>($\text{Na}^+ > 145\text{ mmol/L}$, always Hypertonic $\text{Osm} > 295\text{ mOsm/kg}$) | Evaluate Urine Osmolality (`2.3.8`) and response to Desmopressin (`DDAVP`). | • **Urine Osm $> 600\text{ mOsm/kg}$ (Intact ADH & Renal Concentrating Axis):** Extra-renal hypotonic water loss (insensible sweat/fever/tachypnea, osmotic diarrhea/lactulose) or hypertonic $\text{NaHCO}_3/\text{NaCl}$ overload.<br>• **Urine Osm $< 300\text{ mOsm/kg}$ (`Diabetes Insipidus`):**<br>  - **Central DI (AVP Deficiency):** Urine Osm rises $> 50\%$ after DDAVP; plasma **Copeptin** $< 4.9\text{ pmol/L}$.<br>  - **Nephrogenic DI (AVP Resistance):** $< 10\%$ rise after DDAVP; plasma **Copeptin** $> 21.4\text{ pmol/L}$ (Lithium toxicity, Hypercalcemia, Hypokalemia, congenital `AVPR2`/`AQP2`). | Calculate **Free Water Deficit** (Section 2.2) and replace over $48\text{–}72\text{h}$, lowering $\text{Na}^+$ by **$\le 8\text{–}10\text{ mmol/L per 24h}$** (rapid lowering causes cerebral edema and herniation). |
| **Hypokalemia**<br>($\text{K}^+ < 3.5\text{ mmol/L}$) | Measure **Spot Urine $\text{K}^+ : \text{Creatinine}$ Ratio** (or 24h Urine $\text{K}^+$) + Serum $\text{HCO}_3^-$ + Blood Pressure + **Serum Magnesium (`2.5.4`)**. | • **Renal $\text{K}^+$ Conservation ($\text{Spot Urine K:Cr} < 13\text{ mmol/g}$ or $24\text{h } U_{\text{K}} < 15\text{ mmol/d}$):** Extra-renal GI loss (diarrhea, laxative abuse, villous adenoma) or transcellular shift (insulin, $\beta_2$-agonists, thyrotoxic periodic paralysis, alkalosis).<br>• **Renal $\text{K}^+$ Wasting ($\text{Spot Urine K:Cr} > 13\text{ mmol/g}$ or $24\text{h } U_{\text{K}} > 20\text{ mmol/d}$):**<br>  - *With Metabolic Acidosis ($\downarrow \text{HCO}_3^-$):* **Distal (`Type 1`) or Proximal (`Type 2`) RTA**, DKA, Amphotericin B.<br>  - *With Metabolic Alkalosis ($\uparrow \text{HCO}_3^-$) & Normal/Low BP:* Vomiting (`Urine Cl- < 20`), Loop/Thiazide Diuretics, **Bartter Syndrome**, **Gitelman Syndrome** (with hypomagnesemia & hypocalciuria).<br>  - *With Metabolic Alkalosis ($\uparrow \text{HCO}_3^-$) & Hypertension:* **Primary Hyperaldosteronism (`Conn's`)**, Renovascular HTN, Cushing's, Liddle Syndrome, Licorice. | **CRITICAL SAFETY GUARDRAIL:**<br>1. Always check and replete **Serum Magnesium (`2.5.4`)** concurrently; hypomagnesemia releases inhibition of apical `ROMK` channels, rendering hypokalemia completely refractory to $\text{KCl}$ supplementation!<br>2. Maximum peripheral IV $\text{KCl}$ infusion rate: $\le 10\text{ mmol/hr}$ ($\le 20\text{ mmol/hr}$ via central line with continuous ECG monitoring). |
| **Hyperkalemia**<br>($\text{K}^+ > 5.1\text{ mmol/L}$) | **Step 1:** Exclude **Pseudohyperkalemia** (`H-Index < 50`, $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{ALP}$ normal, repeat on **Lithium Heparin Plasma** if $\text{PLT} > 500 \times 10^9/\text{L}$ or $\text{WBC} > 50 \times 10^9/\text{L}$).<br>**Step 2:** Perform urgent **12-Lead ECG** (peaked T waves $\rightarrow$ prolonged PR/widened QRS $\rightarrow$ sine-wave VF). | • **Transcellular Shift (Intact GFR):** Acidemia (inorganic metabolic acidosis shifts $+0.6\text{ mmol/L K}^+$ per $-0.1\text{ pH}$ drop), insulin deficiency/DKA, Rhabdomyolysis, Tumor Lysis Syndrome, Digoxin toxicity, Succinylcholine, $\beta$-blockers.<br>• **Impaired Renal $\text{K}^+$ Excretion ($\text{Urine K}^+ < 40\text{ mmol/L}$):**<br>  - *Severely Reduced GFR:* AKI Stage 2–3, CKD Stage G4–G5 (`eGFR < 20`).<br>  - *Hyporeninemic Hypoaldosteronism (`Type 4 RTA`) / Aldosterone Blockade:* Diabetic nephropathy, ACEi/ARB, Spironolactone/Eplerenone/Finerenone, Trimethoprim/Amiloride/Pentamidine (`ENaC` blockers), Calcineurin inhibitors (`Tacrolimus`), Addison's Disease. | **3-Tier Emergency Hyperkalemia Protocol ($\text{True K}^+ > 6.0\text{ mmol/L}$ or ECG changes):**<br>1. **Membrane Stabilization ($1\text{–}3\text{ min}$):** IV $10\%\text{ Calcium Gluconate}$ ($10\text{ mL}$ over $2\text{–}3\text{ min}$).<br>2. **Intracellular Shift ($15\text{–}30\text{ min}$):** IV Regular Insulin ($10\text{ U}$) + $25\text{–}50\text{ g}$ Dextrose + Nebulized Salbutamol ($10\text{–}20\text{ mg}$).<br>3. **Total Body Removal:** Loop diuretic, Sodium Zirconium Cyclosilicate / Patiromer, or urgent **Hemodialysis**. |
