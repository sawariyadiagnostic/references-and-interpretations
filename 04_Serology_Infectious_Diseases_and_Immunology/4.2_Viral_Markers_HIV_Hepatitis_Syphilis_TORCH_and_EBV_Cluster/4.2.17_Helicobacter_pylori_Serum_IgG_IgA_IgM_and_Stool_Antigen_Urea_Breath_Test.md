## 🔬 Section 2: Clinical Pathogenesis, Virulence Factors & Diagnostic Modalities Compared

### 2.1 Virulence Factor Architecture & Carcinogenic Pathophysiology
*Helicobacter pylori* is a microaerophilic, flagellated, Gram-negative spiral bacterium classified by the WHO/IARC as a **Group 1 Definite Human Carcinogen**. It colonizes the gastric epithelial mucous layer of approximately 60%–80% of the adult population in India and developing nations, leading to lifelong chronic active gastritis, peptic ulcer disease, gastric adenocarcinoma, and gastric mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                            VIRULENCE FACTOR ARCHITECTURE OF H. PYLORI                             |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. UREASE ENZYME COMPLEX    : Multi-subunit nickel metalloenzyme (UreA/UreB) constituting ~10% of |
|                               total bacterial protein. Hydrolyzes urea -> 2 NH3 + CO2; ammonia   |
|                               neutralizes gastric acid (pH 1.5 -> 6.5) creating a protective cloud.|
| 2. UNIPOLAR FLAGELLA (4-8)  : Sheathed flagella provide high-torque corkscrew motility, allowing  |
|                               rapid swimming across acidic gastric juice into neutral mucous gel. |
| 3. ADHESINS (BabA & SabA)   : BabA binds Lewis b (Leb) blood group antigens; SabA binds sialyl-   |
|                               Lewis x (sLex) receptors on inflamed gastric surface epithelial cells.|
| 4. CagA ONCOPROTEIN (140kD) : Cytotoxin-associated gene A delivered into host cells via a Type IV |
|                               Secretion System (T4SS); tyrosine phosphorylated at EPIYA motifs by |
|                               Src/Abl kinases; activates SHP-2, triggers "hummingbird" phenotype,  |
|                               stimulates massive IL-8 release, and drives gastric carcinogenesis.  |
| 5. VacA CYTOTOXIN (88kD)    : Vacuolating cytotoxin forming hexameric chloride-permeable pores;   |
|                               induces endosomal vacuolation, mitochondrial cytochrome c leakage,  |
|                               apoptosis of epithelial cells, and impairs CD4+ T-cell proliferation.|
| 6. OipA / DupA / IceA       : Outer inflammatory protein A and duodenal ulcer promoting gene A    |
|                               modulate mucosal cytokine cascades and enhance ulcerogenic risk.    |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 2.2 CagA EPIYA Motifs, VacA Allelic Polymorphism & Host Signaling
- **CagA Oncoprotein Variants:** The intracellular biological activity of CagA depends on the number and structure of its C-terminal Glu-Pro-Ile-Tyr-Ala (EPIYA) tyrosine phosphorylation motifs:
  - **Western-type CagA:** Features EPIYA-A, EPIYA-B, and variable repetitions of EPIYA-C motifs (A-B-C or A-B-C-C). The number of EPIYA-C repeats directly correlates with the degree of SHP-2 phosphatase binding, cytoskeletal rearrangement, and gastric cancer risk.
  - **East Asian-type CagA:** Features an EPIYA-D motif instead of EPIYA-C. EPIYA-D displays significantly higher binding affinity for SHP-2, explaining the markedly elevated incidence of gastric adenocarcinoma in East Asian populations compared to Western cohorts.
- **VacA Allelic Polymorphism:** The *vacA* gene exhibits structural heterogeneity across three primary regions:
  - Signal region: Alleles $s1$ ($s1a, s1b, s1c$) or $s2$. The $s1$ allele confers full cytotoxin secretion, while $s2$ encodes a hydrophilic N-terminal extension that blocks pore formation.
  - Intermediate region: Alleles $i1$ or $i2$. The $i1$ allele exhibits potent vacuolating activity.
  - Middle region: Alleles $m1$ or $m2$. The $m1$ allele induces extensive epithelial cell vacuolation across multiple cell lines.
  - Strains harboring the $s1/i1/m1$ genotype produce maximum cytotoxic activity and are strongly associated with peptic ulcer disease and gastric adenocarcinoma.

### 2.3 Correa's Cascade of Gastric Carcinogenesis
Infection initiates an inflammatory cascade that progresses over decades:
$$\text{Normal Mucosa} \longrightarrow \text{Chronic Active Gastritis} \longrightarrow \text{Multifocal Atrophic Gastritis} \longrightarrow \text{Intestinal Metaplasia} \longrightarrow \text{Dysplasia} \longrightarrow \text{Gastric Adenocarcinoma}$$
Eradication of *H. pylori* before the development of irreversible intestinal metaplasia halts this progression and significantly reduces lifetime gastric cancer risk.

### 2.4 Gastric Acid Alteration & Distinct Clinical Phenotypes
Depending on host genetics (e.g., IL-1$\beta$ polymorphisms) and environmental cofactors (dietary salt, smoking), *H. pylori* produces two distinct anatomical and physiological phenotypes:
1. **Antral-Predominant Gastritis (Hyperchlorhydric Phenotype):** Inflammation is concentrated in the non-acid-secreting antrum. Somatostatin-producing D-cells are damaged, relieving inhibitory control on gastrin-producing G-cells. Unchecked hypergastrinemia stimulates parietal cells in the intact corpus, driving gastric acid hypersecretion. The high acid load damages the duodenal bulb, inducing gastric metaplasia of duodenal epithelium, colonization by *H. pylori*, and **Duodenal Ulcer Disease** (protective against gastric cancer).
2. **Corpus-Predominant Gastritis / Pangastritis (Hypochlorhydric Phenotype):** Massive chronic inflammation invades the acid-secreting oxyntic corpus mucosa. Parietal cells undergo progressive apoptosis and atrophy, causing severe **Hypochlorhydria or Achlorhydria**. The loss of acid barrier permits secondary intragastric overgrowth of anaerobic bacteria that convert dietary nitrates into carcinogenic N-nitroso compounds, driving **Gastric Ulcer Disease and Intestinal-type Gastric Adenocarcinoma**.

### 2.6 The "Serological Scar" and the Test-of-Cure Fallacy
Following successful antibiotic eradication of *H. pylori*, circulating serum IgG antibodies decline very slowly due to long-lived memory plasma cell survival:
- At 3 months post-cure: 90%–95% of successfully treated patients remain seropositive.
- At 6 months post-cure: 60%–75% of cured individuals remain seropositive.
- At 12 months post-cure: Up to 50% remain seropositive with demonstrable antibody titers ("serological scar").
- **Clinical Governance Rule:** Re-testing serum IgG within 12 months of eradication therapy is a diagnostic error under `Maastricht VI` and `ACG 2024` guidelines. Clinicians must never order serology to document cure; doing so triggers unwarranted repeated cycles of antibiotic regimens, precipitating gut dysbiosis and antimicrobial resistance.

### 2.7 Strict Pre-Analytical Drug Washout Gates (Maastricht VI Consensus)
Gastric acid suppression and antimicrobial agents drastically reduce *H. pylori* density and down-regulate bacterial urease expression, translocating bacteria from the gastric antrum to the corpus and causing devastating false-negative Stool Antigen and UBT results. The following mandatory washout gates are enforced at Sawariya Diagnostic Lab:

## 🧪 Section 3: Pre-Analytical Phlebotomy, Specimen Integrity & Analytical SOPs

### 3.1 Specimen Collection, Handling & Quantitative HIL Rejection Criteria
- **Serum (CLIA):** Collect 4.0 mL venous blood into Gold-top SST (clot activator + gel separator). Allow 30 min clotting at room temperature ($20\text{–}25^\circ\text{C}$). Centrifuge at $1,800 \times g$ for 10 min. Serum stability: 48 hours at $20\text{–}25^\circ\text{C}$, 14 days at $2\text{–}8^\circ\text{C}$, 12 months at $-20^\circ\text{C}$. Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles.
- **Fecal Antigen (SAT):** Collect 1.0–2.0 g fresh formed or semi-formed stool into a clean, sterile, preservative-free wide-mouth specimen cup. Stability: 24 hours at $20\text{–}25^\circ\text{C}$, 72 hours at $2\text{–}8^\circ\text{C}$, 30 days at $-20^\circ\text{C}$.
- **Rejection Criteria:**
  - Watery, liquid diarrheal stool (causes significant dilution below assay LoD; reject and request formed specimen).
  - Stool preserved in formalin, SAF, PVA, Cary-Blair, or transport media with chemical fixatives (destroys native protein epitope).
  - Serum with severe hemolysis (Hemolysis index $> 500\text{ mg/dL}$), gross icterus ($> 30\text{ mg/dL}$), or severe lipemia ($> 1,500\text{ mg/dL}$).
  - Breath bag collected without documented 6-hour fasting or without 14-day PPI / 28-day antibiotic washout verification.

### 3.2 Analytical SOP 1: Snibe Maglumi X3 Flash-CLIA for Serum IgG/IgA/IgM
1. **Principle:** Indirect two-step chemiluminescence immunoassay. Magnetic micro-beads coated with purified recombinant *H. pylori* CagA, VacA, and Urease antigens capture specific antibodies in patient serum ($40\ \mu\text{L}$).
2. **Wash Cycle:** Following 15-minute incubation at $37^\circ\text{C}$, a magnetic field retains the bead-antibody complexes; unbound serum components are washed away with automated buffer cycles.
3. **Tracer Binding:** An ABEI (N-(4-aminobutyl)-N-ethylisoluminol)-labeled anti-human IgG (or IgA/IgM) monoclonal conjugate is added, forming a sandwich on the microbead surface.
4. **Signal Generation:** After secondary washing, Starter Reagents 1 and 2 ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ and $\text{NaOH}$) are automatically injected into the cuvette, triggering a high-intensity flash chemiluminescent light reaction at $\lambda = 425\text{ nm}$.
5. **Signal Quantitation:** Emitted Relative Light Units (RLUs) are measured by a built-in photomultiplier tube (PMT) and converted into quantitative units (AU/mL) via a 2-point master calibration curve.

### 3.3 Analytical SOP 2: Monoclonal Enzyme/Chemiluminescent Stool Antigen (SAT)
1. **Specimen Extraction:** Dispense precisely $1.0\text{ mL}$ of specimen extraction buffer into an extraction vial. Using the grooved applicator stick, sample approximately $100\text{ mg}$ of formed stool from 3 distinct areas. Vortex vigorously for 30 seconds to produce a homogenous suspension; let stand for 5 minutes.
2. **Pipetting & Incubation:** Transfer $100\ \mu\text{L}$ of clarified stool supernatant into microwells coated with high-affinity monoclonal anti-*H. pylori* antibody. Add $50\ \mu\text{L}$ of enzyme-conjugated secondary monoclonal antibody (HRP-labeled).
3. **Sandwich Formation:** Incubate at $37^\circ\text{C}$ for 60 minutes. Wash wells 5 times with $350\ \mu\text{L}$ wash buffer to remove unbound stool debris and non-specific proteins.
4. **Color Development:** Add $100\ \mu\text{L}$ of TMB substrate ($3,3',5,5'$-tetramethylbenzidine). Incubate in the dark at room temperature for 15 minutes. Add $100\ \mu\text{L}$ of $1\text{ N } \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ stop solution. Measure optical density at $450/630\text{ nm}$ on microplate photometer. Calculate Cutoff Index: $\text{Index} = \text{Sample OD} / \text{Cutoff Value}$.

### 3.4 Analytical SOP 3: $^{13}\text{C}$-Urea Breath Test (UBT) Standardized Procedure
1. **Patient Preparation:** Verify strict $\ge 6\text{ hours}$ fasting (nil per oral except sips of plain water). Confirm 14-day PPI and 28-day antibiotic washout.
2. **Baseline Breath Sample ($T_0$):** Have the patient breathe normally, hold breath for 5 seconds, and exhale completely into the baseline breath collection bag/tube ($T_0$) through an exhalation straw until the container is fully inflated. Seal hermetically.
3. **Substrate Administration:** Dissolve $75\text{ mg}$ of certified $^{13}\text{C}$-enriched urea powder ($> 99\text{ atom } \% \ ^{13}\text{C}$) in $50\text{ mL}$ of water containing $1.4\text{ g}$ of citric acid meal (citric acid slows gastric emptying, keeps the urea in contact with the mucosal lining, and lowers gastric pH to optimize urease activity). Administer orally to the patient immediately.
4. **Resting Phase:** Patient sits quietly for exactly 20 minutes (or 30 minutes depending on manufacturer protocol). Eating, drinking, smoking, or excessive speaking during this interval is strictly prohibited.
5. **Post-Dose Breath Sample ($T_{20}$ or $T_{30}$):** Exhale into the second breath collection bag/tube ($T_{\text{post}}$) in identical fashion.
6. **Spectrometric Analysis:** Analyze both bags on the BreathID or IRIS NDIR spectrometer. The instrument measures the ratio of $^{13}\text{CO}_2$ to $^{12}\text{CO}_2$ in both samples and computes the Delta Over Baseline:
$$\text{DOB } (‰) = \left( \frac{[^{13}\text{CO}_2 / ^{12}\text{CO}_2]_{\text{post}} - [^{13}\text{CO}_2 / ^{12}\text{CO}_2]_{\text{baseline}}}{[^{13}\text{CO}_2 / ^{12}\text{CO}_2]_{\text{standard}}} \right) \times 1000$$
Cutoff: $\text{DOB} \ge 3.0\ ‰$ is Positive for active *H. pylori* urease; $\text{DOB} < 2.5\ ‰$ is Negative; $2.5\text{–}2.9\ ‰$ is Equivocal/Borderline.

### 3.5 Daily IQC, Westgard Multi-Rule Evaluation & Corrective Action Matrix
- **Daily Control Schedule:** Prior to releasing patient results, run two levels of liquid stable control material (Level 1: Negative / Normal; Level 2: Positive / Elevated) on the Maglumi X3 and ELISA platforms.
- **Westgard Rules Evaluated:**
  - $1_{3s}$ Rule: A single control measurement exceeds $\pm 3\ \text{SD}$. Indicates random error; immediately reject the analytical run.
  - $2_{2s}$ Rule: Two consecutive control measurements exceed $+2\ \text{SD}$ or $-2\ \text{SD}$. Indicates systematic error; halt run, perform recalibration.
  - $R_{4s}$ Rule: One control exceeds $+2\ \text{SD}$ and the other exceeds $-2\ \text{SD}$ within the same run. Indicates severe random error; reject run.
  - $4_{1s}$ Rule: Four consecutive control measurements exceed $+1\ \text{SD}$ or $-1\ \text{SD}$. Indicates systematic drift; perform maintenance and recalibration.
  - $10_x$ Rule: Ten consecutive control measurements fall on one side of the mean. Indicates reagent or calibration bias; investigate reagent lot.

### 5.5 Critical Panic Values & Urgent Action Thresholds
- **Tier-1 Critical Panic High (Telephone Read-Back $\le 30\text{ minutes}$):**
  - Identification of active *H. pylori* (Positive SAT $\ge 1.10\text{ Index}$ or UBT $\ge 3.0\ ‰$) in patients with:
    1. Active or recent Peptic Ulcer Hemorrhage (preventing life-threatening re-bleeding).
    2. Gastric MALT Lymphoma (eradication induces complete pathological lymphoma remission in $> 75\%$ of early-stage cases!).
    3. Early Gastric Cancer post-endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD/EMR) (prevents metachronous recurrence).
- **Tier-2 Urgent Clinical Action Range (Notification $< 2\text{ hours}$):**
  - Persistent positive Stool Antigen or UBT at $\ge 4\text{ weeks}$ post-eradication (indicates first-line antibiotic failure requiring rescue salvage regimen).

### 5.6 Special Population Considerations & Physiological Modifiers
- **Pediatric Patients (< 18 Years):** Non-invasive testing in children should strictly use Monoclonal Stool Antigen or $^{13}\text{C}$-UBT. Serology has poor diagnostic accuracy in young children due to underdeveloped humoral memory and low antibody avidity. "Test-and-treat" strategy is NOT recommended in children; diagnosis requires endoscopic biopsy evidence of gastritis or ulceration unless investigating refractory iron deficiency anemia or immune thrombocytopenia (ITP).
- **Pregnancy & Lactation:** $^{13}\text{C}$-UBT uses a non-radioactive, naturally occurring stable carbon isotope and is **100% safe in pregnancy and lactation**. In contrast, $^{14}\text{C}$-UBT uses radioactive carbon-14 ($t_{1/2} = 5,730\text{ years}$) and is **strictly contraindicated in pregnant and lactating women and children**. Monoclonal Stool Antigen is also completely safe during pregnancy. Eradication antibiotics are generally deferred until after delivery and breastfeeding unless active bleeding ulcers are present.
- **Geriatric Patients ($\ge 65\text{ Years}$):** High prevalence of extensive multifocal atrophic gastritis and intestinal metaplasia creates a hostile hypochlorhydric gastric environment, drastically reducing bacterial load. This leads to higher rates of false-negative UBT and biopsy urease tests. Monoclonal Stool Antigen retains superior diagnostic sensitivity in elderly patients.

## 🩺 Section 6: Six Real-World Worked Clinical Scenarios

### Case 1: Post-Treatment Test-of-Cure Fallacy Intercepted
- **Clinical Presentation:** A 44-year-old male from Charkhi Dadri completed 14-day Bismuth Quadruple therapy for an endoscopically confirmed bleeding duodenal ulcer. Four weeks later, his general practitioner ordered "Serum *H. pylori* IgG" to confirm eradication.
- **Laboratory Result:** Serum *H. pylori* IgG CLIA was reported as **Reactive (88.4 AU/mL; Index 6.8)**.
- **Pathologist Intervention:** The duty pathologist contacted the clinician immediately to halt an unnecessary second course of quadruple antibiotics. The pathologist explained that serum IgG antibodies remain elevated as a "serological scar" for 12–24 months post-cure.
- **Reflex Testing:** A Monoclonal *H. pylori* Stool Antigen was ordered after confirming the patient was off pantoprazole for 15 days.
- **Outcome:** Stool Antigen resulted **Negative (0.12 Index)**, confirming complete bacterial eradication. Patient spared severe drug toxicity and antimicrobial resistance.

### Case 2: PPI-Induced False-Negative Stool Antigen in Chronic Dyspepsia
- **Clinical Presentation:** A 38-year-old female schoolteacher presented with persistent epigastric burning, nausea, and postprandial fullness for 6 months. She had been taking OTC Pantoprazole $40\text{ mg}$ daily for 3 months.
- **Initial Test:** Stool Antigen performed at an outside facility while taking daily pantoprazole was reported as **Negative (0.42 Index)**. Clinician labeled her as "functional dyspepsia."
- **Lab Review & Re-Testing:** On consultation at Sawariya Diagnostic Lab, the pathologist recognized the PPI confounding effect. The patient was placed on a 14-day PPI washout, using Famotidine $20\text{ mg}$ BID for 12 days and stopping famotidine 48 hours prior to re-testing.
- **Repeat Result:** Monoclonal Stool Antigen resulted **Strongly Positive (4.65 Index)**.
- **Outcome:** 14-day Bismuth Quadruple therapy initiated; complete symptom resolution achieved; test-of-cure at 6 weeks confirmed negative status.

### Case 3: Acute Upper GI Hemorrhage & Strategic Serology Use
- **Clinical Presentation:** A 66-year-old male presented to the emergency department with hematemesis, melena, and hemodynamic instability (BP 85/50 mmHg, Hb 6.8 g/dL). Urgent endoscopy showed a bleeding Forrest IIa gastric antral ulcer. Patient was started on continuous IV Pantoprazole infusion ($8\text{ mg/hr}$).
- **Diagnostic Dilemma:** Rapid Urease Test (RUT) on antral biopsy was negative (blood in the stomach buffers acid and reduces bacterial urease sensitivity; continuous high-dose IV PPI also suppresses urease).
- **Serology Order:** Serum *H. pylori* IgG CLIA was drawn: **Positive (124.0 AU/mL)**.
- **Clinical Reasoning:** In the acute bleeding phase where UBT, RUT, and Stool Ag are rendered falsely negative by blood and high-dose IV PPIs, serum IgG retains $> 90\%$ sensitivity to identify underlying *H. pylori* etiology.
- **Outcome:** Quadruple therapy prescribed upon hospital discharge; subsequent UBT at 8 weeks confirmed eradication.

### Case 4: Gastric MALT Lymphoma with Complete Remission Post-Eradication
- **Clinical Presentation:** A 52-year-old female evaluated for persistent epigastric discomfort underwent upper endoscopy. Biopsy of a shallow antral mucosal lesion revealed low-grade extranodal marginal zone B-cell lymphoma of MALT type (Ann Arbor stage IE).
- **Laboratory Action:** Urgent $^{13}\text{C}$-Urea Breath Test was performed following proper washout: **$\text{DOB} = 16.4\ ‰$ (Strongly Positive)**.
- **Panic Notification:** Laboratory Director immediately phoned the treating gastroenterologist and oncologist (Tier-1 Panic alert).
- **Treatment & Follow-Up:** 14-day Bismuth Quadruple eradication regimen was administered. Repeat $^{13}\text{C}$-UBT at 6 weeks post-treatment showed **$\text{DOB} = 0.6\ ‰$ (Negative; Cured)**.
- **Long-Term Outcome:** Repeat endoscopy with mapping biopsies at 6 and 12 months showed complete histological regression of the MALT lymphoma without chemotherapy or radiotherapy.

### Case 5: Pediatric Dyspepsia & Refractory Iron Deficiency Anemia
- **Clinical Presentation:** A 10-year-old girl presented with recurrent periumbilical abdominal pain, lethargy, and severe microcytic hypochromic anemia ($\text{Hb } 7.2\text{ g/dL}$, serum ferritin $4.5\ \mu\text{g/L}$) unresponsive to oral iron supplements for 4 months. Stool occult blood was weakly positive.
- **Investigation:** Monoclonal *H. pylori* Stool Antigen performed: **Positive (3.80 Index)**.
- **Pathophysiology:** *H. pylori* gastric colonization causes occult micro-bleeding, achlorhydria-mediated iron malabsorption, and active iron scavenging by bacterial lactoferrin-binding proteins.
- **Outcome:** Child was treated with weight-adjusted 14-day quadruple therapy. Follow-up SAT at 6 weeks was negative. Within 8 weeks, her hemoglobin normalized to $12.4\text{ g/dL}$ with standard oral iron.

### Case 6: Watery Diarrheal Stool Specimen Interception
- **Clinical Presentation:** A 29-year-old male with acute watery gastroenteritis (8 loose watery stools/day for 2 days) presented with acute epigastric cramping. The treating house officer ordered "Stool *H. pylori* Antigen" to rule out *H. pylori* enteritis.
- **Pre-Analytical Gate:** The phlebotomy/accession bench identified the sample as loose, watery liquid stool (Bristol Stool Scale Type 7).
- **Action & Resolution:** Sample was rejected per NABL pre-analytical criteria (dilution effect creates false-negative SAT, and *H. pylori* does not cause acute watery diarrhea). Clinician was advised to order stool multiplex PCR / culture for enteropathogens (which revealed *Campylobacter jejuni*). *H. pylori* testing deferred until full gastrointestinal recovery.

> **What does this test mean?**  
> *Helicobacter pylori* is a bacterium that infects the stomach lining, leading to acidity, burning, ulcers, and long-term stomach complications.  
> 1. **Blood Antibody Test (IgG):** Shows if your body has ever been exposed to the bacteria. Because antibodies stay in your blood for months or years after successful treatment, **a positive blood test does NOT mean you still have an active infection or need more antibiotics**.  
> 2. **Stool Antigen Test or Breath Test:** These tests detect live bacteria currently living in your stomach. They are the only reliable non-invasive tests to confirm active infection and to check if your treatment was successful.  
> 3. **Important Instruction:** You must stop taking gas/acidity tablets (pantoprazole, omeprazole, etc.) for at least **2 weeks** and antibiotics for at least **4 weeks** before taking the stool or breath test, otherwise the test may falsely show negative!

**हिंदी (Hindi Patient Guide):**
> **इस जांच रिपोर्ट का क्या अर्थ है?**  
> *हेलिकोबैक्टर पाइलोरी (H. pylori)* पेट में रहने वाला एक जीवाणु (बैक्टीरिया) है जो पेट में गैस, जलन, पेप्टिक अल्सर (घाव) और पेट की अन्य बीमारियों का मुख्य कारण बनता है।  
> 1. **खून की जांच (IgG एंटीबॉडी):** यह जांच केवल यह बताती है कि क्या आपके शरीर में कभी यह बैक्टीरिया पहुंचा था। सफल इलाज के बाद भी खून में एंटीबॉडी कई महीनों या वर्षों तक बनी रहती है। इसलिए **खून की जांच से यह तय नहीं किया जा सकता कि बीमारी अभी भी बची है या ठीक हो चुकी है।**  
> 2. **मल की जांच (Stool Antigen) या सांस की जांच (Breath Test):** ये जांचें पेट में मौजूद जीवित बैक्टीरिया की पहचान करती हैं। इलाज के बाद बैक्टीरिया पूरी तरह खत्म हुआ या नहीं, इसकी पुष्टि केवल मल या सांस की जांच से ही की जानी चाहिए।  
> 3. **अति-महत्वपूर्ण नियम:** मल या सांस की जांच से कम से कम **14 दिन (2 सप्ताह) पहले गैस/एसिडिटी की दवाइयां (पेंटोप्राजोल, ओमेप्राजोल आदि)** और कम से कम **28 दिन (4 सप्ताह) पहले एंटीबायोटिक दवाइयां** पूरी तरह बंद होनी चाहिए, अन्यथा रिपोर्ट गलत तरीके से 'नेगेटिव' आ सकती है।
