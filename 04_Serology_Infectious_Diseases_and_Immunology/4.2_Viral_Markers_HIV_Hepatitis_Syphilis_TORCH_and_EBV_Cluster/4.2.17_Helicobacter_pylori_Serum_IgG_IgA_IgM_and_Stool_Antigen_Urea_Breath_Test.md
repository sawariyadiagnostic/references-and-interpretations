### 5.2 Physiological, Obstetric, Acute Trauma/ICU & Environmental Modifiers
- **Post-Eradication Serological Scar (`IgG` Persistence):** Quantitative serum `H. pylori IgG` declines slowly over **$6\text{–}24\text{ months}$** after successful antibiotic eradication (`50%` still positive at 1 year) and **MUST NEVER be used as a Test-of-Cure**.
- **Pharmacological Suppression Modifiers (`PPIs`, Bismuth, Antibiotics):** Proton Pump Inhibitors (Omeprazole, Pantoprazole, Rabeprazole) raise gastric $\text{pH}$ and suppress *H. pylori* bacterial load and urease activity in the antrum, causing **$20\%\text{–}40\%$ false-negative Stool Antigen and $^{13}\text{C}$-Urea Breath Tests** unless `PPIs` are stopped for $\ge 2\text{ weeks}$ and antibiotics/bismuth for $\ge 4\text{ weeks}$.
- **Acute Upper GI Bleeding:** Active peptic ulcer hemorrhage buffers gastric acid and washes out mucosal bacteria, reducing Rapid Urease Test (`RUT`) sensitivity while serum `IgG` remains unaffected.

## 🔬 Section 7: In-Vivo Pharmacokinetics ($t_{1/2}$), Pathophysiological Severity Grading, Differential Diagnosis & Smart LIMS Reflex Cascades

### 7.1 In-Vivo Kinetics & Turnover Dynamics
Fecal H. pylori antigen clears within days to 2 weeks of bacterial eradication; Serum H. pylori IgG persists for 6–24+ months ('serological scar') even after 100% successful cure.

### 7.2 Pathophysiological Differential Diagnosis
- **Causes of Elevated / Positive / Abnormal-High Values:**
  Monoclonal H. pylori Stool Antigen: NEGATIVE (< 0.90 Index): No Active H. pylori Antigen Detected. CAVEAT: Verify that the patient stopped all Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs) for ≥ 14 days and all Antibiotics/Bismuth for ≥ 28 days prior to sample collection; recent PPI/antibiotic use causes up to 40% false-negative results. | Serum H. pylori IgG / IgA: REACTIVE (≥ 1.10 Index): H. pylori Antibody Reactive — Indicates Current Active Infection OR Past Cured Exposure ('Serological Scar'). Serum IgG is the preferred screening test ONLY when the patient cannot stop PPIs or has acute peptic ulcer bleeding (where blood in the stomach and IV PPIs cause false-negative RUT/UBT/Stool Ag). Do NOT use Serum IgG to monitor treatment success.
- **Causes of Decreased / Deficient / Abnormal-Low Values:**
  Monoclonal H. pylori Stool Antigen: POSITIVE (≥ 1.10 Index): ACTIVE Helicobacter pylori Gastric Infection Confirmed. Per Maastricht VI / ACG 2024 Guidelines, all active H. pylori infections should be eradicated (14-day Bismuth Quadruple Therapy or Susceptibility-Guided / Concomitant Therapy, given high > 20–35% Clarithromycin and > 70% Metronidazole resistance in India). Mandatory Test-of-Cure via Stool Antigen or ¹³C-UBT is required ≥ 4 weeks after completing antibiotics (and ≥ 2 weeks off PPIs).

### 7.3 Smart LIMS Algorithmic Reflex & Diagnostic Cascade Rules

  - IF Patient has Dyspepsia WITH Alarm Symptoms (Age ≥ 50–60, dysphagia, odynophagia, unexplained weight loss, iron-deficiency anemia, hematemesis/melena, or family history of gastric cancer) -> Do NOT rely on non-invasive 'Test-and-Treat'; advise **Urgent Upper GI Endoscopy (UGIE) with Sydney System Gastric Biopsies**.
  - IF Serum H. pylori IgG is ordered for post-treatment eradication confirmation -> Append mandatory stewardship banner redirecting clinician to **Monoclonal H. pylori Stool Antigen or ¹³C-Urea Breath Test (≥ 4 weeks off antibiotics & ≥ 2 weeks off PPIs)**.
