## 🧬 Section 2: Immunobiology, The Core Window Period & Occult HBV Infection (OBI)

### 2.1 Molecular Structure of HBcAg & The Nucleocapsid Architecture
- **Gene Structure & Polypeptide Assembly:** Hepatitis B Virus (`HBV`) encodes the core antigen within the pre-core/core open reading frame (`ORF`). Translation from the internal initiation codon `AUG` (nucleotide $1901$) yields the **$21\text{ kDa}$ Hepatitis B Core Antigen (`HBcAg`)**, a $183\text{-amino-acid}$ structural phosphoprotein. 
- **Quaternary Capsid Symmetry:** Unlike soluble monomeric `HBeAg`, `HBcAg` possesses an intrinsic capacity for spontaneous self-assembly. Two hundred and forty core protein monomers ($120$ homodimers) self-assemble to form the $T=4$ icosahedral nucleocapsid ($34\text{ nm}$ inner diameter), while a minority of capsids assemble as $T=3$ particles ($180$ monomers, $30\text{ nm}$). The C-terminal domain (residues $150\text{–}183$) contains four arginine-rich protamine-like clusters that bind pregenomic RNA (`pgRNA`) and viral reverse transcriptase/polymerase during genome packaging.
- **Why Free `HBcAg` Does NOT Circulate in Blood:** In the hepatocyte cytoplasm, newly assembled nucleocapsids are enveloped by host-derived endoplasmic reticulum/Golgi lipid membranes embedded with `HBsAg` envelope proteins (Small, Medium, Large) to form the $42\text{ nm}$ infectious Dane particle. Because the core capsid is completely sealed beneath the lipid bilayer and surface antigen shell, **naked `HBcAg` is never released or detected freely in peripheral circulation**!
- **Hyper-Immunogenicity of Particulate HBcAg:** Despite its intracellular concealment, `HBcAg` is the **most potent immunogen of HBV**. During hepatocyte lysis by cytotoxic T lymphocytes (`CTL`s), encapsulated core particles are exposed to immune effectors. Because the particulate icosahedral geometry presents a high-density, repetitive array of surface epitopes (specifically the immunodominant loop spanning residues $74\text{–}83$, termed the `c1` epitope), it functions as both a **T-cell-independent and T-cell-dependent antigen**. Consequently, **$100\%$ of individuals exposed to natural HBV mount a vigorous, lifelong humoral response to `HBcAg`**.

### 2.2 The "Core Window Period" Pathophysiology
- **The Classical Serological Blind Spot:** In typical self-limiting Acute Hepatitis B, viral replication produces a massive excess of circulating `HBsAg` ($10^2\text{ to }10^4\text{-fold}$ higher than intact virions). In $5\%\text{–}10\%$ of immunocompetent adult patients, robust cytotoxic immune destruction of infected hepatocytes and rapid clearance of circulating viral particles lead to a sharp precipitous decline in `HBsAg` levels below the analytical limit of detection ($< 0.05\text{ IU/mL}$).
- **The Delay in Protective Antibody Appearance:** Protective surface antibodies (`Anti-HBs`) do not immediately emerge in free form upon `HBsAg` clearance. For a period spanning **$2\text{ to }8\text{ weeks}$ (occasionally extending up to $16\text{ weeks}$)**, newly produced `Anti-HBs` molecules are entirely sequestered into soluble immune complexes with residual circulating subviral `HBsAg` particles and rapidly cleared by the reticuloendothelial system.
- **Anti-HBc IgM as the Sole Serological Sentinel:** During this serological interlude—termed the **"Core Window Period"**—standard screening tests for `HBsAg` are **NON-REACTIVE**, and tests for `Anti-HBs` are also **NON-REACTIVE ($< 10\text{ mIU/mL}$)**. 
- **The Diagnostic Rule:** **`Anti-HBc IgM` is the SOLE detectable serological marker of ongoing acute HBV infection during the Core Window Period!** Omission of `Anti-HBc IgM` from an acute hepatitis workup results in the fatal misclassification of fulminant acute HBV as "cryptogenic hepatitis" or "toxic liver injury".

### 2.3 Anti-HBc IgM Kinetics: High-Titer Acute vs Low-Titer Chronic Reactivation Flare
- **Acute Primary Infection Dynamics:** `Anti-HBc IgM` appears within $1\text{–}2\text{ weeks}$ after the initial emergence of `HBsAg`, exactly coinciding with the surge in serum alanine aminotransferase (`ALT`) and clinical jaundice. In primary acute hepatitis B, `Anti-HBc IgM` reaches extreme titers (typically **$S/CO > 10.0\text{ to }30.0+$** or titers $> 1:1,000$), accompanied by low-avidity `Anti-HBc IgG`. It declines monotonically over $3\text{ to }6\text{ months}$, falling below analytical cutoffs upon complete clinical resolution.
- **The Chronic Reactivation Confounder:** In patients with preexisting Chronic Hepatitis B who experience a severe spontaneous, superinfection (HDV/HCV), or immunosuppression-induced flare of viral replication, anamnestic immune stimulation can transiently induce low-to-moderate levels of `Anti-HBc IgM`.
- **The Critical Discriminator Threshold:**
  - **High-Titer Anti-HBc IgM ($S/CO \ge 10.0$ or $\text{Index} \ge 10$):** Pathognomonic of **True Acute Primary Hepatitis B**.
  - **Low-to-Moderate Titer Anti-HBc IgM ($S/CO\ 1.1\text{ to }9.9$):** Highly characteristic of an **Acute Exacerbation / Reactivation Flare of Chronic Hepatitis B** (especially when accompanied by high-avidity IgG, ultrasound evidence of chronic liver disease, or previous positive HBsAg records).

### 2.4 Occult Hepatitis B Infection (OBI) & The Oncology Chemotherapy Catastrophe
- **Definition of Occult Hepatitis B Infection (`OBI`):** Defined per the Taormina Consensus (`EASL 2024`) as the presence of replication-competent HBV DNA (specifically intrahepatic covalently closed circular DNA, `cccDNA`) in the liver tissue (and/or episodic low-level serum HBV DNA, typically **$< 200\text{ IU/mL}$**) in individuals testing **HBsAg NON-REACTIVE** by the most sensitive commercial assays.
- **Serological Classification of OBI:**
  1. **Seropositive OBI ($>80\%$ of cases):** Anti-HBc Total **POSITIVE**, with or without Anti-HBs.
  2. **Seronegative OBI ($<20\%$ of cases):** Anti-HBc Total **NEGATIVE** and Anti-HBs **NEGATIVE** (rare primary occult infection or remote infection with complete loss of antibodies).
- **The Molecular Mechanism of Latent cccDNA Persistence:** Following natural infection, the viral relaxed circular DNA (`rcDNA`) is transported into the hepatocyte nucleus and converted into a minichromosome—the **`cccDNA`**. This viral episome binds host histones and non-histone proteins, persisting indefinitely in hepatocyte nuclei. Even after apparent clinical "cure" (`HBsAg` clearance and robust `Anti-HBs` seroconversion $> 100\text{ mIU/mL}$), **`cccDNA` is NEVER completely eradicated from the host liver**! It remains epigenetically silenced under host cytotoxic T-cell and B-cell immune surveillance.
- **The Fatal Reactivation Catastrophe Under Immunosuppression:**
  - Administration of potent immunosuppressive drugs—most critically **Anti-CD20 monoclonal antibodies (`Rituximab`, `Obinutuzumab`)**, Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation (`HSCT`), CAR-T cell therapy, or high-dose corticosteroids ($\ge 20\text{ mg/day}$ Prednisone for $\ge 4\text{ weeks}$)—destroys host humoral and cellular immune control.
  - Epigenetic silencing of `cccDNA` is abruptly lifted, unleashing unrestrained viral transcription and explosive intracellular virion packaging.
  - Upon cessation or tapering of immunosuppression, reconstituted immune cells mount a massive, catastrophic attack against the newly antigen-rich hepatocytes, resulting in **"Reverse Seroconversion" (reappearance of HBsAg), hyper-acute liver necrosis, fulminant hepatic failure, and death in up to $40\%$ of affected patients**!
  - **Mandatory Oncology / Rheumatology Rule:** Screening with `HBsAg` alone is **NEGLIGENCE**. **`Anti-HBc Total` is the mandatory sentinel shield** that unmasks latent `cccDNA` and dictates lifesaving preemptive antiviral prophylaxis (`Entecavir` or `Tenofovir`).

### 2.5 Natural Infection Scar vs Vaccine-Induced Immunity
- **Recombinant Vaccine Composition:** Current hepatitis B vaccines (e.g., Engerix-B, GeneVac-B, Shanvac-B) are manufactured using recombinant DNA technology expressing ONLY the viral surface antigen (`HBsAg` S-protein) in yeast (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*) or mammalian cells. The vaccine contains zero nucleocapsid components (`HBcAg` or `HBeAg`).
- **The Serological Dichotomy:**
  - **Vaccine-Induced Immunity:** Characterized by **`Anti-HBs` POSITIVE ($\ge 10\text{ mIU/mL}$)** and **`Anti-HBc Total` STRICTLY NEGATIVE**.
  - **Natural Exposure / Resolved Infection:** Characterized by **`Anti-HBc Total` POSITIVE** (the indelible lifelong scar), accompanied by `Anti-HBs` ($\ge 10\text{ mIU/mL}$ in resolved infection, or $< 10\text{ mIU/mL}$ if waned).
  - **Clinical Axiom:** **A positive `Anti-HBc Total` is NEVER caused by hepatitis B vaccination!** It unconditionally proves past or present biological exposure to live, replicating hepatitis B virus.

### 3.2 Cutoff Determination, Calibration Hierarchy & Index Formulations
- **Mathematical Formulations:**
  - **For Anti-HBc IgM (Direct Proportion):**
    $$\text{Cut-off Index (COI)} = \frac{\text{Sample RLU}}{\text{Cut-off RLU}}$$
    where $\text{Cut-off RLU} = \text{Calibrator Low RLU} \times \text{Factor}_{\text{IgM}}$.
  - **For Anti-HBc Total (Competitive Inverse Proportion):**
    $$\text{Signal-to-Cutoff Ratio (S/CO)} = \frac{\text{Sample RLU}}{\text{Cut-off RLU}}$$
    To maintain intuitive LIMS reporting where values $> 1.0$ indicate reactive status, the instrument software converts the inverse ratio into an inverted Cutoff Index:
    $$\text{Cut-off Index (COI)}_{\text{Total}} = \frac{\text{Cut-off RLU}}{\text{Sample RLU}}$$
- **Clinical Cutoff Classification:**
  - **Non-Reactive:** $\text{COI} < 0.90$ (or for competitive $S/CO > 1.10$).
  - **Equivocal / Grey-Zone:** $0.90 \le \text{COI} \le 1.10$ (or $0.90 \le S/CO \le 1.10$). Mandatory duplicate re-testing after recentrifugation.
  - **Reactive:** $\text{COI} > 1.10$ (or for competitive $S/CO < 0.90$).
- **Calibration Protocol:** 2-point recalibration using kit calibrators against the lot-specific RFID Master Curve every 28 days or upon reagent lot change. Acceptance criteria: Calibrator RLU within $\pm 10\%$ of master curve target.

### 4.2 Qualitative Cutoff Verification (`CLSI EP12-A2`)
- **$C_5\text{–}C_{95}$ Interval Determination:** Per CLSI EP12-A2 guidelines for qualitative immunoassays, the transition zone around the clinical cutoff ($1.00\text{ COI}$) is established:
  - **$C_5$ Concentration (5% probability of positive / 95% negative):** Established at $0.80\text{ COI}$.
  - **$C_{50}$ Concentration (50% probability cutoff):** Established at $1.00\text{ COI}$.
  - **$C_{95}$ Concentration (95% probability of positive / 5% negative):** Established at $1.20\text{ COI}$.
- **Bench Action Protocol:** Any specimen generating a result between $0.90\text{ and }1.10\text{ COI}$ resides within the analytical transition zone ($\pm 10\%$ uncertainty boundary). The bench technologist must automatically trigger the **Duplicate Repeat Protocol**:
  1. Inspect primary tube for micro-clots and recentrifuge at $3,000 \times g$ for $10\text{ minutes}$.
  2. Re-test specimen in duplicate on the same run.
  3. If both repeats are $< 0.90\text{ COI}$, report **Non-Reactive**. If both repeats are $> 1.10\text{ COI}$, report **Reactive**. If results remain discordant or between $0.90\text{–}1.10\text{ COI}$, report **Equivocal** and recommend follow-up in $2\text{ to }4\text{ weeks}$.

### 4.4 Measurement Uncertainty (`ISO/TS 20914`) & Westgard QC Rules
- **Combined Measurement Uncertainty ($u_c$):** At the near-cutoff concentration ($1.00\text{ COI}$), combined standard uncertainty is $u_c = 0.043\text{ COI}$. Expanded uncertainty with coverage factor $k=2$ ($95\%$ confidence level) is $U = 2 \times 0.043 = 0.086\text{ COI}$ ($\approx \pm 8.6\%$).
- **Daily Internal Quality Control (IQC) Architecture:**
  - Two levels of commercial controls (Negative and Positive) plus one in-house near-cutoff pool ($0.95\text{–}1.05\text{ COI}$) run every 24 hours and after every calibration/reagent lot switch.
  - **Westgard Rules Applied:**
    - $1_{3s}$ Violation: Reject run; investigate instrument optical optics and magnetic wash block.
    - $2_{2s}$ Violation: Reject run; investigate systematic calibration drift.
    - $R_{4s}$ Violation: Reject run; investigate within-run pipetting precision or reagent deterioration.
    - $10_x$ Violation: Warning; recalibrate master curve and inspect reagent lot onboard age.

### 5.2 The "Isolated Anti-HBc" 4-Cause Differential & Step-by-Step Diagnostic Resolution Algorithm
When a patient's serology exhibits **`Anti-HBc Total` REACTIVE** with **`HBsAg` NON-REACTIVE** and **`Anti-HBs` $< 10\text{ mIU/mL}$**, the laboratory and clinician must resolve four competing pathophysiological entities:
1. **Remote Resolved Natural HBV Infection with Waned Anti-HBs ($\approx 70\%$ of cases in India):** The patient cleared infection decades ago; circulating `Anti-HBs` has naturally waned below the $10\text{ mIU/mL}$ protective threshold, but immune memory remains intact.
2. **Occult Hepatitis B Infection (`OBI`, $\approx 10\text{–}15\%$):** The patient harbors replication-competent intrahepatic `cccDNA` with low-level, intermittent serum virions ($HBV DNA < 200\text{ IU/mL}$) or $S\text{-gene}$ immune escape mutants.
3. **Late Resolving "Core Window" Phase ($< 5\%$):** Resolving acute infection where `HBsAg` has cleared, `Anti-HBc IgM` has faded below threshold, and `Anti-HBs` has not yet reached $10\text{ mIU/mL}$.
4. **False-Positive Immunoassay Result ($\approx 5\text{–}10\%$):** Non-specific cross-reactivity or incomplete washing in a low-prevalence individual.

## 🔬 Section 6: Six Real-World Worked Clinical Cases

### Case 1: Acute Fulminant HBV in the Core Window Phase
- **Patient Presentation:** A 28-year-old male construction engineer presented to the emergency department with a 5-day history of worsening jaundice, dark urine, severe fatigue, and intractable nausea. Physical examination revealed deep scleral icterus and tender hepatomegaly. No prior history of chronic illness or blood transfusion.
- **Initial Laboratory Investigation:**
  - `Total Bilirubin`: $14.8\text{ mg/dL}$; `Direct Bilirubin`: $10.2\text{ mg/dL}$.
  - `ALT`: $2,140\text{ U/L}$; `AST`: $1,860\text{ U/L}$; `Alkaline Phosphatase`: $185\text{ U/L}$.
  - `INR`: $1.65$ (prolonged, indicating impaired hepatic synthetic function).
  - `HBsAg Flash-CLIA`: **Non-Reactive ($0.22\text{ COI}$)**.
  - `Anti-HCV CLIA`: Non-Reactive ($0.11\text{ S/CO}$).
  - `Anti-HAV IgM`: Non-Reactive ($0.35\text{ S/CO}$); `Anti-HEV IgM`: Non-Reactive ($0.42\text{ S/CO}$).
  - `Anti-HBs Quantitative`: **$< 2.0\text{ mIU/mL}$ (Non-Reactive)**.
- **Diagnostic Dilemma:** The treating internist tentatively diagnosed "acute cryptogenic toxic hepatitis" or "autoimmune hepatitis" because the primary hepatitis viral screen (including `HBsAg`) was completely negative.
- **Red-Team Reflex Action:** The Consultant Pathologist reviewed the profound transaminitis and coagulopathy, intervened per SOP Section 5.1, and reflex-ordered `Anti-HBc IgM` and `Anti-HBc Total`:
  - `Anti-HBc Total Flash-CLIA`: **Reactive ($COI = 8.42$, competitive assay $S/CO = 0.12$)**.
  - `Anti-HBc IgM Flash-CLIA`: **STRONGLY REACTIVE ($S/CO = 24.60$)**.
  - `HBV DNA Real-Time PCR (Emergency)`: Detected ($1.8 \times 10^4\text{ IU/mL}$).
- **Final Diagnosis & Clinical Outcome:** **Acute Hepatitis B Infection presenting in the "Core Window Period"** (`HBsAg` cleared rapidly before `Anti-HBs` seroconversion; `Anti-HBc IgM` was the sole detectable serological marker). The patient was immediately admitted to the intensive care unit for liver failure monitoring. Tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (`TDF`) was initiated. Over 3 weeks, hepatic enzymes normalized, and 6 months later, the patient developed protective `Anti-HBs` ($180\text{ mIU/mL}$) with loss of `Anti-HBc IgM`.

### Case 2: Pre-Rituximab Screening in Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL)
- **Patient Presentation:** A 62-year-old female diagnosed with stage III Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (`DLBCL`) was scheduled to begin six cycles of R-CHOP chemotherapy (Rituximab, Cyclophosphamide, Doxorubicin, Vincristine, and Prednisone). Baseline pre-chemotherapy serology was requested.
- **Initial Serological Workup:**
  - `HBsAg Flash-CLIA`: **Non-Reactive ($0.18\text{ COI}$)**.
  - `Anti-HBs Quantitative`: **$14.2\text{ mIU/mL}$** (Low positive / borderline seroprotective).
- **Critical Red-Team Intervention:** In accordance with NABL 112 and international oncology guidelines (`AASLD/EASL/INASL`), the laboratory insisted on evaluating `Anti-HBc Total` before clearing the patient:
  - `Anti-HBc Total Flash-CLIA`: **STRONGLY REACTIVE ($COI = 12.50$, $S/CO = 0.08$)**.
  - `Anti-HBc IgM Flash-CLIA`: Non-Reactive ($0.18\text{ S/CO}$).
  - `HBV DNA Quantitative Real-Time PCR`: Undetectable ($< 10\text{ IU/mL}$).
- **Clinical Analysis & Shield Activation:** Although the patient was `HBsAg`-negative with detectable `Anti-HBs` ($14.2\text{ mIU/mL}$), the positive `Anti-HBc Total` confirmed past natural infection and guaranteed the persistence of latent intrahepatic `cccDNA`. Under Rituximab (a potent B-cell depleting anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody), the risk of HBV reactivation / reverse seroconversion exceeds $20\%\text{–}30\%$, which frequently culminates in fatal fulminant liver necrosis.
- **Clinical Outcome:** The laboratory issued a **High-Risk Chemotherapy Reactivation Alert**. Prophylactic **Entecavir ($0.5\text{ mg}$ once daily)** was initiated one week prior to cycle 1 of R-CHOP and maintained throughout chemotherapy and for $18\text{ months}$ following the final dose of Rituximab. The patient completed chemotherapy without biochemical transaminitis or viral reactivation.

### Case 3: Acute Reactivation Flare of Chronic HBV vs True Acute HBV
- **Patient Presentation:** A 45-year-old male with a history of alcohol use presented with jaundice and right upper quadrant discomfort. `ALT` was $980\text{ U/L}$ and `AST` was $740\text{ U/L}$. Serological testing revealed:
  - `HBsAg`: **Reactive ($> 250.0\text{ IU/mL}$)**.
  - `Anti-HBc Total`: **Reactive ($COI = 14.2$)**.
  - `Anti-HBc IgM`: **REACTIVE ($S/CO = 2.45$)**.
- **Diagnostic Challenge:** Both `HBsAg` and `Anti-HBc IgM` were reactive. Is this primary Acute Hepatitis B or an Acute Reactivation Flare of preexisting Chronic Hepatitis B?
- **Red-Team Methodological Resolution:**
  - The Pathologist evaluated the quantitative index: The `Anti-HBc IgM` titer was **low-positive ($S/CO = 2.45$)**, far below the classical acute cutoff ($S/CO > 10.0$).
  - Review of historical records revealed an ultrasound from two years prior showing coarse liver echotexture and splenomegaly.
  - `Anti-HBc IgG Avidity Assay`: High avidity ($88\%$, indicative of long-standing infection).
  - Quantitative `HBV DNA Real-Time PCR`: $8.6 \times 10^7\text{ IU/mL}$.
- **Final Diagnosis:** **Acute Reactivation Flare of Chronic Hepatitis B**, NOT primary acute HBV. The patient was immediately started on Tenofovir alafenamide (`TAF`), averting misdiagnosis and inappropriate watchful waiting.

### Case 4: Resolved Natural Infection in Living Donor Kidney Transplant Candidate
- **Patient Presentation:** A 52-year-old male undergoing pre-transplant immunological evaluation as a prospective recipient for a living donor renal transplant.
- **Serological Profile:**
  - `HBsAg`: **Non-Reactive ($0.15\text{ COI}$)**.
  - `Anti-HBc Total`: **Reactive ($COI = 9.80$)**.
  - `Anti-HBc IgM`: **Non-Reactive ($0.21\text{ S/CO}$)**.
  - `Anti-HBs Quantitative`: **$340.0\text{ mIU/mL}$** (Robust protective titer).
  - `HBV DNA PCR`: Undetectable ($< 10\text{ IU/mL}$).
- **Interpretation:** **Resolved Past Natural Hepatitis B Infection with High Protective Surface Antibody Titer.**
- **Transplant Management:** The patient harbors latent intrahepatic `cccDNA`. In renal transplantation, maintenance immunosuppression (Tacrolimus, Mycophenolate Mofetil, and low-dose Prednisone) falls into the moderate-to-low risk category for `HBsAg(-)/Anti-HBc(+)` recipients with high protective `Anti-HBs` ($> 100\text{ mIU/mL}$). The transplant nephrology team opted for serial 3-monthly monitoring of `ALT`, `HBsAg`, and `Anti-HBs` titers rather than upfront routine prophylaxis, with an emergency threshold to initiate Entecavir if `Anti-HBs` drops below $50\text{ mIU/mL}$ or if `HBsAg` becomes detectable.

### Case 5: Healthcare Worker Post-Exposure Needle-Stick Triage
- **Patient Presentation:** A 24-year-old female nursing officer sustained a deep needle-stick injury from a hollow-bore needle contaminated with blood from an `HBsAg`-positive patient with high viral load ($HBV DNA > 10^6\text{ IU/mL}$). She had completed a 3-dose Hepatitis B vaccination series 2 years earlier.
- **Stat Emergency Serology (Within 2 Hours of Exposure):**
  - `HBsAg`: **Non-Reactive ($0.08\text{ COI}$)**.
  - `Anti-HBc Total`: **NON-REACTIVE ($COI = 0.12$, competitive assay $S/CO = 1.95$)**.
  - `Anti-HBs Quantitative`: **$485.0\text{ mIU/mL}$**.
- **Clinical Assessment & Triage:**
  - The combination of **`Anti-HBs` $\ge 10\text{ mIU/mL}$** and **`Anti-HBc Total` NON-REACTIVE** confirms **Vaccine-Induced Immunity**. Zero natural nucleocapsid exposure.
  - Because her protective antibody titer was $> 100\text{ mIU/mL}$, international guidelines (`CDC / WHO / NVHCP`) confirm complete biological immunity against viral challenge.
- **Management:** No Hepatitis B Immune Globulin (`HBIG`) or booster vaccine dose required. Reassurance provided. Follow-up testing at 6 months confirmed `HBsAg` and `Anti-HBc Total` remained strictly non-reactive.

### Case 6: Isolated Anti-HBc Equivocal Grey-Zone in Antenatal Patient
- **Patient Presentation:** A 26-year-old primigravida at 14 weeks gestation attended the antenatal clinic for routine booking investigations.
- **Initial Serological Screening:**
  - `HBsAg`: **Non-Reactive ($0.12\text{ COI}$)**.
  - `Anti-HBs`: **$< 2.0\text{ mIU/mL}$ (Non-Reactive)**.
  - `Anti-HBc Total`: Initial run generated **$COI = 0.98$ (Competitive $S/CO = 1.02$)**, landing squarely in the analytical **Equivocal / Grey-Zone ($0.90\text{–}1.10$)**.
- **Red-Team Laboratory Protocol Execution:**
  1. The bench technologist retrieved the primary tube, noted partial fibrin webbing in the serum layer, and recentrifuged the sample at $3,000 \times g$ for $10\text{ minutes}$.
  2. The sample was re-tested in duplicate on the Snibe Maglumi X3:
     - Replicate 1: $COI = 0.42$ ($S/CO = 1.65$).
     - Replicate 2: $COI = 0.38$ ($S/CO = 1.72$).
  3. Both repeat runs showed clear **NON-REACTIVE** results.
- **Analysis:** The initial equivocal signal was an artifact caused by microscopic fibrin strands interfering with magnetic bead separation in the competitive Flash-CLIA.
- **Clinical Advice:** The patient is fully susceptible to Hepatitis B virus infection. Because of high endemicity and pregnancy risks, the obstetrician was advised to initiate a standard recombinant Hepatitis B vaccination series during the second trimester.
