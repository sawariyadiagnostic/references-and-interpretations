### 1.2 Reaction Chemistry, Optical Measurement Physics & Calibration Hierarchy

Each diagnostic methodology within Cluster `4.1` adheres to defined physical and immunochemical mechanisms:
1. **Dengue NS1 Antigen Microplate Sandwich ELISA (`4.1.2`):**
   - *Reaction Mechanism:* Microplate wells are pre-coated with monoclonal anti-dengue NS1 antibodies directed against conserved epitopes of all four DENV serotypes. Patient serum is incubated with peroxidase-conjugated monoclonal anti-NS1. In the presence of NS1, a sandwich complex (`Solid Phase Ab — NS1 Ag — HRP Conjugate Ab`) forms.
   - *Detection Physics:* Following wash cycles using phosphate-buffered saline with $0.05\%$ Tween-20 (`PBST`), tetramethylbenzidine (`TMB`) substrate is added. Horseradish peroxidase catalyzes oxidation of `TMB` by hydrogen peroxide to form a blue di-imine product, which upon addition of $1\text{ N } \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ stop solution turns stable yellow, photometrically measured at $\lambda = 450\text{ nm}$ (reference filter $620\text{–}630\text{ nm}$). Optical density is proportional to NS1 concentration.
2. **Dengue $\mu$-Capture MAC-ELISA (`4.1.3`):**
   - *Reaction Mechanism:* Anti-human $\text{IgM}$ antibodies ($\mu$-chain specific) coated on polystyrene microtiter wells selectively capture patient serum $\text{IgM}$, completely eliminating competing $\text{IgG}$ and avoiding non-specific binding from Rheumatoid Factor. Inactivated quadrivalent DENV antigen is added, followed by enzyme-labeled monoclonal anti-DENV detector antibody and `TMB` chromogen.
3. **Malaria Bivalent Immunochromatography (`4.1.4`):**
   - *Reaction Mechanism:* Whole blood lysed by surfactant releases intra-erythrocytic antigens. Mobile colloidal gold-conjugated monoclonal antibodies against *P. falciparum* histidine-rich protein-2 ($\text{Pf HRP-2}$) and pan-specific parasite lactate dehydrogenase ($\text{Pan pLDH}$) bind target antigens. Capillary action carries the complexes across a nitrocellulose membrane where immobilized capture lines bind the conjugates, producing visible pink/purple bands at the test and procedural control positions.
4. **Giemsa-Romanowsky Smear Photomicroscopy (`4.1.5`):**
   - *Staining Physics:* Azure B (cationic dye) binds acidic nuclear chromatin of *Plasmodium*, staining it reddish-purple. Eosin Y (anionic dye) binds basic cytoplasmic proteins, staining parasite cytoplasm clear sky-blue and host erythrocytes pale pinkish-grey. Strict pH buffering at $7.20 \pm 0.05$ prevents acid precipitate and ensures pathognomonic stippling visibility.
5. **Quantitative Tube Agglutination (`4.1.6` & `4.1.10`):**
   - *Physical Mechanism:* Suspensions of killed bacterial cells (*Salmonella enterica* or *Brucella abortus*) react with patient serum in serial doubling dilutions. Somatic `O` antigens form dense, compact, chalky-granular agglutinates that settle slowly ($37^\circ\text{C} \times 18\text{h}$ or $56^\circ\text{C} \times 4\text{h}$). Flagellar `H` antigens cross-link into loose, flocculent, feathery clumps within $2\text{ hours}$ at $37^\circ\text{C}$.

### 1.3 Microplate Reader Calibration, Washer Metrology & Optical QA (`4.1.11`)

To satisfy `ISO 15189:2022 Clause 6.4` and `NABL 112`, automated plate readers and washers undergo regular metrological verification:
1. **Photometric Linearity & Accuracy Verification:**
   - Evaluated semi-annually using calibrated neutral density solid glass filters traceable to **NIST SRM 930e**. Absorbance linearity must satisfy $r^2 \ge 0.999$ across the dynamic range of $0.000$ to $3.000\text{ OD}$ at $\lambda = 405, 450, 492,\text{ and } 620\text{ nm}$. Photometric accuracy tolerance: $\le \pm 1.0\% \pm 0.005\text{ OD}$.
2. **Microplate Washer Residual Volume Verification:**
   - Evaluated monthly by dispensing $300\text{ }\mu\text{L}$ of deionized water per well, performing an 8-cycle wash, and measuring residual well volume via gravimetric micro-pipetting. Residual volume must not exceed **$\le 2.0\text{ }\mu\text{L per well}$** across all 96 wells to prevent background staining and false-positive optical drift.
3. **Incubator Thermal Mapping:**
   - Water baths and dry incubators undergo multi-probe digital temperature logging across all four quadrant positions, maintaining $37.0 \pm 0.5^\circ\text{C}$ and $56.0 \pm 0.5^\circ\text{C}$ traceable to NPL (National Physical Laboratory) standards.

## 🌍 Section 2: Regional Epidemiology & Chronobiology of Seasonal Fevers in Haryana

### 2.1 Regional Eco-Epidemiological Landscape (Charkhi Dadri, Bhiwani & Mahendragarh)

The semi-arid agro-ecological belt of South-Western Haryana represents a unique multi-pathogen confluence zone characterized by extreme seasonal climate variations:
1. **The Post-Monsoon Vector Explosion (July through November):**
   - Monsoon rains (late June to August) followed by stagnant water collection in unpaved irrigation drains, construction ditches, air-cooler trays, discarded tires, and agricultural canals create hyper-favorable breeding habitats for:
     - ***Aedes aegypti* & *Aedes albopictus*:** Diurnal container breeders driving epidemic surges of **Dengue Virus (DENV 1–4)** and **Chikungunya Virus (CHIKV)**. Peak transmission occurs between **August and November**, subsiding only when ambient temperatures fall below $15^\circ\text{C}$ in late November.
     - ***Anopheles culicifacies* & *Anopheles stephensi*:** Crepuscular/nocturnal breeders in slow-moving canals and domestic cisterns driving transmission of ***Plasmodium vivax* (75–85% of regional cases)** and ***Plasmodium falciparum* (15–25% of cases)**.
2. **Agricultural Chigger Vector Dynamics (Scrub Typhus — *Orientia tsutsugamushi*):**
   - In rural Charkhi Dadri (Badhra, Jhuppa, Bond Kalan) and neighbouring Bhiwani/Mahendragarh, extensive farming of bajra (pearl millet), cotton, and mustard creates dense ground foliage bordering scrub vegetation.
   - Farm workers and laborers resting in field margins are bitten by microscopic six-legged larval trombiculid mites (**"chiggers"**, primarily *Leptotrombidium deliense*). Transmission of *O. tsutsugamushi* occurs during painless feeding, resulting in **Scrub Typhus surges from August to December**. This condition is frequently misdiagnosed as enteric fever or severe dengue.
3. **Dairy Farming Zoonotic Reservoir (Brucellosis & Leptospirosis):**
   - With dense domestic water-buffalo (*Murrah* breed) and cattle populations in rural Haryana, raw unpasteurized milk/butter consumption and unassisted handling of bovine placental membranes/aborted fetuses cause endemic human **Brucellosis (*Brucella melitensis* & *B. abortus*)**, presenting as undulating fever, spondylodiscitis, and chronic arthralgia.
   - Waterlogging during heavy monsoon rains leads to mixing of rodent (*Rattus norvegicus*) and livestock urine with standing flood water, causing sporadic **Leptospirosis (*Leptospira interrogans*)** cases presenting with conjunctival suffusion, jaundice, and acute renal impairment.
4. **Waterborne Enteric Transmission (Typhoid & Paratyphoid Fever):**
   - Ingress of open sewage into aged municipal drinking-water supply lines during monsoon downpours precipitates perennial outbreaks of *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhi and Paratyphi A/B.

### 2.2 The Day-by-Day Diagnostic Window Concept (Kinetic Chronobiology)

Diagnostic biomarker sensitivity in acute tropical fevers depends strictly on the **Day of Illness (Day 1 = First calendar day of documented fever $\ge 38.0^\circ\text{C} / 100.4^\circ\text{F}$)**:

```
[FEVER ONSET: DAY 1]
   │
   ├── DAYS 1 TO 4: ACUTE VIREMIC / PARASITEMIC / BACTEREMIC PHASE
   │   ├── High Pathogen Load: Dengue NS1 (+), Malaria Smear/RDT (+), Blood Culture (+)
   │   └── Humoral Immunity Absent: Dengue IgM (-), Widal (-), Scrub Typhus IgM (-)
   │       └── WARNING: Ordering antibody tests here yields DANGEROUS FALSE-NEGATIVES!
   │
   ├── DAYS 5 TO 7: SEROCONVERSION / CRITICAL TRANSITION GAP
   │   ├── Pathogen Cleared / Masked: Dengue NS1 falls (bound by host IgG/IgM complexes)
   │   ├── Early IgM Appears: Dengue IgM (+), TyphiDot IgM (+), Scrub Typhus IgM (+)
   │   └── DENGUE DEFERVESCENCE ALERT: Fever breaks, but capillary leakage accelerates!
   │       └── Daily Monitoring: Hematocrit (PCV) rise > 20% + Platelet drop < 50,000/μL
   │
   └── DAYS 8 TO 14+: CONVALESCENT / IMMUNE-MEDIATED PHASE
       ├── High-Titer Humoral Antibodies: Dengue IgG (+), Scrub Typhus IgM peak, TyphiDot IgG (+)
       └── Agglutination Serology Becomes Valid: Widal Tube Titer shows ≥ 4-fold diagnostic rise
```

### 2.4 Molecular Pathophysiology & Virulence Mechanisms

1. **Dengue Capillary Leak & Antibody-Dependent Enhancement (ADE):**
   - *Soluble NS1 (sNS1) Endothelial Disruption:* Secreted hexameric sNS1 binds Toll-like receptor 4 (`TLR4`) on human microvascular endothelial cells, activating p38 MAPK and NF-$\kappa$B pathways. This triggers secretion of sialidases and heparanases, cleaving the endothelial glycocalyx layer (heparan sulfate and syndecan-1 shedding) and producing profound transient hyperpermeability (plasma leakage into pleural/peritoneal cavities).
   - *ADE Mechanism in Heterologous Secondary Infection:* When previously infected with DENV-1, sub-neutralizing cross-reactive IgG antibodies bind DENV-2 or DENV-3 virions via Fab fragments. The immune complexes enter monocytes and macrophages via **$\text{Fc}\gamma\text{RIIa}$ (CD32)** receptors, increasing intracellular viral replication by $> 100\text{-fold}$ and triggering a catastrophic cytokine surge (**TNF-$\alpha$, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10**), resulting in Dengue Hemorrhagic Fever (`DHF`) and Dengue Shock Syndrome (`DSS`).
2. **Malaria Cytoadherence & Hypnozoite Biology:**
   - *PfEMP-1 Sequestration:* *P. falciparum* synthesizes erythrocyte membrane protein 1 (`PfEMP-1`) that inserts into the host RBC membrane, forming "knobs". PfEMP-1 binds endothelial **CD36, ICAM-1, and EPCR** in cerebral microvessels, sequestering late trophozoites and schizonts away from splenic filtration. This causes microvascular occlusion, tissue hypoxia, and cerebral malaria.
   - *P. vivax Hypnozoite Liver Dormancy:* Following sporozoite invasion of hepatocytes, a fraction differentiate into dormant **hypnozoites** that reactivate weeks to months later, causing clinical relapses. Following confirmation of *P. vivax* and exclusion of G6PD deficiency, **Primaquine ($0.25\text{–}0.50\text{ mg/kg}$ daily for $14\text{ days}$)** is required for radical cure.
3. **Scrub Typhus Endothelial Tropism:**
   - *Orientia tsutsugamushi* obligately infects endothelial cells of arterioles, capillaries, and venules, initiating widespread microvascular endothelial injury and perivascular mononuclear cuffing (disseminated vasculitis), manifesting as pneumonitis, ARDS, myocarditis, and acute kidney injury.

### 3.2 The Clinical Danger of Over-Relying on Widal Agglutination in Rural Haryana

> [!CAUTION]
> **The North Indian Widal Overdiagnosis Trap:**  
> Healthy residents across rural Haryana (Charkhi Dadri, Bhiwani, Mahendragarh) maintain high endemic baseline titers against *Salmonella enterica* ($S. \text{Typhi Somatic O [TO]} \le 1:40\text{–}1:80$; $S. \text{Typhi Flagellar H [TH]} \le 1:80\text{–}1:160$) secondary to repeated environmental exposure and typhoid vaccination.  
> 
> When a patient presenting on **Day 2 to Day 4 of fever** undergoes a rapid slide Widal test:
> 1. The test detects pre-existing baseline memory antibodies ($1:80$), yielding a **false-positive diagnosis of typhoid fever**.
> 2. Simultaneously, **anamnestic recall** occurs: acute viremia from Dengue or bacteremia from Scrub Typhus non-specifically activates memory B-cells, triggering a temporary rise in anti-*Salmonella* flagellar antibodies without active typhoid infection!
> 3. Consequently, patients with life-threatening **Dengue with plasma leakage** or **Scrub Typhus with multi-organ dysfunction** are started on oral fluoroquinolones/cefixime while Dengue shock monitoring is neglected and Doxycycline is withheld.

#### Mandatory Laboratory Rules for Widal Testing at Sawariya Diagnostic Lab:
- **Rule 1 (First-Week Ban):** Widal testing is clinically uninformative and strongly discouraged during **Days 1–4 of fever**. Automated Blood Culture (`BACTEC FX40`) is the mandatory diagnostic modality in Week 1.
- **Rule 2 (Slide Screen Elimination):** Positive rapid slide agglutination results must be confirmed by **Quantitative Tube Agglutination** incubated at $37^\circ\text{C}$ for $18\text{ hours}$ before releasing numeric titers.
- **Rule 3 (Local Endemic Baseline Shield):** A single acute-phase serum titer is considered potentially significant only if:
  $$\text{S. Typhi Somatic O (TO)} \ge 1:160 \qquad \text{and/or} \qquad \text{S. Typhi Flagellar H (TH)} \ge 1:160\text{–}1:320$$
  Definitive confirmation requires demonstrating a **$\ge 4\text{-fold}$ rise in tube titer** between acute (Day 3–5) and convalescent (Day 10–14) sera.

### 3.3 Severe Dengue Warning Signs & Defervescence Monitoring Protocol

Per WHO (2009/2023) and NVBDCP guidelines, severe dengue is characterized by **plasma leakage leading to shock or fluid accumulation with respiratory distress**, **severe bleeding**, or **severe organ involvement**. The most critical clinical window is the **Defervescence Phase (Days 3 to 7)** when the body temperature drops to normal ($37.5\text{–}38.0^\circ\text{C}$), during which capillary permeability abruptly peaks.

#### Critical Warning Signs Requiring Immediate Hospital Admission:
1. **Hemoconcentration:** A rapid rise in **Hematocrit ($\text{PCV}$) $\ge 20\%$ above baseline** (or absolute $\text{Hct} > 45\%$ in adult females, $> 48\%$ in adult males) indicates active plasma leakage into serous cavities:
   $$\Delta \text{Hct (\%)} = \frac{\text{Current Hct} - \text{Baseline Hct}}{\text{Baseline Hct}} \times 100 \ge 20\%$$
2. **Accelerated Thrombocytopenia:** A rapid drop in **Platelet count to $< 50,000/\mu\text{L}$** (especially a rate of decline $> 30,000/\mu\text{L}$ within $24\text{ hours}$).
3. **Clinical Symptoms of Vascular Leak:** Persistent vomiting ($\ge 3$ episodes in $24\text{ hours}$), severe abdominal pain or tenderness, lethargy, restlessness, and postural hypotension.
4. **Third-Space Fluid Accumulation:** Gallbladder wall thickening ($> 3\text{ mm}$ on ultrasound), right-sided pleural effusion, or ascites.

#### Fluid Resuscitation Guidelines & Hematocrit Adjustment Protocol:
- In compensated shock, initiate isotonic crystalloids (Ringer's Lactate or Normal Saline) at **$5\text{–}7\text{ mL/kg/hour}$ for $1\text{–}2\text{ hours}$**, then reduce to $3\text{–}5\text{ mL/kg/hour}$ for $2\text{–}4\text{ hours}$, then $2\text{–}3\text{ mL/kg/hour}$ titrated against hourly urine output ($\ge 0.5\text{–}1.0\text{ mL/kg/hour}$) and serial hematocrit.
- If hematocrit falls rapidly without clinical improvement, suspect **hidden occult internal bleeding** and cross-match packed red blood cells.

### 3.4 Scrub Typhus Eschar Identification & Immediate Clinical Action

*Orientia tsutsugamushi* penetrates cutaneous tissue via chigger saliva containing cytotoxic and anticoagulant enzymes, generating a pathognomonic primary lesion:
- **Morphology:** Begins as a painless erythematous papule that ulcerates and forms a circumscribed, punched-out **black necrotic crust surrounded by an erythematous indurated halo** ($4\text{–}10\text{ mm}$ in diameter) resembling a cigarette burn.
- **Hidden Anatomical Distribution:** Eschar lesions are completely painless and non-pruritic; patients are often unaware of them. Bench staff and clinicians must examine covered warm, moist skin folds:
  - **Axillae, groin, perineum, under breast/brassiere bands, umbilical fold, waistline, and behind knees.**
- **Immediate Clinical Action:** Identification of an eschar in an acutely febrile patient in Haryana is **pathognomonic of Scrub Typhus**. **Oral Doxycycline ($100\text{ mg}$ twice daily for 7 days) or oral Azithromycin ($500\text{ mg}$ once daily for 5 days)** must be initiated immediately without awaiting serology results to prevent progression to pneumonitis, ARDS, and acute kidney injury.

## 🔬 Section 4: Cluster-Wide Quality Specifications, Qualitative Metrology & Cutoff Verification (`CLSI EP12-A2 / EP15-A3`)

### 4.1 Qualitative Serology Metrology & Signal-to-Cutoff (S/CO) Dynamics

Qualitative and semi-quantitative immunoassay platforms (ELISA, CLIA, RDT) at Sawariya Diagnostic Lab operate under standardized **Index / Signal-to-Cutoff (S/CO) ratios**:
$$\text{Cutoff Value } (\text{CO}) = \bar{X}_{\text{NC}} + F \qquad \text{or} \qquad \text{CO} = K \times \bar{X}_{\text{PC}}$$
$$\text{Sample Index (S/CO)} = \frac{\text{Optical Density (OD) or Chemiluminescent Signal of Patient Sample}}{\text{Assigned Assay Cutoff Value (CO)}}$$

#### Master Interpretation Thresholds Across Serological Platforms:
- **Negative / Non-Reactive:** $\text{S/CO} < 0.90$ (No detectable antigen or antibody above background noise).
- **Equivocal / Borderline ("Grey Zone"):** $0.90 \le \text{S/CO} < 1.10$ (Analytical signal falls within the assay's $95\%$ confidence transition interval).
  - *Action Mandate:* Automatically repeat the specimen in duplicate from the primary tube. If repeat results remain within $0.90\text{–}1.10$, request a fresh redraw in **$48\text{ to }72\text{ hours}$** to observe for rising antibody kinetics.
- **Positive / Reactive:** $\text{S/CO} \ge 1.10$ (Confirmed reactive result).

### 4.3 Six-Sigma Quality Control & Westgard Rules for Immunoassay Platforms

Internal Quality Control (`IQC`) for microplate ELISA and Snibe MAGLUMI CLIA runs every $24\text{ hours}$ and with each new analytical batch:
1. **Control Materials:** Reagent kit manufacturer negative control, low-positive control ($\text{S/CO } 1.5\text{–}2.5$), and high-positive control ($\text{S/CO } 4.0\text{–}6.0$), supplemented with third-party pooled internal patient controls.
2. **Westgard Multi-Rule Implementation for Serology (`CLSI C24-Ed4`):**
   - **$1_{3s}$ Rule:** Run rejected if a single control exceeds $\bar{X} \pm 3\text{ SD}$ (random error or sudden reagent deterioration).
   - **$2_{2s}$ Rule:** Run rejected if two consecutive controls exceed $\bar{X} \pm 2\text{ SD}$ on the same side of the mean (systematic error).
   - **$R_{4s}$ Rule:** Run rejected if the difference between two controls within the same run exceeds $4\text{ SD}$ (random error).
   - **$10_x$ Rule:** Run rejected if 10 consecutive control values fall on one side of the mean (systematic calibration drift).
3. **Measurement Uncertainty for Semi-Quantitative Titers (`ISO/TS 20914:2019`):**
   - For tube agglutination (Widal, Brucella SAT), measurement uncertainty is expressed as $\pm 1$ doubling dilution step. A reported titer of $1:160$ has an expanded uncertainty interval encompassing $1:80$ to $1:320$ ($k=2, 95\%$ confidence). Consequently, single titer differences within one doubling dilution are not clinically significant.

### 4.4 Method Verification Protocol for Qualitative Immunoassays (`CLSI EP15-A3`)

Prior to introducing a new serological assay lot or platform, Sawariya Diagnostic Lab executes a 5-day verification protocol:
1. **Replicate Design:** Run two concentration levels (one weak positive near cutoff $\text{S/CO } 1.2\text{–}1.5$, one negative $\text{S/CO } 0.3\text{–}0.6$) tested in **5 replicates per run across 5 consecutive days ($n=25$ total replicates)**.
2. **Repeatability ($s_r$) & Within-Laboratory Precision ($s_{WL}$):**
   $$s_r = \sqrt{\frac{1}{D} \sum_{d=1}^D s_d^2} \qquad \text{and} \qquad s_{WL} = \sqrt{s_r^2 + s_{between-day}^2}$$
3. **Acceptance Criteria:** Within-laboratory precision for optical density ratios must satisfy $\text{CV} \le 12.0\%$ at the weak positive decision level. Qualitative concordance across 20 known clinical negative and 20 known positive specimens must demonstrate $100\%$ agreement.

### 5.1 Storage Integrity & Thermal Stress Management in Haryana Summer

During peak summer months (May to July), ambient temperatures in Charkhi Dadri and Bhiwani frequently reach $42\text{–}47^\circ\text{C}$. Lateral flow nitrocellulose membranes and conjugated monoclonal antibodies are highly vulnerable to heat-induced degradation:
1. **Cold-Chain Receipt Verification:** All incoming RDT shipments must be inspected for temperature excursions. Reagents arriving without intact ice-packs or with expired chemical indicators are quarantined and rejected.
2. **Controlled Storage Facility:** Rapid test kits are maintained inside a dedicated, air-conditioned storage room at **$15\text{–}25^\circ\text{C}$** or a calibrated medical refrigerator at **$2\text{–}8^\circ\text{C}$** with dual digital continuous data logging. Test cassettes must equilibrate to ambient temperature ($20\text{–}25^\circ\text{C}$) for $15\text{ minutes}$ before opening sealed foil pouches.
3. **Desiccant Capsule Inspection:** The silica gel desiccant pellet inside each pouch is visually inspected; any color shift indicating moisture ingress warrants immediate disposal of the individual cassette.

### 8.2 Scenario 2: Malaria Staining Quality, Parasitemia Index & HRP-2 Persistence Verification

  - *"For parasitemia quantification, we utilize our standardized two-tier protocol per WHO Malaria Microscopy Guidelines. On the thick film, parasites are counted against $200\text{ WBCs}$. Assuming an average leukocyte count of $8,000/\mu\text{L}$:*
    $$\text{Parasites / }\mu\text{L} = \frac{\text{Number of Parasites Counted} \times 8,000}{\text{Number of WBCs Counted (200)}}$$
    *If $\ge 500\text{ parasites}$ are seen before reaching $200\text{ WBCs}$, the count is stopped at that field. On thin films with high parasitemia, we examine $1,000\text{ RBCs}$ under oil immersion to report the exact Parasitic Index as $\%$ infected erythrocytes."*

## ⚖️ Section 10: Medico-Legal, Patient-Safety & CPA 2019 Regulatory Shield

1. **Statutory Disease Reporting Mandate (IDSP / NVBDCP):** Under the Epidemic Diseases Act and National Vector Borne Disease Control Programme directives, all confirmed laboratory diagnoses of **Dengue, Malaria (*P. falciparum* and *P. vivax*), and Chikungunya** must be reported daily via **IDSP Form L** to the District Surveillance Officer (DSO), Charkhi Dadri.
2. **Consumer Protection Act (CPA) 2019 Compliance:** Diagnostic serology results reflect the immunochemical and parasitological state of the collected specimen at the time of venipuncture. Test results must be integrated with the clinical history, duration of fever, physical examination findings (especially rash, eschar, and tourniquet test), and serial hematological trends.
3. **Emergency Point-of-Care Exclusion:** Rapid screening cards are qualitative screening aids and are not designed for standalone legal or forensic determinations.
4. **Data Protection & Privacy (`DPDP Act 2023`):** Patient infectious disease records, HIV/Hepatitis co-testing data, and critical communication logs are encrypted in the laboratory LIMS with role-based access security.
5. **Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules 2016/2019:** All infected serological reaction tubes, ELISA plates, rapid test cassettes, and blood-contaminated tips/vacutainers are decontaminated via autoclaving at $121^\circ\text{C}$ ($15\text{ psi}$ for $30\text{ minutes}$) or immersion in freshly prepared $1\%$ Sodium Hypochlorite prior to segregated disposal in yellow non-chlorinated bio-hazard bags.
