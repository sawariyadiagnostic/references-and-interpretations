## ⏱️ 4.1.0 — ICMR / NVBDCP Acute Febrile Illness (AFI) Day-by-Day Kinetic Selection Matrix

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Single Biggest Cause of Tropical Fever Misdiagnosis in North India:** Ordering antibody tests (`Dengue IgM`, `Widal`, `Scrub Typhus IgM`, `Chikungunya IgM`) on **Day 1 to Day 4 of fever** yields predictable **Window-Period False-Negatives**, while ordering antigen tests (`Dengue NS1`) after **Day 6–7** yields false-negatives due to immune-complex clearance. Always anchor test selection to **Day 1 = First calendar day of documented fever ($\ge 38.0^\circ\text{C} / 100.4^\circ\text{F}$)**.

| Fever Duration Window | Optimal First-Line Diagnostic Tests (High Sensitivity) | Premature / Suboptimal Tests (High False-Negative Rate) | Mandatory Serial Monitoring & Reflex Actions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Phase I: Early Acute Viremic / Bacteremic Window (Days 1 to 4 of Fever)** | • **Dengue:** `Dengue NS1 Antigen` (ELISA/CLIA) $\pm$ `Dengue RT-PCR`<br>• **Malaria:** `Thick & Thin Blood Smear` + `Bivalent HRP-2/pLDH RDT`<br>• **Enteric Fever:** **Automated Blood Culture (`BACTEC`)** prior to antibiotics<br>• **Chikungunya:** `CHIKV RT-PCR` | • `Dengue IgM` ($<20\%$ positive on Days 1–3)<br>• `Widal Test` (Negative in Week 1)<br>• `Scrub Typhus IgM` / `Leptospira IgM` / `Chikungunya IgM` | • Baseline & daily **Complete Blood Count (`CBC` — Hematocrit/PCV, Platelet Count, Total/Differential WBC)**, `CRP`, `SGOT/SGPT` |
| **Phase II: Immunological Transition / "Window Gap" (Days 5 to 7 of Fever)** | • **Dengue:** Combined **`Dengue NS1` + `Dengue IgM & IgG Capture ELISA/CLIA`**<br>• **Malaria:** Repeat `Thick/Thin Smear` + `RDT`<br>• **Enteric Fever:** `Blood Culture` + `TyphiDot IgM`<br>• **Zoonotic/Rickettsial:** `Scrub Typhus IgM ELISA`, `Leptospira IgM ELISA`, `Chikungunya IgM ELISA` | • Standalone `Dengue NS1` rapid card (declining sensitivity as host $\text{IgM/IgG}$ sequesters $\text{NS1}$ into immune complexes)<br>• Standalone `Widal Slide Test` | • If `NS1` and `IgM` are both negative/equivocal on Day 4–5 ("Window Gap"), **repeat `Dengue IgM` after 48–72 hours**.<br>• Watch for **Critical Defervescence Phase (Days 4–7)** plasma leakage in Dengue! |
| **Phase III: Convalescent / Immune-Mediated Phase (Day 8 to Day 14+ of Fever)** | • **Dengue:** `Dengue IgM & IgG Capture ELISA/CLIA`<br>• **Enteric Fever:** **Quantitative Tube `Widal Test`** (paired sera $\ge 4\text{-fold}$ rise) + `Stool/Bone Marrow Culture`<br>• **Post-Monsoon / Zoonotic:** `Scrub Typhus IgM ELISA`, `Leptospira IgM` ($\pm$ `MAT`), `Brucella SAT` (with $1:16\text{–}1:1280$ prozone dilution) | • `Dengue NS1 Antigen` (usually cleared by Day 7–9)<br>• `CHIKV RT-PCR` (viremia cleared by Day 6–7) | • Evaluate for secondary hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (`HLH` — Ferritin, Triglycerides, Fibrinogen), hepatorenal dysfunction (`Creatinine`, `Bilirubin`), or co-infections. |

## 🦟 4.1a — MASTER & STANDALONE: Dengue Complete Profile (`Dengue NS1 Antigen`, `IgM`, `IgG`) *(Top Haryana Medico-Legal Priority)*

> [!CAUTION]
> **Why Dengue Reports Cause Disputes in Haryana:** If a patient tests on **Day 1–3 of fever**, `Dengue NS1` is positive while `IgM` is still negative; if tested on **Day 6–8**, `NS1` often turns negative as host antibodies clear the antigen while `IgM` turns positive! Furthermore, NCVBDC/IDSP mandates that Rapid ICT cards are screening tests only and must be confirmed by Capture ELISA / CLIA / RT-PCR.

### **[LIMS COPY-PASTE BLOCK — DENGUE NS1 ANTIGEN, IgM & IgG INTERPRETATION]**

**Clinical Overview & Viral Kinetics:**  
Dengue virus (*Flavivirus*, serotypes $\text{DENV-1, 2, 3, 4}$) exhibits a biphasic diagnostic window based on the **exact day of fever onset (Day 1 = First day of fever)**:
- **Viremic / Antigen Phase (Day 1 to Day 5 of Fever):** Non-Structural Protein 1 (**$\text{NS1}$ Antigen**) is secreted in high concentrations into blood from **Day 1**, peaking on Days 2–4, and declining after Day 5–7 as anti-NS1 antibodies form immune complexes (cleared even faster—by Day 3–4—in Secondary Dengue).
- **Serological Antibody Phase (Day 5 Onward):**
  - **Primary Dengue Infection:** **$\text{Dengue IgM}$** becomes detectable by **Day 4–5** in $50\%$ of patients and by **Day 6–10** in $> 95\%$ of patients (peaking at 2 weeks and persisting for $2\text{–}3\text{ months}$). **$\text{Dengue IgG}$** appears late (Day 10–14) at low titers and persists for life.
  - **Secondary Dengue Infection** *(Re-infection with a heterologous serotype — high risk of Antibody-Dependent Enhancement [ADE], Dengue Hemorrhagic Fever, and Dengue Shock Syndrome):* Rapid anamnestic surge of **high-titer $\text{Dengue IgG}$ within Day 2–4 of fever** ($\text{IgM:IgG}$ optical density/index ratio $< 1.2\text{–}1.4$), accompanied by a truncated $\text{NS1}$ window and blunted/variable $\text{IgM}$.

**Integrated Dengue Serological Pattern Matrix:**
| `NS1 Antigen` | `Dengue IgM` | `Dengue IgG` | Typical Day of Fever & Clinical Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reactive (+)** | Non-Reactive (-) | Non-Reactive (-) | **Acute Early Dengue Infection (Typically Days 1 – 5 of Fever):** Active viremia prior to antibody seroconversion. Monitor serial CBC (**Platelets & Hematocrit/PCV**) daily. |
| **Reactive (+)** | **Reactive (+)** | Non-Reactive (-) | **Acute Primary Dengue Infection (Transition Phase, Typically Days 4 – 7 of Fever).** |
| Non-Reactive (-) | **Reactive (+)** | Non-Reactive (-) | **Recent Acute Primary Dengue Infection (Typically $> \text{Day 5–6}$ of Fever):** $\text{NS1}$ antigen cleared by rising host $\text{IgM}$ response. |
| **Reactive / (-)** | **Reactive / (-)** | **Strongly Reactive (+)** | **Acute Secondary Dengue Infection** *(if acute fever with rising $\text{IgG}$ / $\text{IgM:IgG}$ ratio $< 1.2$)* **OR Past Resolved Dengue Infection** *(if $\text{NS1}$ and $\text{IgM}$ are negative in an afebrile/unrelated illness)*. |
| Non-Reactive (-) | Non-Reactive (-) | Non-Reactive (-) | **No Evidence of Dengue at Current Stage:** If sampled on **Day 1–3**, repeat $\text{NS1}$/RT-PCR if suspicion persists; if sampled on **Day 4–5 ("Window Gap" as $\text{NS1}$ falls and $\text{IgM}$ is still rising)**, **repeat Dengue $\text{IgM}$ Capture ELISA after 48–72 hours**. |

**🛡️ MANDATORY MEDICO-LEGAL & NCVBDC/ICMR REGULATORY SHIELD (DENGUE):**
1. **Rapid Immunochromatographic Card (ICT) vs Capture ELISA / RT-PCR:** Per **NCVBDC (Govt. of India) & ICMR guidelines**, Rapid Immunochromatographic (ICT) lateral-flow tests are **rapid point-of-care screening tests only** (lower sensitivity and specificity than ELISA). All rapid screening results (especially negative results in clinically suspected cases or positive rapid cards for IDSP statutory notification) must be confirmed by **NIV/ICMR-approved $\text{NS1}$ / $\text{IgM}$ Capture MAC-ELISA, Quantitative CLIA, or Dengue RT-PCR**.
2. **Flavivirus & Autoimmune Cross-Reactivity:** Dengue $\text{IgM}$ and $\text{IgG}$ antibodies cross-react extensively with other *Flaviviruses* (**Japanese Encephalitis, Zika, Yellow Fever, West Nile**) and may yield false-positive $\text{IgM}$ reactivity in **Malaria, Leptospirosis, Scrub Typhus, Chikungunya, Rheumatoid Factor positivity, or SLE**.
3. **Clinical Monitoring Mandate (Defervescence Phase Alert):** Serological positivity or negativity does not predict clinical severity; the highest risk of plasma leakage and shock occurs during **defervescence (Days 4–7 when fever subsides)**. **Serial monitoring of Complete Hemogram (specifically $\ge 20\%$ rise in Hematocrit/$\text{PCV}$ indicating hemoconcentration/plasma leakage and serial Platelet Counts)** and WHO clinical warning signs (severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, mucosal bleeding, hepatomegaly $>2\text{ cm}$, lethargy/restlessness) is mandatory. *Not valid for medico-legal purposes.*

## 🩸 4.1b — STANDALONE: Malaria Testing (`Rapid Malaria Pf/Pv Antigen` & `Peripheral Smear for MP`)

### **[LIMS COPY-PASTE BLOCK — MALARIA ANTIGEN & PERIPHERAL SMEAR (MP) INTERPRETATION]**

**Clinical Overview:**
1. **Thick & Thin Peripheral Blood Smear Microscopy (Gold Standard):**
   - **Thick Smear (Dehemoglobinized):** Concentrates red blood cells by $20\text{–}40\times$, providing maximal sensitivity for detecting low-level parasitemia (expert detection threshold $\sim 5\text{–}50\text{ parasites}/\mu\text{L}$; minimum $100\text{–}200$ oil-immersion fields or $200\text{–}500\text{ WBCs}$ examined before reporting negative).
   - **Thin Smear (Methanol-Fixed):** Preserves host RBC morphology (enlarged stippled RBCs with Schüffner's dots in *P. vivax*; normal-sized RBCs with Maurer's clefts, multiple ring forms, accole/appliqué forms, and crescentic banana-shaped gametocytes in *P. falciparum*) for definitive species identification and **Parasitic Index ($\%$ infected RBCs or parasites/$\mu\text{L}$)** quantification.
2. **Rapid Diagnostic Test (`RDT` — Bivalent Immunochromatographic Antigen Detection):**
   - **$\text{HRP-2}$ (Histidine-Rich Protein 2):** Water-soluble protein specific solely to ***Plasmodium falciparum* (*P.f.*)** asexual stages and young gametocytes.
   - **$\text{pLDH}$ (Parasite Lactate Dehydrogenase) / Aldolase:** Glycolytic enzyme produced **only by viable, metabolically active intraerythrocytic parasites** (species-specific *P. vivax* $\text{pLDH}$, *P. falciparum* $\text{pLDH}$, or Pan-malarial $\text{pLDH}$); clears rapidly within $2\text{–}5\text{ days}$ of effective antimalarial therapy.

**🛡️ MANDATORY MEDICO-LEGAL & DIAGNOSTIC SHIELD (MALARIA):**
1. **A Single Negative Smear or RDT Does NOT Exclude Malaria:** Because *Plasmodium* parasites undergo synchronous schizogony and mature *P. falciparum* trophozoites/schizonts sequester in deep visceral capillaries, peripheral parasitemia fluctuates markedly. If clinical suspicion remains high despite an initial negative result, **repeat Thick & Thin Blood Smears every 6 to 12 hours for 36–48 hours** (ideally during or shortly after a febrile paroxysm and **prior to administering empirical antimalarial drugs**).
2. **False-Positive `HRP-2` Persistence After Successful Treatment (Up to 28–42 Days):** *P. falciparum* **$\text{HRP-2}$ antigen persists in the bloodstream for $2\text{ to }6\text{ weeks}$ after complete parasite clearance**, so an $\text{HRP-2}$-based RDT **cannot be used to monitor treatment response or diagnose early treatment failure/recrudescence** (use Thick/Thin Microscopy or $\text{pLDH}$ instead).
3. **`pfhrp2/pfhrp3` Gene Deletion False-Negative Alert:** Up to $5\%\text{–}15\%$ of *P. falciparum* isolates in pockets of India harbor *pfhrp2/pfhrp3* gene deletions and will test **falsely negative on $\text{HRP-2}$-only rapid cards** while testing positive on **Microscopy and Pan/Pf-$\text{pLDH}$**. Conversely, high-titer **Rheumatoid Factor (RF)**, heterophile antibodies, or chronic hepatitis/schistosomiasis can cause false-positive RDT lines.
4. **Critical Parasitemia Alert:** Any detection of ***P. falciparum* asexual parasitemia**, mixed *P.f. + P.v.* infection, cerebral/organ dysfunction, or **Parasitic Index $\ge 2\%$ in non-immune patients ($\ge 5\%$ hyperparasitemia by WHO criteria, or $>100,000\text{–}250,000\text{ parasites}/\mu\text{L}$)** is a **Critical Panic Value** requiring immediate physician notification. *Not valid for medico-legal purposes.*

## 🌡️ 4.1c — STANDALONE: Enteric Fever / Typhoid (`Widal Test` & `TyphiDot IgM/IgG`) *(Crucial Indian Medico-Legal Shield)*

> [!CAUTION]
> **Why Widal & TyphiDot Need an Ironclad Shield in Haryana:** The Widal test is one of the most over-interpreted tests in India. Baseline antibodies in healthy North Indians, anamnestic cross-reactions during Malaria/Dengue/TB, and false-negatives in Week 1 of fever are routine. This shield protects your laboratory under ICMR Standard Treatment Workflows.

### **[LIMS COPY-PASTE BLOCK — WIDAL TEST & TYPHIDOT INTERPRETATION]**

**Clinical Overview:**
- **Enteric Fever Kinetics (*Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhi & Paratyphi A/B/C):**
  - **Week 1 of Fever (Days 1–7):** **Automated Blood Culture (`BACTEC` / `BacT/ALERT`, $8\text{–}10\text{ mL}$ in adults, $3\text{–}5\text{ mL}$ in children)** is the **Gold Standard** ($80\%\text{–}90\%$ sensitive in Week 1 prior to antibiotics).
  - **Week 2 Onward (After Day 7–10 of Fever):** Agglutinating antibodies against **Somatic `O` Antigen ($\text{TO}$ — predominantly $\text{IgM}$, rises first at Day 6–8 and disappears within 3–6 months)** and **Flagellar `H` Antigen ($\text{TH, AH, BH}$ — predominantly $\text{IgG}$, rises at Day 10–12 and persists for months to years)** become detectable.
- **TyphiDot ($\text{IgM} / \text{IgG}$ against $50\text{ kDa}$ Outer Membrane Protein [OMP] of *S. Typhi*):** Detects *S. Typhi* OMP antibodies earlier than Widal (typically from Day 5–7 of fever), though it does not detect *S. Paratyphi A/B* and suffers from tropical cross-reactivity.

**Endemic North Indian Baseline & Diagnostic Titer Guide (Single Acute Serum):**
| Antigen Tested | Typical Endemic Baseline in Healthy North Indians | Suggestive Single-Serum Titer *(In Compatible Week 2 Clinical Fever)* |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ***S. Typhi* Somatic `O` (`TO`)** | Up to $1:40 \text{ – } 1:80$ | **$\ge 1:160$** *(Active/recent somatic lipopolysaccharide antigen response)* |
| ***S. Typhi* Flagellar `H` (`TH`)** | Up to $1:80 \text{ – } 1:160$ | **$\ge 1:160 \text{ – } 1:320$** *(Note: Isolated $\uparrow\text{TH}$ with negative $\text{TO}$ often reflects past infection, TCV/TAB vaccination, or anamnestic recall)* |
| ***S. Paratyphi* `AH` / `BH`** | $< 1:40 \text{ – } 1:80$ | **$\ge 1:160$** *(Specific flagellar response for *S. Paratyphi A* or *B*)* |

**🛡️ MANDATORY ICMR / WHO MEDICO-LEGAL SHIELD FOR WIDAL & TYPHIDOT:**
1. **A Single Widal or TyphiDot Result is NEVER Definitively Diagnostic of Typhoid Fever:** Per **WHO and ICMR Standard Treatment Workflows**, definitive serological confirmation of Enteric Fever requires demonstrating a **$\ge 4\text{-fold}$ rise in Quantitative Tube Agglutination antibody titer between acute-phase serum (Week 1) and convalescent-phase serum collected $7\text{ to }10\text{ days}$ later**. Rapid slide Widal tests have high false-positive rates and must be verified by quantitative tube titration.
2. **Causes of False-Positive Widal / TyphiDot Reactivity:**
   - **Endemic Background Titers & Prior Vaccination:** Healthy individuals in typhoid-endemic regions (including Haryana/NCR) or recipients of Typhoid Conjugate/Ty21a vaccination frequently maintain baseline $\text{TO/TH}$ titers of $1:80\text{–}1:160$ and persistent $\text{IgG}$ positivity.
   - **Anamnestic Cross-Reaction (Non-Specific Recall):** Any unrelated acute febrile illness—specifically **Malaria, Dengue, Scrub Typhus, Brucellosis, Tuberculosis, Viral Hepatitis, or Chronic Liver Disease / Autoimmune Disorders (RA/SLE)**—can trigger a non-specific polyclonal memory rise in *Salmonella* $\text{TH}$ or $\text{TO}$ antibodies ("anamnestic reaction").
   - **Antigenic Sharing:** Somatic $\text{O}$ antigen ($9, 12$) is shared across numerous non-typhoidal *Salmonella* species, *Enterobacteriaceae*, and *Yersinia*.
3. **Causes of False-Negative Widal Results:** Blood collected **too early (within the first 5–7 days of fever)** before antibody synthesis, **early empirical administration of antibiotics** (which blunts the humoral antibody response), or the **Prozone Phenomenon** in hyper-immune sera unless serially diluted ($1:20\text{ to }1:640$). **Automated Blood Culture prior to antibiotic initiation remains the diagnostic gold standard.** *Not valid for medico-legal purposes.*

## 🕷️ 4.1d — STANDALONE: `Chikungunya IgM`, `Scrub Typhus IgM / Weil-Felix` & `Leptospira IgM / Brucella SAT`

### **[LIMS COPY-PASTE BLOCK — CHIKUNGUNYA, SCRUB TYPHUS, LEPTOSPIRA & BRUCELLA]**

**1. Chikungunya $\text{IgM}$ (*Alphavirus* — Acute Febrile Symmetric Polyarthralgia):**
- **Kinetics:** In the first **1 to 5 days of fever**, **Chikungunya Real-Time RT-PCR** is the test of choice; **Chikungunya $\text{IgM}$** becomes detectable from **Day 5–7 of symptom onset**, peaks at 3–4 weeks, and **persists for 2 to 6 months** (occasionally up to 12 months in patients with chronic post-Chikungunya arthritis).
- **Caveat:** A negative $\text{IgM}$ before Day 5 does not exclude acute Chikungunya; conversely, a positive $\text{IgM}$ may reflect recent infection over the past several months, cross-reactivity with related *Alphaviruses* (*O'nyong-nyong, Sindbis*), or false-positive binding from high-titer **Rheumatoid Factor (`RF`)** unless $\text{IgG/RF}$ pre-absorption or $\mu$-capture ELISA is utilized.

**2. Scrub Typhus $\text{IgM}$ (*Orientia tsutsugamushi* — Post-Monsoon Rural/Agricultural Fever) & Weil-Felix:**
- **Kinetics:** $\text{IgM}$ antibodies against the recombinant $56\text{ kDa}$ outer membrane protein appear by **Day 5–7 of fever** ($\text{IgM}$ Capture ELISA using optical density cutoff $\ge 0.5\text{–}0.8$ has $>90\%$ sensitivity/specificity and is vastly superior to rapid ICT cards and the obsolete **Weil-Felix `Proteus OX-K` agglutination test**, which has $<50\%$ sensitivity and only turns positive at titers $\ge 1:160\text{–}1:320$ in Week 2).
- **Clinical Correlation:** Look for pathognomonic painless necrotic **eschar** (axilla, groin, inframammary, waist), generalized lymphadenopathy, thrombocytopenia, elevated hepatic transaminases ($\text{AST/ALT}$), and pneumonitis/meningoencephalitis; **empirical Doxycycline or Azithromycin must NOT be delayed** in clinically suspected cases if tested before Day 5.

**3. Leptospira $\text{IgM}$ (*Leptospira interrogans* — Weil’s Hepatorenal-Hemorrhagic Syndrome) & Brucella $\text{SAT}$:**
- **Leptospira Kinetics:** In the **Leptospiremic Phase (Days 1–7)**, Whole-Blood/Serum PCR is preferred; **Leptospira $\text{IgM}$** appears in the **Immune Phase (from Day 5–7 onward)** and persists for $3\text{–}6\text{ months}$. Confirmatory reference testing requires paired sera **Microscopic Agglutination Test (MAT — $\ge 4\text{-fold}$ rise or single titer $\ge 1:400$)**.
- **Brucella Standard Tube Agglutination (`SAT` — *B. melitensis / B. abortus*):** Significant single titer in endemic North India is **$\ge 1:160$** (or **$\ge 1:80$ after $2\text{-Mercaptoethanol [2-ME]}$ $\text{IgM}$ cleavage**, which confirms active $\text{IgG}$-mediated Brucellosis). **Mandatory Prozone Protocol:** Because chronic/subacute Brucellosis frequently exhibits a severe **Prozone Phenomenon** (false-negative at $1:20\text{–}1:80$) and blocking $\text{IgA}$ antibodies, tube dilutions must be carried out up to **$1:640\text{ – }1:1280$**. *All infectious serology results must be correlated clinically; not valid for medico-legal purposes.*
