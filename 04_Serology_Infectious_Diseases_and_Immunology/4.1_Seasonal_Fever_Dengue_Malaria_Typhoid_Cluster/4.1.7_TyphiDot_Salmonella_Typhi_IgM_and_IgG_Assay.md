## 1. Document Control, Standardized Coding & Metrological Traceability Hierarchy

### 1.1 Master Test Scope & Mandatory Regulatory Policy
This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) governs the execution, verification, interpretation, quality assurance, and medico-legal reporting of the TyphiDot rapid immunoassay (detecting $\text{IgM}$ and $\text{IgG}$ antibodies against the specific $50\text{ kDa}$ Outer Membrane Protein [`OMP`] of *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhi) at Sawariya Diagnostic Lab, Charkhi Dadri, Haryana.
1. **Clinical Triage Scope:** TyphiDot serves as a rapid, qualitative serological screen for early enteric fever triage. Its primary clinical strength lies in detecting specific anti-$50\text{ kDa}$ OMP $\text{IgM}$ antibodies as early as **Day 2 to 3 of acute fever**—a clinical phase when the conventional Widal agglutination test is entirely non-reactive or within baseline.
2. **Definitive Microbiological Companion:** In accordance with statutory directives from the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) Enteric Fever Surveillance Network, National Centre for Disease Control (NCDC), and National Accreditation Board for Testing and Calibration Laboratories (`NABL 112`), **automated continuous-monitoring blood culture (BD BACTEC FX40) remains the mandatory gold standard**. Serological detection of anti-OMP antibodies does not yield antimicrobial susceptibility profiles and cannot confirm eradication.
3. **The Paratyphoid Blind Spot Mandate:** Because TyphiDot utilizes the outer membrane protein antigen exclusive to ***Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhi**, it is inherently **incapable of detecting *Salmonella enterica* serovar Paratyphi A or B**. In North India and Haryana, *S. Paratyphi A* accounts for $20\%\text{ to }35\%$ of all culture-confirmed enteric fever cases. A negative TyphiDot result must never be used to rule out enteric fever without pairing with blood culture or quantitative tube Widal agglutination (`SOP SDL-SER-SOP-4.1.6`).

Diagnostic TyphiDot test kits at Sawariya Diagnostic Lab maintain calibrator traceability under `ISO 17511:2020` and `CLSI I/LA20`:
1. **Primary Antigen Calibrator:** Purified $50\text{ kDa}$ outer membrane porin protein isolated from reference strains of *Salmonella enterica* subsp. *enterica* serovar Typhi (ATCC 14028 / Ty2 / 901-O) characterized via sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC).

### 1.5 Pre-Analytical Phlebotomy, Specimen Handling & Quantitative HIL Rejection Matrix
1. **Phlebotomy:** Collect $3.0\text{ to }4.0\text{ mL}$ of venous blood into a sterile plain clot-activator vacutainer with gel separator (SST / Gold Top). Venipuncture must adhere strictly to aseptic technique.
2. **Plasma Compatibility:** $\text{K}_2\text{-EDTA}$ or lithium-heparin plasma is acceptable if validated by kit manufacturer. However, **pure non-hemolyzed serum is strongly preferred** at Sawariya Diagnostic Lab to eliminate potential matrix interferences from anticoagulant salts and micro-fibrin web formation.
3. **Centrifugation:** Allow complete clot retraction at ambient temperature ($20\text{–}25^\circ\text{C}$) for $30\text{ minutes}$. Centrifuge at $1,500\text{ to }2,000\times g$ for $10\text{ minutes}$. Transfer clear serum immediately into secondary polypropylene cryovials.
4. **Specimen Stability:** Serum remains stable at ambient temperature ($20\text{–}25^\circ\text{C}$) for $\le 8\text{ hours}$, at $2\text{–}8^\circ\text{C}$ for up to $7\text{ days}$, and at $-20^\circ\text{C}$ for up to $6\text{ months}$. Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles ($> 2$).
5. **Quantitative HIL Interference Matrix (`CLSI EP07 / EP37`):**
   - **Hemolysis ($H$):** Reject specimens with Free Hemoglobin $> 200\text{ mg/dL}$ (Grade 2+). Free hemoglobin tints the nitrocellulose membrane pinkish-red, masking faint colloidal gold-positive $\text{IgM}$ bands or creating ambiguous pseudo-lines.
   - **Lipemia ($L$):** Reject specimens with Triglycerides $> 500\text{ mg/dL}$. Severe chylomicronemia impedes capillary lateral-flow chromatography across the nitrocellulose strip, causing erratic migration or high background noise. Clarify lipemic sera via high-speed microcentrifugation ($12,000\times g$ for $15\text{ minutes}$) or request an 8-hour fasting recollection.
   - **Icterus ($I$):** Bilirubin up to $20\text{ mg/dL}$ does not interfere with colloidal gold reading. Read under standardized diffuse white daylight.

## 2. Biochemistry of Outer Membrane Protein `OMP 50 kDa` & Kinetic Comparison vs Widal

### 2.1 Cellular & Molecular Biology of the $50\text{ kDa}$ Outer Membrane Protein
*Salmonella enterica* subsp. *enterica* serovar Typhi possesses a complex outer cell envelope consisting of lipopolysaccharides (LPS), capsular polysaccharide (Vi antigen), and transmembrane outer membrane proteins (OMPs):
1. **Porin Protein Architecture:** The $50\text{ kDa}$ antigen is a major outer membrane porin (OmpS1 / OmpC/F family trimeric porin) spanning the outer bacterial membrane. It functions as a hydrophilic pore mediating selective passive diffusion of small nutrients and electrolytes while maintaining bacterial structural integrity against host bile salts and complement.
2. **Structural Invariance:** Extensive SDS-PAGE, mass spectrometry, and genomic sequencing across hundreds of wild-type clinical isolates have demonstrated that the $50\text{ kDa}$ OMP is **genetically and antigenically invariant across all *Salmonella* Typhi strains**, regardless of geographic origin or antimicrobial resistance profile.
3. **Epitope Specificity vs. Somatic LPS:** Unlike the somatic lipopolysaccharide ($9, 12$) antigens utilized in the Widal test—which share oligosaccharide repeating units with non-typhoidal *Salmonella* (e.g., *S.* Enteritidis, *S.* Dublin, *S.* Typhimurium) and *Enterobacteriaceae*—the $50\text{ kDa}$ outer membrane porin features unique, folded polypeptide conformational loops exposed on the cell surface that induce highly specific human humoral immune responses.

### 2.3 The Typhidot-M Innovation: Molecular Mechanism of Competitive $\text{IgG}$ Masking
In hyper-endemic regions of South Asia and North India (including Haryana), repeated subclinical exposure to *Salmonella* Typhi leads to elevated baseline levels of high-affinity anti-$50\text{ kDa}$ OMP $\text{IgG}$ antibodies in the general population.
1. **The Masking Mechanism:** When an endemic individual experiences an acute secondary reinfection, memory B-cells immediately mount a vigorous secondary $\text{IgG}$ surge. These high-affinity, high-titer bivalent $\text{IgG}$ antibodies saturate the limited $50\text{ kDa}$ OMP epitopes on the nitrocellulose test strip. Although acute pentameric $\text{IgM}$ is present in the patient's blood, the bulky $\text{IgM}$ molecules are **sterically blocked and out-competed by high-affinity $\text{IgG}$**, preventing $\text{IgM}$ binding.
2. **The Resulting Diagnostic Trap:** In standard non-absorbed rapid cards, the test demonstrates an **isolated $\text{IgG}$ line (+)** and a **completely negative $\text{IgM}$ line (-)**. The clinician erroneously concludes that the patient merely has "past resolved exposure" and withholds essential antibiotic therapy during acute typhoid sepsis!
3. **The Typhidot-M Solution (Goat Anti-Human $\text{IgG}$ Pre-absorption):** Typhidot-M incorporates a pre-analytical incubation step wherein the patient's serum is treated with goat anti-human $\text{IgG}$ antiserum. The goat antibodies bind, cross-link, and precipitate all circulating host $\text{IgG}$ molecules. With competitive $\text{IgG}$ fully neutralized and inactivated, the acute pentameric $\text{IgM}$ binds unhindered to the $50\text{ kDa}$ OMP antigen dots, yielding a clear, unequivocal positive $\text{IgM}$ signal.

### 2.6 Clinical Pharmacology, Antimicrobial Blunting & Special Population Stratification
1. **Early Antimicrobial Blunting:** Initiation of over-the-counter antibiotics (e.g., Cefixime, Ofloxacin, Azithromycin) within 24–48 hours of fever onset abruptly curtails bacterial multiplication and decreases antigenic shedding, attenuating the peak anti-OMP antibody titer. While blood culture positivity falls steeply from $>85\%$ to $<30\%$ within 24 hours of antibiotic administration, TyphiDot $\text{IgM}$ remains positive because even brief antigenic exposure triggers early B-cell differentiation.
2. **Antimicrobial Safety in Pregnancy & Lactation:**
   - **Ceftriaxone (FDA Category B):** First-line agent in pregnant women presenting with severe enteric fever. Safe across all trimesters; monitor neonate for kernicterus displacement if delivered near administration.
   - **Azithromycin (FDA Category B):** Preferred oral agent for uncomplicated typhoid in pregnancy.
   - **Fluoroquinolones (Ciprofloxacin / Ofloxacin - FDA Category C):** Strictly contraindicated in pregnancy and nursing mothers due to arthropathy and cartilage erosion in developing fetal joints.
   - **Chloramphenicol:** Historically contraindicated near term due to fatal cardiovascular collapse ("Gray Baby Syndrome") from immature neonatal glucuronyl transferase.
3. **Pediatric Phlebotomy & Dehydration Management:** Pediatric patients with enteric fever frequently present with high fever, vomiting, and refusal to feed, resulting in rapid hypovolemic dehydration. Phlebotomists must collect only $1.0\text{ to }3.0\text{ mL}$ for pediatric blood culture bottles (BACTEC Peds Plus) and $1.0\text{ mL}$ for SST serum to prevent iatrogenic anemia.

### 3.2 Physical Chemistry of Colloidal Gold Conjugation & Nitrocellulose Membrane Kinetics
1. **Colloidal Gold Nanoparticle Physics:** The detection conjugate comprises monodisperse, spherical $40\text{ nm}$ gold nanoparticles synthesized via sodium citrate reduction of tetrachloroauric acid ($\text{HAuCl}_4$). Affinity-purified goat anti-human $\text{IgM}$ ($\mu$-chain specific) and goat anti-human $\text{IgG}$ ($\gamma$-chain specific) antibodies are electrostatically adsorbed onto the gold nanoparticle surface at their isoelectric point ($\text{pI } 8.2 \pm 0.2$). The resulting conjugate exhibits strong localized surface plasmon resonance, generating intense optical absorption at $\sim 525\text{ nm}$ (perceived visually as a sharp purple-pink band).
2. **Membrane Capillary Dynamics:** The chromatographic strip uses a surfactant-treated nitrocellulose membrane with a calibrated mean pore diameter of $0.45\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ and a capillary migration speed of $120\text{ seconds} / 4\text{ cm}$. The running buffer (Tris-buffered saline, $\text{pH } 7.4$, with $0.1\%$ Tween-20, $1.0\%$ Bovine Serum Albumin, and $0.09\%$ Sodium Azide) maintains stable hydrodynamic flow and prevents non-specific hydrophobic protein aggregation.

### 3.3 Step-by-Step Rapid Lateral-Flow ICT Analytical SOP
1. **Reagent Equilibration:** Remove the foil pouch containing the rapid test cassette and buffer vial from $2\text{–}8^\circ\text{C}$ refrigeration. Allow all components to equilibrate to ambient room temperature ($20\text{–}25^\circ\text{C}$) for **$20\text{ minutes}$** before unsealing.
2. **Device Labeling & Inspection:** Place the cassette on a clean, dry, level bench surface. Label the device with the patient's unique 10-digit accession number and barcode. Inspect the desiccant pellet; discard any device exhibiting a color change indicating moisture exposure.
3. **Sample Inoculation:** Using a calibrated micropipette, draw exactly **$10\text{ }\mu\text{L}$ of non-hemolyzed serum or plasma**. Dispense the sample vertically into the center of the sample well (`S`), ensuring complete liquid transfer without bubble formation.
4. **Buffer Dispensing:** Hold the buffer dropper bottle strictly vertically directly above the sample well. Dispense exactly **$2\text{ drops}$ (approximately $80\text{ }\mu\text{L}$)** of assay running buffer into the buffer/sample well.
5. **Incubation & Read-Window Discipline:** Start a calibrated digital laboratory timer immediately. Allow the chromatographic front to migrate across the membrane.
   - **Mandatory Read Window:** Read the test visually under diffuse daylight illumination at **$15\text{ minutes}$**.
   - **The Evaporation / Ghost Line Prohibition:** **NEVER interpret or report results after $20\text{ minutes}$.** As the nitrocellulose membrane dries, evaporative concentration of trace colloidal gold and non-specific protein deposition generate faint, shadowy artifactual lines ("ghost lines") across the `IgM` and `IgG` zones, causing disastrous false-positive sign-outs.

### 3.4 Visual Band Reading & Grey-Zone Shadow Line Protocol
1. **Valid Control (`C`) Line:** A distinct purple-pink line must always appear in the Control (`C`) region. If the `C` line fails to appear at 15 minutes, the run is **INVALID**. Discard the cassette, record the incident in the non-conformance log, and repeat the test immediately with a new device from a different foil pouch.
2. **`IgM` Test Line:** A distinct pink-to-purple band at the `IgM` position indicates the presence of anti-$50\text{ kDa}$ OMP $\text{IgM}$ antibodies.
3. **`IgG` Test Line:** A distinct pink-to-purple band at the `IgG` position indicates the presence of anti-$50\text{ kDa}$ OMP $\text{IgG}$ antibodies.
4. **Faint / Grey-Zone Shadow Line Protocol:**
   - Any extremely faint, diffuse, or incomplete line where band intensity is $< 10\%$ of the control line must be classified as **Grey-Zone / Indeterminate**.
   - Do NOT sign out as positive.
   - Inspect the patient's Complete Blood Count (CBC): assess WBC count and absolute eosinophil count (enteric fever typically exhibits leukopenia or normal WBC with **absolute eosinopenia $0\%$**).
   - Inoculate automated blood culture (BACTEC FX40) and request a repeat serum sample in **$48\text{ to }72\text{ hours}$** to document definitive band seroconversion.

### 3.5 Analytical Cross-Reactivity & Specificity Barriers
1. **Non-Typhoidal *Salmonella* & Paratyphoid Fever:** Extensive cross-reactivity studies demonstrate that antibodies against *Salmonella enterica* serovars Paratyphi A, Paratyphi B, and *Salmonella* Typhimurium do not bind the $50\text{ kDa}$ OMP target. This high analytical specificity creates a clinical blind spot: *S. Paratyphi A* enteric fever is **consistently negative on TyphiDot**.
2. **Polyclonal B-Cell Activation in Dengue & Malaria:** During acute *Plasmodium falciparum / vivax* malaria and acute Dengue virus infection, intense host pro-inflammatory cytokine release ($\text{IL-1}$, $\text{TNF-}\alpha$, $\text{IL-6}$) triggers massive non-specific polyclonal activation of circulating B-lymphocytes. In $15\%\text{ to }20\%$ of cases, low-affinity polyreactive $\text{IgM}$ antibodies are secreted that weakly bind colloidal gold conjugates, producing faint false-positive TyphiDot $\text{IgM}$ bands.
3. **Rheumatoid Factor ($\text{RF}$) & Heterophile Antibodies:** Sera with high-titer $\text{IgM}$ rheumatoid factor ($> 80\text{ IU/mL}$) can cross-link the Fc portion of immobilized capture antibodies and gold conjugate antibodies, producing an artifactual false-positive test band in the absence of *Salmonella* antibodies.

3. **Measurement Uncertainty for Qualitative Immunoassays:** In accordance with `ISO/TS 20914`, qualitative lateral-flow assays express uncertainty as the **$C_{50}$ cut-off zone (grey zone)**—the analyte concentration that yields a $50\%$ probability of a positive result. ### 5.2 Blood Culture Companion & Antimicrobial Stewardship Shield
Because South Asia is currently experiencing high rates of antimicrobial resistance in *Salmonella enterica* serovars—including widespread fluoroquinolone resistance ($>85\%$) and the emergence of Extensively Drug-Resistant (`XDR`) *S.* Typhi resistant to Chloramphenicol, Ampicillin, Co-trimoxazole, Fluoroquinolones, and third-generation Cephalosporins (mediated by the `blaCTX-M-15` ESBL gene)—serological assays cannot stand alone.
1. **The Inoculation Mandate:** Prior to administering empirical antibiotics, every patient with suspected enteric fever must have blood inoculated into automated blood culture bottles:
   - **Adults:** $8.0\text{ to }10.0\text{ mL}$ of whole blood inoculated directly into BD BACTEC Plus Aerobic/F bottles (containing antibiotic-binding polymeric resins that neutralize empirical antibiotics).
   - **Pediatrics:** $1.0\text{ to }3.0\text{ mL}$ inoculated into BD BACTEC Peds Plus/F bottles.
2. **Clinical Utility:** TyphiDot enables immediate empirical triage within 15 minutes to guide supportive therapy and initial antibiotic selection while awaiting the definitive 24–48 hour blood culture and susceptibility report.

### 5.3 Critical ("Panic") Alert Protocol (`CLSI GP47`)
While TyphiDot is a qualitative serological test, certain presentation patterns mandate immediate telephonic escalation under `CLSI GP47`:
1. **Critical Alert Trigger:** A positive TyphiDot $\text{IgM}$ result in an inpatient or emergency room patient presenting with:
   - Severe persistent abdominal pain, guarding, or rigidity (indicating impending or established terminal ileal perforation).
   - Toxic encephalopathy, delirium, hypothermia, or hemodynamic instability (systolic $\text{BP} < 90\text{ mmHg}$).
   - Profound leukopenia ($\text{WBC} < 2,500/\mu\text{L}$) or severe thrombocytopenia ($\text{Platelets} < 50,000/\mu\text{L}$).
2. **Communication & Read-Back Protocol:** The duty pathologist must telephone the attending physician within **$30\text{ minutes}$**, communicate the positive $\text{IgM}$ result, record the full read-back confirmation, and document the clinician's name, employee ID, and exact timestamp in the LIMS Critical Alert Log (`SDL-LIMS-CRIT-4.1.7`).

## 6. Six Real-World Worked Clinical Cases & Diagnostic Traps

### Case 1: Early Acute Primary Typhoid in Week 1 (Day 3 Fever)
- **Patient Profile:** 21-year-old male college student residing in Charkhi Dadri town presenting with high continuous fever ($103.0^\circ\text{F}$) for 3 days, frontal headache, anorexia, dry cough, and coated tongue. Pulse $74\text{ bpm}$ (sphygmo-thermic dissociation / relative bradycardia).
- **Initial Serological Investigations (Day 3):**
  - Slide & Tube Widal: Somatic `TO` $< 1:20$, Flagellar `TH` $< 1:20$ (completely negative, as expected in Week 1).
  - TyphiDot Rapid ICT: **`IgM` Strongly Positive (distinct purple-pink band); `IgG` Negative; Control `C` Valid**.
  - Complete Blood Count: Total Leukocyte Count $3,400/\mu\text{L}$ (leukopenia); Neutrophils $68\%$, Lymphocytes $30\%$, Monocytes $2\%$; **Absolute Eosinophil Count $0/\mu\text{L}$ ($0\%$, classic eosinopenia)**; Platelet Count $185,000/\mu\text{L}$; Hemoglobin $14.2\text{ g/dL}$.
  - Liver Function Tests: Serum Bilirubin $0.9\text{ mg/dL}$, SGOT/AST $54\text{ U/L}$, SGPT/ALT $62\text{ U/L}$, Serum Albumin $4.1\text{ g/dL}$.
  - BD BACTEC Automated Blood Culture: Inoculated prior to antibiotics; bottle flagged positive at $16\text{ hours}$. Subculture yielded Gram-negative motile bacilli, non-lactose fermenting colonies on MacConkey agar; serotyping confirmed *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhi susceptible to Azithromycin and Ceftriaxone.
- **Diagnostic Resolution:** Early acute primary *S.* Typhi enteric fever. Because the outer membrane porin induced early $\text{IgM}$ synthesis, TyphiDot was positive on Day 3 while Widal remained completely blind. Appropriate empirical Azithromycin was initiated on Day 3, resulting in defervescence within 72 hours.

### Case 2: Acute Secondary Re-infection with High Endemic $\text{IgG}$ Masking
- **Patient Profile:** 34-year-old female schoolteacher from Dadri Rural presenting with continuous fever ($102.5^\circ\text{F}$) of 5 days duration, abdominal cramps, toxic dullness, and hepatomegaly. Medical history revealed culture-confirmed typhoid fever 2 years prior.
- **Laboratory Investigation & Discrepancy:**
  - Standard Non-Absorbed Rapid Card: `Control` Valid, **`IgM` Negative (-), `IgG` Strongly Positive (+)**.
  - The treating general practitioner initially assumed "past resolved immunity" and considered alternative viral causes.
  - Sawariya Diagnostic Lab Workup: Due to the high clinical index of suspicion and toxic appearance, the pathologist reflexed the sample to the **Typhidot-M protocol (Goat Anti-Human $\text{IgG}$ Pre-absorption)**.
  - Typhidot-M Result: After neutralization and precipitation of competing host $\text{IgG}$, **`IgM` unmasked as Strongly Positive (+)**!
  - CBC & Biochemistry: TLC $4,200/\mu\text{L}$, AEC $0\%$, Platelets $156,000/\mu\text{L}$, Quantitative CRP $38.4\text{ mg/L}$.
  - Blood Culture: BD BACTEC flagged positive at $22\text{ hours}$ with *S.* Typhi.
- **Diagnostic Resolution:** Acute secondary *S.* Typhi re-infection. Extremely high circulating memory $\text{IgG}$ titers had sterically masked acute pentameric $\text{IgM}$ on the standard rapid cassette. Anti-IgG pre-absorption neutralized the interference, unmasking the true acute state and preventing diagnostic failure.

### Case 3: Acute *Salmonella Paratyphi A* Infection Missed by TyphiDot
- **Patient Profile:** 29-year-old male agricultural worker presenting with remittent fever ($102.8^\circ\text{F}$) of 8 days duration, malaise, and loose stools. Pulse $82\text{ bpm}$.
- **Laboratory Findings:**
  - TyphiDot Rapid ICT: **`Control` Valid, `IgM` Negative (-), `IgG` Negative (-)**.
  - Slide Widal: Reported as "Negative" by a local dispensary.
  - Sawariya Diagnostic Lab Quantitative Tube Titration:
    - `TO`: $1:160$ ($3+$ granular agglutination, reflecting shared somatic factor 12).
    - `TH`: $1:40$ (baseline).
    - **`AH`: $1:320$ ($4+$ fluffy cotton-wool floccules, specific for *S.* Paratyphi A)**.
    - `BH`: $< 1:20$.
  - Automated Blood Culture: BD BACTEC flagged positive at $20\text{ hours}$; subculture and agglutination confirmed ***Salmonella enterica* serovar Paratyphi A**.
  - CBC Profile: TLC $4,600/\mu\text{L}$, Eosinophils $0\%$, Platelets $172,000/\mu\text{L}$.
- **Diagnostic Resolution:** Acute Paratyphoid Fever A. TyphiDot was completely non-reactive because *S. Paratyphi A* lacks the $50\text{ kDa}$ outer membrane porin specific to *S.* Typhi. This case illustrates the critical danger of relying on TyphiDot to exclude enteric fever in Haryana, where Paratyphi A prevalence is substantial.

### Case 4: False-Positive TyphiDot $\text{IgM}$ in Severe Dengue Shock Syndrome
- **Patient Profile:** 19-year-old female presenting on Day 4 of sudden-onset high continuous fever ($104.0^\circ\text{F}$), intense retro-orbital pain, severe generalized arthralgia, and diffuse petechial rash.
- **External Laboratory Error:** An unaccredited clinic performed a rapid TyphiDot test, observed a faint pink line at the $\text{IgM}$ position, and issued a diagnosis of "Acute Typhoid Fever". The patient was prescribed oral Cefixime and discharged home without fluid monitoring.
- **Deterioration & Sawariya Diagnostic Lab Evaluation (Day 5):**
  - Clinical State: Cold, clammy extremities, rapid thready pulse ($124\text{ bpm}$), blood pressure $84/60\text{ mmHg}$ (narrow pulse pressure), severe abdominal tenderness.
  - Complete Blood Count: Hematocrit surged to $51.2\%$ (marked plasma leakage from baseline $36\%$); Platelet count crashed to $14,000/\mu\text{L}$; WBC $2,800/\mu\text{L}$.
  - Dengue Serology: **Dengue NS1 Antigen (CLIA) Strongly Reactive ($78.4\text{ S/CO}$)**; Dengue $\text{IgM}$ Reactive.
  - Automated Blood Culture: Sterile after 5 days of incubation.
  - TyphiDot Review: Retesting at Sawariya Lab showed a faint shadow line caused by intense polyclonal B-cell activation and colloidal gold entrapment.
- **Diagnostic Resolution:** Severe Dengue with Dengue Shock Syndrome (DSS). The false-positive TyphiDot $\text{IgM}$ led to antibiotic misdirection while life-threatening plasma leakage was neglected. Emergency intravenous crystalloid resuscitation in the ICU stabilized the patient.

### Case 5: Post-Infectious Residual $\text{IgM}$ Persistence ("Test-of-Cure" Fallacy)
- **Patient Profile:** 42-year-old businessman who had culture-confirmed *S.* Typhi bacteremia 6 weeks prior, successfully treated with a 14-day course of intravenous Ceftriaxone with complete symptom resolution. Now presents with sudden fever ($102.0^\circ\text{F}$), burning micturition, and right flank tenderness of 24 hours duration.
- **Diagnostic Investigation:**
  - Referring Clinic Action: Performed TyphiDot, which showed **`IgM` Positive and `IgG` Positive**. The clinician told the patient his "typhoid had relapsed" and prescribed another round of Ceftriaxone.
  - Sawariya Diagnostic Lab Evaluation:
    - CBC Profile: TLC $14,800/\mu\text{L}$ (marked leukocytosis with neutrophilic left shift: $84\%$ neutrophils); Eosinophils $2\%$ (not eosinopenic).
    - Urine Routine & Microscopy: Pale cloudy urine, Protein $1+$, **Pus cells $60\text{–}80/\text{hpf}$ with clumps**, Bacteria $3+$.
    - Urine Culture: $> 100,000\text{ CFU/mL}$ of *Escherichia coli* susceptible to Nitrofurantoin and Fosfomycin.
    - Blood Culture (BD BACTEC): Sterile after 5 days.
- **Diagnostic Resolution:** Acute *E. coli* Pyelonephritis. The positive TyphiDot $\text{IgM}$ represented physiological antibody persistence (which typically lingers for $60\text{ to }90\text{ days}$ following acute typhoid). TyphiDot is **strictly contraindicated as a Test-of-Cure or for diagnosing early relapse**.

### Case 6: High-Titer Rheumatoid Factor ($\text{RF}$) Cross-Reactivity in an RA Flare
- **Patient Profile:** 54-year-old female with established seropositive Rheumatoid Arthritis on Methotrexate presenting with low-grade fever ($100.8^\circ\text{F}$), severe bilateral wrist and knee pain, morning stiffness, and fatigue.
- **Laboratory Findings:**
  - TyphiDot Rapid ICT: **`Control` Valid, `IgM` Positive (+), `IgG` Negative (-)**.
  - Complete Blood Count: WBC $9,800/\mu\text{L}$; Eosinophils $3\%$ (normal; enteric fever typically shows $0\%$); Platelet Count $340,000/\mu\text{L}$.
  - Inflammatory & Serological Markers: Quantitative CRP $46.0\text{ mg/L}$; **Rheumatoid Factor ($\text{RF IgM}$) Markedly Elevated at $340\text{ IU/mL}$** (Reference $< 14\text{ IU/mL}$). Procalcitonin normal ($0.06\text{ ng/mL}$).
  - Blood Culture: Sterile after 5 days.
- **Diagnostic Resolution:** Acute flare of Rheumatoid Arthritis with false-positive TyphiDot $\text{IgM}$ mediated by high-titer $\text{IgM-RF}$ cross-linking rapid assay capture antibodies. Escalation of anti-inflammatory therapy led to defervescence without antimicrobial administration.
