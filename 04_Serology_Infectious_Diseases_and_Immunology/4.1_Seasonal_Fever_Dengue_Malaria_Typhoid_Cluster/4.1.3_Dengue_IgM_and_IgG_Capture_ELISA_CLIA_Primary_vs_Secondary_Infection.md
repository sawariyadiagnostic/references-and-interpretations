## 2. Pre-Analytical Phlebotomy, Window-Period Timing, Stability & HIL Rejection Matrix

### Optimal Sampling Window & Immunological Kinetics
- **Target Window:** **Day 5 of Fever Onward** (for Primary Dengue $\text{IgM}$); **Day 3 of Fever Onward** (for Secondary Dengue anamnestic $\text{IgG}$ surge).
- **The Day 4–5 "Window Gap" Pitfall:** Between **Day 4 and Day 5 of fever**, `Dengue NS1` may have already declined below the limit of detection while `Dengue IgM` has only seroconverted in $\sim 50\%$ of patients. Any patient testing negative on Day 4–5 with persistent clinical suspicion **must have a repeat serum sample tested for `Dengue IgM Capture` after $48\text{ to }72\text{ hours}$ (Day 6–8)**, when sensitivity exceeds $95\%\text{–}98\%$.
- **Specimen Type:** Serum ($2.0\text{ mL}$ from SST / Red-top tube), centrifuged at $1500 \times g$ for $10\text{ minutes}$.

### Specimen Stability & Quantitative HIL Rejection Matrix
| Storage Condition | Maximum Stability | Interference Factor | Quantitative Rejection Threshold & Corrective Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ambient ($20\text{–}25^\circ\text{C}$)** | $\le 8\text{ hours}$ | **Hemolysis Index ($H$)** | Reject for Microplate ELISA if **$\text{Hb} > 150\text{ mg/dL}$** (peroxidase substrate interference); Maglumi CLIA acceptable up to $500\text{ mg/dL}$. |
| **Refrigerated ($2\text{–}8^\circ\text{C}$)** | **Up to $7\text{ days}$** | **Lipemia Index ($L$)** | High-speed centrifuge ($10,000 \times g$, $10\text{ min}$) if **$\text{TG} > 500\text{ mg/dL}$**; aspirate clear infranatant below lipid layer. |
| **Frozen ($-20^\circ\text{C}$)** | $6\text{ months}$ | **Rheumatoid Factor (`RF`)** | High-titer $\text{IgM-RF}$ ($> 100\text{ IU/mL}$) can bridge patient Flavivirus $\text{IgG}$ onto anti-$\mu$ capture wells; use kits with $\text{RF}$-absorbent diluent. |

## 3. Hardware-Agnostic Analytical SOP, `IgM:IgG` Ratio Calculation & Six Sigma IQC

1. **Why Capture ELISA/CLIA (`MAC-ELISA` / `GAC-ELISA`) Is Mandatory Over Indirect ELISA:**
   - In **Indirect ELISA**, viral antigen is coated on the plate; if a patient has high-titer $\text{IgG}$ (Secondary Dengue), $\text{IgG}$ saturates all viral epitopes, causing a **false-negative `IgM`** AND making any harmless past Dengue exposure ($>70\%$ of North Indian adults) test positive for `IgG`.
   - In **Capture `IgG` ELISA (`GAC-ELISA`, e.g., Panbio)**, the calibrator cutoff is intentionally calibrated to match a **Hemagglutination Inhibition (`HI`) titer of $\ge 1:2,560$**, so `Capture IgG` only turns positive during an **acute Secondary Dengue anamnestic surge**, remaining negative in low-titer remote past infections!
2. **Cutoff Index (`COI` / `Panbio Units`) & Grey-Zone Duplicate Verification:**
   - **Dengue IgM Capture:** Non-Reactive $< 0.90\text{ COI}$ ($< 9.0\text{ Units}$); **Equivocal / Grey-Zone $0.90\text{–}1.10\text{ COI}$ ($9.0\text{–}11.0\text{ Units}$)**; Reactive $> 1.10\text{ COI}$ ($> 11.0\text{ Units}$).
   - **Dengue IgG Capture (Secondary Surge Threshold):** Non-Reactive $< 1.80\text{ COI}$ ($< 18.0\text{ Panbio Units}$); Equivocal $1.80\text{–}2.20\text{ COI}$ ($18.0\text{–}22.0\text{ Units}$); **Reactive (Acute Secondary Dengue) $> 2.20\text{ COI}$ ($> 22.0\text{ Panbio Units}$)**.
   - **WHO Primary vs Secondary `IgM:IgG` Index Ratio (Days 4–10 of Fever):**
     $$\text{IgM : IgG Ratio} = \frac{\text{Dengue IgM Index (or OD)}}{\text{Dengue IgG Index (or OD)}}$$
     - **Ratio $\ge 1.2\text{ to }1.4$:** Consistent with **Acute Primary Dengue Infection**.
     - **Ratio $< 1.2$** (with elevated $\text{IgG}$ in acute febrile setting): Consistent with **Acute Secondary Dengue Infection**.
3. **Six Sigma IQC & EQAS:**
   - Run Negative Control, Cutoff Calibrator (in duplicate/triplicate), and Positive Control in every run. Maintain internal $\text{CV}_A \le 7.5\%$ and participate in NIV/NABL EQAS cycles.

## 6. NABL & CLSI GP47 Critical ("Panic") Alerts & Statutory Notification

> [!CAUTION]
> **ANTIBODY-DEPENDENT ENHANCEMENT (`ADE`) CRITICAL WARNING IN SECONDARY DENGUE:**
> In Secondary Dengue (e.g., past `DENV-1` infection followed by acute `DENV-2` infection), non-neutralizing cross-reactive $\text{IgG}$ antibodies bind the new serotype and facilitate massive viral entry into monocytes/macrophages via $\text{Fc}\gamma\text{R}$ receptors (**Antibody-Dependent Enhancement**), triggering a cytokine storm, endothelial glycocalyx disruption, and acute capillary plasma leakage during **Defervescence (Days 4–7)**.
> - **Immediate Panic Notification (<30 min):** Any Secondary Dengue serological profile accompanied by **$\ge 20\%$ Hematocrit (`PCV`) rise**, **Platelets $< 20,000\text{–}30,000/\mu\text{L}$**, pleural effusion/ascites, or mucosal bleeding.

## 7. Kinetic Day-by-Day Rise/Peak/Persistence & Smart LIMS Reflex Cascades

- **Persistence Caveat ("Residual IgM" Pitfall):** Because `Dengue IgM` remains detectable for **$2\text{ to }3\text{ months}$** (and occasionally up to $6\text{ months}$ by ultra-sensitive CLIA) after recovery, a patient who had Dengue in September and develops **Enteric Fever, Malaria, or Bacterial Sepsis in November** may still test **weakly positive for `Dengue IgM`**!
- **Smart LIMS Reflex Cascade:**
  - If `Dengue IgM` is weakly reactive ($1.1\text{–}2.5\text{ COI}$) with `NS1` negative, normal Platelets, and **markedly elevated `CRP` ($> 60\text{ mg/L}$) or neutrophilic leukocytosis**, trigger an automatic LIMS comment: *"Evaluate for bacterial sepsis/typhoid/scrub typhus with residual past Dengue IgM or polyclonal cross-reactivity."*

## 8. Analytical Cross-Reactivity & ISO 22367 Risk FMEA

1. **Pan-Flavivirus Cross-Reactivity:** Conserved envelope (`E` protein fusion loop) epitopes cause extensive cross-reactivity of `Dengue IgM` and `IgG` with **Japanese Encephalitis Virus (`JEV` — endemic in UP/Bihar/Assam/Haryana belts), Zika Virus (`ZIKV`), West Nile Virus (`WNV`), and Yellow Fever vaccination**.
2. **Non-Flavivirus False-Positive `IgM`:** Acute **Malaria (*P. falciparum / P. vivax*), Leptospirosis, Scrub Typhus, Chikungunya, Infectious Mononucleosis (`EBV/CMV`), Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (`SLE`), and Rheumatoid Factor (`RF`)** frequently cause false-positive `Dengue IgM` reactivity (especially on rapid lateral-flow ICT cards).

| Failure Mode (ISO 22367) | Root Cause | Clinical Risk | Mandatory Mitigation Control |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Isolated `Dengue IgG (+)` on Indirect ELISA/Rapid Card Misdiagnosed as Acute Dengue** | Indirect assay detects lifelong low-titer past $\text{IgG}$ ($>70\%$ of North Indians) | True cause of acute fever (Malaria/Typhoid/Sepsis) missed! | Explicitly distinguish **Isolated Past `IgG`** vs **Acute Secondary Dengue** in Section 9/10 |
| **False-Negative `Dengue IgM` on Day 3 of Fever** | Tested prior to Day 5 seroconversion | Patient discharged without platelet/PCV monitoring | Enforce combined `NS1 + IgM/IgG` testing or mandatory $48\text{–}72\text{h}$ repeat alert |

Monitor serial Hematocrit (PCV), Platelet counts, and clinical warning signs every 12–24 hours."*
